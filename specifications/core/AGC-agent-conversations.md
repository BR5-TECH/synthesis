# Agent conversations

**Spec code:** `AGC`

## Intent
The backend that connects a conversation to an agent. When an author addresses one of the project's enrolled personas (`AGR-agent-registry.md`) from a conversational surface, this module registers a **turn**, assembles the material a participant in that surface would have — what is under discussion and everything said in the conversation so far — hands it to the loop that holds the conversation (`../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md`) against the endpoint `AAP-ai-api-integrations.md` resolves for that agent, and delivers whatever comes back into the surface the question came from, as an ordinary contribution by an agent participant rather than as a notification about one. What context a conversation carries differs by what the discussion is about — a file of the project, a draft, or a note — so context assembly is a **builder per target kind** rather than one fixed payload, and a target kind added later brings its own builder without changing a single operation here. A discussion that also carries a fragment target adds the fragment's passage to the material, and nothing else changes. The material reaches the loop in named sections, which is what lets an instruction about how to treat a passage under discussion name the section holding it. A section carries **pictures as well as prose**: an image the draft's prompt embeds and an image a participant attached to a comment are both part of what a participant in that conversation would have in front of them, so where the endpoint serving the turn accepts image content they reach the model as image content, in the position the prompt or the comment history puts them. Where it does not, they reach it as **safe metadata in that same position** — the media type and the name or reference, never the bytes — the turn is an ordinary successful conversation, and the surface that asked the question says plainly that the pictures were not sent. A turn is not obliged to produce an answer: the loop may end it on a **question** put to the author, on a **question set** recorded against a discussion, or on a **proposal** offering a rewrite of the prompt under discussion — a draft's, or one the project already holds — and each is delivered here as the turn's one contribution exactly as an answer would be. The question set is the one of them that is not a comment: it is a durable record the discussion holds until the author answers it, and the answers arrive later as ordinary comments dispatching fresh turns. An agent therefore behaves like a person in the conversation — it is addressed by name, it answers where it was asked, several can be asked at once, one can be asked again before it has finished answering, and one that has asked something stops talking until it is answered. Where a turn fails for a reason repeating the call could plausibly fix, and the loop's own attempts are spent, what this module leaves behind is an **offer to ask again** in the place the answer would have gone — because a conversation gains a line only when an agent actually said something, and a failure the author can act on is one they should be able to act on without leaving the card they were already reading. Everything about that offer is memory of the running session and nothing about it is a contribution. **Everything operational about a turn is memory of the running session, and the one thing that outlives it is a count.** The turn record, the request assembled for it, the exchange it accumulated, the tool calls active in it, its failure, the recovery and image-notice registries, and the agent session it ran inside are all held in memory and are all gone when the application closes — nothing of them is written to the conversation log, resumed after a relaunch, or copied anywhere. What a conversation leaves behind is the comment it contributed, which `CMS-comments-storage.md` persists, and — for a turn in a **draft's** conversation alone — a small **lifecycle record** appended to that draft's statistics log (`DSS-draft-statistics-storage.md`): the turn's own id, the draft, the discussion, when execution began and ended, how it ended, and whether it ever reached the model. That record is what lets one model-reaching logical turn be counted once as refinement work and its actual execution measured, and it carries none of the material the turn worked on: no prompt, no context, no exchange, no tool argument, no tool result, no credential, and no word the agent wrote. Out of scope: defining or enrolling an agent, which is `AGR-agent-registry.md`'s; how a conversation with a model is actually held — the prompt it works under, the tools it is lent, the rounds it takes, the calls it repeats, the deadlines it runs under, and what it logs — all of which are `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md`'s; editing a file, running a command, or otherwise acting on the author's behalf, so a turn produces a message and leaves the material it was given byte-for-byte as it found it, and the backends that do act live in `AIC-agentic-integrations.md`; storing, rendering, and applying a proposed change, all of which are `DCP-draft-change-proposals.md`'s and `../ui/DCR-draft-change-review.md`'s; waiting for an author to answer a question or decide a proposal, since a turn that puts either ends on it and nothing here is held open, watched, or resumed — the reply arrives whenever it arrives, as an ordinary comment dispatching a fresh turn, and one that never arrives costs nothing; defining a tool, each of which is `../tools/TLC-tool-conventions.md`'s group; storing a conversation, since the surface a turn was delivered into is the record and no **operational** part of a turn survives the running application; folding, deduplicating, or presenting the lifecycle and usage records this module appends, all of which are `DSS-draft-statistics-storage.md`'s and `../ui/DFI-draft-information.md`'s; fetching what a `url` attachment addresses, since an address stays an address and an agent is told what it points at rather than shown it; and choosing an endpoint, a key, or a model, all of which are resolved rather than decided here.

## Contract surface
The module owns the in-memory set of turns in flight, the per-target-kind context builders, the recovery registry, and the Tauri commands below. Names match `../ui/CMT-comments.md` and `../ui/NAW-new-artifact.md` byte-for-byte. How a registered turn actually reaches a model is `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md`'s.

### The origin
An origin names the conversation a turn belongs to: the discussion's id, its owner target, and its fragment target where it has one. The kind of the `target` is what selects the context builder (AGC-FR-05) and what the loop selects its prompt and its tool set by (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-03, CVL-FR-08), and it is the extension point through which a future owner kind joins this module. Discussions of every origin — the Editor, a Flow, a Diff, a History view, a draft, a note — take the same record, so there is no branch between a passage-level and a whole-target conversation:

```
ConversationOrigin {
  discussion_id,                        // the discussion; the same id for every turn in it
  target: DiscussionTarget,             // the owner: artifact, draft, or note
                                        //   (per CMS-comments-storage.md)
  fragment_target?: FragmentTarget      // present when the discussion targets a fragment
                                        //   of an artifact or a draft prompt
}
```

The backend resolves `target` and `fragment_target` from the discussion that `discussion_id` names at dispatch, so a caller cannot misstate either.

### The turn

```
AgentTurnState = "running" | "delivered" | "awaiting_reply" | "failed" | "cancelled"

AgentTurnFailure =
    "agent_not_found"        // the nickname resolves to no agent enrolled in this project
  | "agent_unavailable"      // it resolves, but no provider resolves or the active provider no longer serves its model
  | "discussion_locked"      // the conversation takes no further contribution
  | "context_unavailable"    // none of the material the builder needs could be read
  | "unreachable" | "rejected" | "timed_out" | "empty_reply" | "keychain_unavailable"

ActiveToolCall {
  id,                        // unique within the turn
  tool_name,                 // the name the model called, e.g. "search_specifications"
                             //   or the provider-native entry type "openrouter:web_search"
  activation_seq             // 1, 2, 3 … within the turn: ascending with activation,
                             //   unique, and never reused (AGC-FR-33)
}

AgentTurn {
  id,                        // unique for the lifetime of the running application
  agent_id,
  nickname,
  origin:      ConversationOrigin,
  trigger_comment_id,        // the comment that addressed the agent
  state:       AgentTurnState,
  failure:     AgentTurnFailure | null,
  retry_permitted,           // bool; true only while this turn is its conversation's
                             //   current recoverable failure (AGC-FR-31)
  active_tool_calls: ActiveToolCall[],   // the calls in flight now, activation_seq
                                         //   ascending; empty in every terminal state
  images_omitted,            // bool; true where this turn's input held images the
                             //   resolved endpoint could not take, so text and image
                             //   metadata were sent in their place (AGC-FR-37)
  started_at,                // RFC 3339 UTC
  ended_at                   // RFC 3339 UTC; null while running
}
```

`timed_out` carries two distinct expiries and the difference decides whether the call is repeated: the **provider-call** deadline of `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-17, which bounds one `complete` invocation and is repeated on expiry, and the **whole-turn** deadline of CVL-FR-16, which bounds the turn and is never repeated (CVL-FR-18). A turn terminating on the first is a recoverable failure and one terminating on the second is not, and `retry_permitted` is what says which a given turn was.

### The durable record a turn leaves

Everything in "The turn" above is **session state**: it lives in memory for the running application, it is never written to the conversation log, and a relaunch finds none of it (AGC-FR-ZPKW). A turn in a **draft's** conversation additionally leaves two kinds of durable line in that draft's statistics log, and nothing else anywhere:

```
RefinementTurnRecord {
  turn_id,             // the AgentTurn's own id; the stable identity of the record
  draft_id,            // origin.target.draft_id
  discussion_id,       // origin.discussion_id
  fragment_targeted,   // bool; true where origin.fragment_target is present
  reached_model,       // bool; true once one complete invocation was made
  started_at,          // RFC 3339 UTC; the turn's own started_at
  ended_at,            // RFC 3339 UTC; the instant it reached a terminal state
  outcome              // the terminal AgentTurnState: delivered, awaiting_reply,
                       //   failed, or cancelled
}

RefinementUsageRecord {
  usage_id,            // the provider usage record's stable identity, from the loop
  turn_id,             // the turn the usage belongs to
  round, attempt,      // the logical model-call round and the physical attempt
  representation,      // "per_call" | "turn_aggregate"
  input_tokens?, cached_input_tokens?, uncached_input_tokens?, output_tokens?
}
```

Both are handed to `DSS-draft-statistics-storage.md` through `record_statistics_event` (DSS-FR-EKZI) as a `conversation_turn` event and a `token_usage` event of scope `conversation` and bucket `refinement`. Neither carries an `AgentRequest`, an `AgentExchange`, an `InputSection`, an `ActiveToolCall`, a tool argument, a tool result, a recovery-registry entry, an image, a comment body, a credential, or any part of the answer (AGC-FR-LHRC).

### Tauri commands
- `"dispatch agent turn"` → `dispatch_agent_turn(nickname, origin, trigger_comment_id)` → `AgentTurn` in state `running`, or a typed refusal carrying the same `AgentTurnFailure` vocabulary. Returns as soon as the turn is registered; the loop proceeds afterwards.
- `"cancel agent turn"` → `cancel_agent_turn(turn_id)` → `AgentTurn`. Idempotent against a turn that has already terminated.
- `"list agent turns"` → `list_agent_turns(origin?)` → `AgentTurn[]`, the turns still outstanding — those `running` and those `awaiting_reply` (AGC-FR-28) — most recently started first. An `origin` narrows the result to that conversation; a null `origin` returns every outstanding turn anywhere, which is what the chrome roster reads (per `../ui/AGT-agents.md` AGT-FR-05).
- `"list recoverable agent turn failures"` → `list_recoverable_agent_turn_failures(origin?)` → `AgentTurn[]`, the **recovery registry** of AGC-FR-31: at most one turn per conversation, each `failed`, each carrying `retry_permitted` true, most recently failed first. An `origin` narrows the result to that conversation; a null `origin` returns every entry anywhere, which is what a surface listing conversations reads. A turn it returns is not outstanding and `list_agent_turns` returns it under neither form.
- `"list agent turn image notices"` → `list_agent_turn_image_notices(origin?)` → `AgentTurn[]`, the **notice registry** of AGC-FR-39: at most one turn per conversation, each carrying `images_omitted` true, most recently ended first. An `origin` narrows the result to that conversation; a null `origin` returns every entry anywhere. An entry is not outstanding and `list_agent_turns` returns it under neither form.
- `"retry agent turn"` → `retry_agent_turn(turn_id)` → a **new** `AgentTurn` in state `running`, or a typed refusal carrying the same `AgentTurnFailure` vocabulary. It refuses any turn that is not its conversation's current registry entry (AGC-FR-32).

Every command errors with a typed `no_project_open` when no project is open.

### Events
- `"agent turn state changed"` — carries one `AgentTurn`, emitted when a turn is registered, each time the tool calls active in it change, and again when it reaches a terminal state (AGC-FR-21, AGC-FR-34).

A turn's states divide two ways, and the two divisions are independent. A state is **terminal** when the turn has made its last model call and its working state is gone — `delivered`, `awaiting_reply`, `failed`, and `cancelled` all are, and `running` alone is not. A turn is **outstanding** when `list_agent_turns` still returns it — `running` and `awaiting_reply` are, the other three are not. `awaiting_reply` is therefore both: the turn is over and nothing of it survives but the registration recording that this agent asked something in this conversation, which is what keeps a surface from drawing it as still working and what a fresh turn for that agent retires (AGC-FR-28, AGC-FR-29).

### Internal (no UI consumer; not Tauri commands and unreachable from the frontend)
- `record_refinement_lifecycle(turn)` — composes the `RefinementTurnRecord` of the Contract surface for a **draft-scoped** turn that has just reached a terminal state and hands it to `DSS-draft-statistics-storage.md` (AGC-FR-YQMD). It is called once per such turn and never for a turn whose target is an artifact or a note.
- `record_refinement_usage(turn, usage)` — composes the `RefinementUsageRecord` for one reliable provider-reported usage record the loop surfaced (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-WQZD) and hands it to the same sink (AGC-FR-BVNT). It is called once per surfaced record and never for a non-draft origin.

- `build_input(origin, trigger_comment_id, accepts_image_input)` — the per-target-kind builder set of AGC-FR-05; returns the `InputSection[]` the loop's request carries (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-01), each section holding its ordered content parts. `accepts_image_input` is the resolved endpoint's own capability (AGC-FR-36) and decides whether an image is carried as image content or as the metadata that stands in for it (AGC-FR-35, AGC-FR-37). On a draft origin it also reads that draft's proposals through `DCP-draft-change-proposals.md`'s `read_proposal_changes` (DCP-FR-HNWD), which is the only store this builder reads beyond the conversation and the material under discussion.

## Functional requirements
1. **AGC-FR-01** A **turn** is one agent, taking up one message, in one conversation: it names the agent addressed, the origin it was addressed in, and the comment that addressed it, and it produces exactly one contribution to that conversation or none at all. That contribution is the agent's **answer** (AGC-FR-16), or the one **question** it asked instead of answering, or the one **question set** it recorded instead of answering (AGC-FR-VRHM), or the one **proposal** it offered instead of answering (`../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-15) — never two of them and never more than one of any. Nothing here batches, merges, or splits a turn.
2. **AGC-FR-02** `dispatch_agent_turn` registers the turn and returns it in state `running` without waiting for the model, so no surface is blocked by a conversation. The refusals it can return before registering are the ones knowable without a call — an unresolvable nickname, an unavailable agent, a locked conversation — and every other outcome arrives as a state change on the registered turn.
3. **AGC-FR-03** A dispatch is never refused because another turn is already running. Any number of turns may be in flight in one conversation, for one agent, and across the whole application at once: an author who asks a second question before the first is answered is answered twice, and an agent asked again while it is still thinking is asked again, exactly as a person addressed twice in a conversation is.
4. **AGC-FR-04** A turn names exactly one agent. A message addressing several agents produces one dispatch per distinct agent (per `../ui/CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-EACI), each an independent turn with its own state, its own failure, and its own reply, so one agent's failure never suppresses another's answer and the order the answers arrive in is the order they complete.
5. **AGC-FR-05** Input assembly is a **builder per target kind**. `build_input(origin, trigger_comment_id)` dispatches on `origin.target.kind` — `artifact`, `draft`, or `note` — to the builder for that kind, each of which declares for itself what material the conversation calls for. An optional `origin.fragment_target` is read by every builder in the same way (AGC-FR-06) and adds a branch to none. Adding an owner kind therefore adds a builder and changes no command signature, no payload shape, and no requirement of the call.
6. **AGC-FR-06** Every builder produces **three** sections in this order and the input carries no fourth: the **material** section, naming what is under discussion; `discussion_history`, opening with the discussion's fragment as its path, offsets, and quoted passage where the discussion has a fragment target and continuing with the discussion's comments oldest first; and `current_comment`, holding the body of the comment that addressed the agent. The fragment belongs to the discussion history, a discussion being targeted at a fragment rather than a comment. A discussion with no fragment target (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-53) opens that section with its comments directly, an absent fragment being nothing to report rather than something to describe. The material section is tagged `artifact` on the two target kinds whose subject is a file or a draft, and `note_context` on a `note` target, whose subject is a note and not any file (AGC-FR-07). Exactly one of the two tags occurs in any one input: a note turn carries no `artifact` section, so nothing offers the agent the artifact the note happens to be attached to as the thing being discussed.
7. **AGC-FR-07** The `artifact` builder fills `artifact` with the **whole source of the file the discussion is about**, together with its project-relative path — the Markdown of a Markdown artifact, the JSON document of a Flow, and the text of a file carrying no artifact type alike, each read as the bytes on disk decode rather than as any surface renders them, a discussion being about the file as it is and an agent asked what to do with a workflow is better served by the graph's own document than by a description of it. The `draft` builder fills it with the **whole of the draft's prompt**, named by its draft-relative path and carrying the draft's name; a draft is one prompt (per `DRS-draft-storage.md` DRS-FR-11), so a discussion about the draft as a whole and a discussion targeted at a fragment of it read the same document. A fragment target adds no material: the passage it names is already inside the whole source, and it appears in `discussion_history` (AGC-FR-06). The two differ in what they read and in nothing else: the sections, their order, and their tags are the same. Every one of them also names the **artifact type** of the material — `skill`, `agent`, `prompt`, `spec`, `flow`, `instructions`, `scenario`, or `scratchpad` (per `ASC-artifact-scanning.md` ASC-FR-02) — what an agent is asked to do with a document turning on what kind of document it is, and one asked to guess from a filename guessing wrong for exactly the artifacts whose filename does not identify them (per `ASC-artifact-scanning.md` ASC-FR-19). The type is the one the Project panel shows for the same path, resolved through the single resolver of ASC-FR-06 rather than re-derived. A draft's prompt is outside the scan, so its type is resolved against the path it would occupy once the draft graduates — its destination root joined with its draft-relative path (per `DRS-draft-storage.md` DRS-FR-07) — the classification the same bytes carry the moment they are published. Material the scan resolves no type for names none: the attribute is absent rather than carrying a value for the absence, which would be a ninth type in a fixed set of eight.
    The `note` builder produces a `note_context` section instead, and no `artifact` section at all. Its body is the note's **current** body as `NTC-notes-storage.md` holds it at the moment of dispatch (NTC-FR-03), so a note edited after its conversation began is discussed as it now reads. Its attributes are `note_id`; `scope_kind`, which is `entity` or `project` (per `NTC-notes-storage.md` NTC-FR-04); `entity_name` and `entity_path` where the note is entity-scoped and that entity currently resolves; `last_known_entity_path` where it is entity-scoped and unresolved (per `NTC-notes-storage.md` NTC-FR-10); and `revision` where the note carries one (per `NTC-notes-storage.md` NTC-FR-18). The entity is named as **scope** and never as material: a note attached to a specification is a remark about something the author noticed, and the file it is filed against is where it is filed rather than what is being discussed — an agent handed that file as the subject would answer a question nobody asked. Where the author does want the file in the conversation, they say so in it, and the agent reads it with `read_file` like any other material it decides it needs.
8. **AGC-FR-08** Every comment in the `discussion_history` section names its author by handle — an agent's nickname and a human's login alike, from the participant the log stamped (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-11).
9. **AGC-FR-09** A comment carrying attachments (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-42) contributes them into the section holding it — `discussion_history` for an earlier comment, `current_comment` for the one that addressed the agent — in the order the comment carries them and in the position that comment occupies. A stored **image** `blob` is carried as **image content** where the resolved endpoint accepts image content (AGC-FR-35) and as metadata where it does not (AGC-FR-37). Every other attachment is metadata in both cases, and always was: a `blob` that is not an image is named by its filename and its media type, a `url` by its label and failing that its address together with the media type it is expected to serve, and a `prompt_proposal` by the prompt it names, and a `proposal` on the terms of AGC-FR-RVQP. Nothing here **fetches**: an address is never resolved, requested, or read, so what a `url` attachment serves reaches no request whatever the endpoint accepts (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-43).
10. **AGC-FR-RVQP** A `proposal` attachment in `discussion_history` names each change with its kind, its outcome, and a bounded excerpt of the text it changed. The text a change offered is rendered for a rejected or an undecided change alone. The rendering is bounded with its section (AGC-FR-11), and an older proposal contributes its counts alone.
    - *Why:* An accepted change already stands in the `artifact` section, and an agent that cannot see which passages a conversation settled proposes into them again.
10. **AGC-FR-10** A comment carrying quotes (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-16) is rendered into the section holding it — `discussion_history` for an earlier comment, `current_comment` for the one that addressed the agent — with its quotes between its author's handle and its own body, each naming the **handle of the participant who wrote the quoted comment** and carrying the **excerpt** the quote holds, in the order the comment holds them. A reply that singled out a passage reaches the agent as a reply to that passage rather than as a remark that merely follows it, which is what a reader of the discussion sees — a card renders a quote as an attributed block above the quoting comment's own body, naming its author (per `../ui/CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-13) — and what an agent answering a conversation several participants are in needs in order to answer the right one. What a quote adds is the **pointer** rather than the text: it names a comment of the same discussion (CMS-FR-16), whose whole body is therefore already in `discussion_history` in its own place. A quote naming a comment the fold produced none for carries its excerpt with no handle rather than being dropped. Quotes are text like every other part of a section and are bounded with it (AGC-FR-11).
11. **AGC-FR-11** A builder bounds the material it gathers, and a section holding less than the whole of its material carries `truncated` and says so in its own text. An agent is never silently handed half a document: it is either given the material or told that what it was given is partial.
12. **AGC-FR-12** A section whose material cannot be read — an artifact that no longer resolves, a file whose bytes do not decode as UTF-8, a note the project no longer holds — is omitted and the remaining sections are still assembled, so one unreadable file never costs the agent the rest of its input. A turn for which neither the material section nor `discussion_history` could be produced fails `context_unavailable` and contributes nothing.
13. **AGC-FR-13** Input is assembled at dispatch. A turn answers the conversation as it stood when the agent was addressed, so a message posted while a turn is in flight belongs to the next turn rather than changing the one already running.
14. **AGC-FR-14** At dispatch the turn resolves the AI API provider the open project resolves to, with the project override applied under the rules of `AAP-ai-api-integrations.md` AAP-FR-16 (through `resolve_agent_provider`, AAP-FR-APRV). The call is made against the base URL and key `resolve_ai_api_endpoint` returns for that provider, carrying the agent's own stored model and reasoning, and carrying that model's own image-input capability and route metadata, which are what AGC-FR-36 and the adapter's route restriction decide on (per `AAP-ai-api-integrations.md` AAP-FR-34, AAP-FR-35, AAP-FR-RTMZ). An agent stores no provider (per `AGR-agent-registry.md` AGR-FR-06), so an agent's turn follows a change of the active provider on its next dispatch, and the provider's own stored model and reasoning selections never reach an agent. A turn dispatched for an agent whose model the resolved provider does not serve is refused `agent_unavailable` before it is registered. For a `custom` provider the adapter is configured with the model's `mode` and, when `mode` is null or absent, selects the route as it does for any unrestricted model (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-ZPGW).
15. **AGC-FR-15** Every way a turn can fail carries its own value of `AgentTurnFailure`, because they call for different corrections: a nickname naming nobody, an agent whose provider or model no longer serves it, a conversation that has been locked, material that could not be read, a host that cannot be reached, credentials the provider refused, a call that timed out, a keychain that would not open, and a model that answered with nothing at all. The vocabulary divides once more, on whether repeating the call that failed could plausibly help: `unreachable`, a provider-call `timed_out`, and `empty_reply` are **recoverable** and everything else is not (`../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-18). The division is what decides whether the failure is retried automatically, and, once the attempts are spent, whether the author is offered the turn again (AGC-FR-31).
16. **AGC-FR-16** A turn that produces an answer delivers it into its origin as a contribution by the agent: the reply is appended through `CMS-comments-storage.md`'s agent write path (CMS-FR-41), stamped with an agent participant carrying the agent's id, its nickname as the handle, the model that produced the answer, and the agent's title as the registry holds it at the moment of the append — a snapshot of what the agent was answering as, taken once and never resolved again (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-65). The result is a comment the fold, the rail, and the Comments panel read exactly as they read a human's.
17. **AGC-FR-17** A delivered turn's comment carries no attachment: the model's answer is appended as a body alone (AGC-FR-16). An agent contributes prose to a conversation and never a file, so every `url` and every `blob` a discussion holds was put there by the participant who attached it. The one exception is the comment a turn ends on by proposing (`../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-15), which carries exactly one reference attachment and nothing besides — a `proposal` where the material is a draft's prompt and a `prompt_proposal` where it is a prompt artifact of the project (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-60, CMS-FR-66) — and that is not a file either: it holds no bytes and no address, naming a candidate this module never composed, never reads, and never applies.
18. **AGC-FR-18** A turn that fails or is cancelled contributes nothing, however many times it reached the provider on its way there. No partial answer, no placeholder, and no note about the failure is appended to any conversation — the failure is reported to the surface through the turn's own state (AGC-FR-21) and, where it is recoverable, through the registry the surface reads its offer to retry from (AGC-FR-31), and a conversation gains a line only when an agent actually answered or actually asked. A registry entry is not a contribution: it is memory of the running session, it is in no log, it is in no discussion, and it is in nothing any reader of the conversation ever sees. A question already posted when a turn is afterwards cancelled stays where it was posted, no route here removing a line a conversation has taken (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-04).
19. **AGC-FR-19** A turn whose conversation is locked fails `discussion_locked` and appends nothing, whether the lock was already in place when the turn was dispatched or was set while it was in flight. A lock is what stops a conversation taking further contributions (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-17), and an agent is subject to it exactly as an author is.
20. **AGC-FR-20** `cancel_agent_turn(turn_id)` abandons the model call, terminates the turn as `cancelled`, and appends nothing. It succeeds against a turn that has already terminated without changing it, so a cancellation racing a delivery never rewrites what was delivered. A turn is cancelled on the same terms when its agent is deleted or withdrawn from the project (per `AGR-agent-registry.md` AGR-FR-11 and AGR-FR-15), when the note a turn whose target is that note belongs to is deleted (AGC-FR-30), when the project closes, and when the application quits. A turn already `awaiting_reply` has terminated, so an explicit cancel leaves it as it stands and its question where it was posted; but each of those three routes discards its registration, an agent that is gone being owed nothing and a project that is closed holding no conversation to be owed it in.
21. **AGC-FR-21** `"agent turn state changed"` is emitted when a turn is registered, each time the tool calls active in it change while it runs (AGC-FR-34), and again when it reaches a terminal state, and carries the whole `AgentTurn` every time. A terminal event is emitted exactly once per turn, and no event carries a turn's id after it — including the event carrying `awaiting_reply`, which is that turn's one terminal event and is followed by no other however long the author takes to reply. A consumer watching only events therefore converges on the same outstanding set `list_agent_turns` would return, without polling, on two conditions it can meet from what it already does: it reads the state it was sent rather than treating every terminal event as a removal, an `awaiting_reply` turn staying outstanding rather than leaving; and it drops an `awaiting_reply` turn when it dispatches for that same agent and origin, which is the one thing that retires one (AGC-FR-29) and which the consumer is itself the cause of. Retirement emits nothing, so an outstanding set is never corrected by an event it did not expect. A terminal event carrying `failed` also carries `retry_permitted`, so a consumer that was watching when a turn failed learns from that one event whether an offer to retry stands and never has to derive it from the failure value; a consumer that was not watching — one mounted afterwards — reads the same thing from `list_recoverable_agent_turn_failures` (AGC-FR-31). Entering, replacing, and retiring a registry entry emit nothing of their own, on the same terms retirement does.
22. **AGC-FR-22** `list_agent_turns(origin?)` returns the turns still **outstanding** — those `running` and those `awaiting_reply` — most recently started first, each carrying the tool calls active in it and the order they were activated in (AGC-FR-33), so a surface mounted or remounted while an agent is thinking renders what is outstanding and what each agent is doing at that moment rather than appearing idle, and one mounted a week after an agent asked something still knows that turn is open and draws nothing for it (AGC-FR-28, per `../ui/CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-ZOLW, CTA-FR-JQDM). An `origin` narrows the result to one conversation and a null `origin` returns every outstanding turn anywhere, which is what a roster reporting who is currently answering needs (per `../ui/AGT-agents.md` AGT-FR-05). A turn that is `delivered`, `failed`, or `cancelled` is absent under either form.
23. **AGC-FR-23** A turn exists only in memory and only for the lifetime of the running application. Nothing about a turn — its request, its context, its state, its active tool calls, or its failure — is written to disk, because the contribution delivered into the conversation is the whole of the record and a turn interrupted by a relaunch left nothing to resume. The registration of a turn `awaiting_reply` is memory on the same terms and outlives a relaunch no more than any other: the question itself is in the conversation's log where the author can still read and answer it, and whom that answer dispatches is then the surface's ordinary rule (per `../ui/CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-EACI, CTA-FR-QUXJ) rather than anything this module remembered. Every conversation carries who is active in it in its own comments, so an answer written whenever it comes reaches the agent the author last named there, and one written into a conversation where the author named nobody addresses nobody and waits.
24. **AGC-FR-24** Every turn that is running is reported through `PRG-progress-reporting.md` as an in-flight operation of kind `agent`, so work an agent is doing in a conversation the author is not currently looking at is visible in the status bar like any other long-running operation. A turn is running from the moment it holds a call slot (AGC-FR-25). The operation is registered at that moment, so a turn that waits for a slot is queued work and is not reported. The operation ends when the turn reaches any terminal state, `awaiting_reply` among them: an agent waiting on the author is not working, and a status bar that said otherwise would report a machine as busy for as long as a person took to read a question. A turn that ends before it holds a slot registers no operation.
    - **Label.** The label is supplied by this module and names the agent and the subject of the conversation. For a discussion or a comment about a draft it is `@<nickname> is thinking about <draft name>…`, and for one about an artifact it is `@<nickname> is thinking about <artifact path>…`, where the artifact path is the project-relative path of the artifact. For a note it is `@<nickname> is thinking…`. A draft whose name cannot be read is named by its draft id. The label is complete as supplied and the status bar adds nothing to it (per `PRG-progress-reporting.md` PRG-FR-HDQS).
    - **Activation.** The operation carries the activation destination `{ type: "discussion", discussionId }` with the id of the turn's discussion (per `PRG-progress-reporting.md` PRG-FR-KXQW), so the status bar reveals the conversation through the one `revealDiscussion` route (per `../ui/CVP-conversation-presentation.md` CVP-FR-06).
25. **AGC-FR-25** The number of turns holding a model call at one moment is bounded. A turn holds its slot for the whole of its loop rather than for one round of it, so a turn that reaches for tools between calls does not surrender and re-contend for a slot partway through an answer it is still composing. It releases the slot on reaching any terminal state, `awaiting_reply` among them, so a turn that ended by asking the author something holds nothing at all while it waits and cannot crowd out an agent that is actually working. A turn dispatched beyond the bound is still registered and still `running`, and it begins its first call as a slot frees. It is not reported as in-flight progress while it waits (AGC-FR-24). A dispatch is delayed by load and never refused for it (AGC-FR-03).
26. **AGC-FR-26** No key, and no part of one, appears in a turn record, an event payload, a failure value, a progress label, or a log line. A key exists in this module only for the duration of the call that presents it and is never retained between turns, on the same terms every other key path in the application observes (per `AAP-ai-api-integrations.md` AAP-FR-08).
27. **AGC-FR-27** Every command in the contract surface exists with a typed payload in the walking-skeleton build. A stub may register a turn, skip the model entirely, and deliver a canned reply through the same append path, provided it returns the documented shapes and emits the documented events.
28. **AGC-FR-28** A turn that terminates `awaiting_reply` **is discarded**, and discarded silently. Its exchange, everything it had gathered, its agent session and that session's temp directory (`../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-24), its concurrency slot (AGC-FR-25), and its progress operation (AGC-FR-24) are all released at that moment, exactly as they are for a turn that delivered. Nothing is held open, nothing is watched, and nothing polls: what survives is the registration alone — the agent, the origin, and the state — which is what `list_agent_turns` keeps returning (AGC-FR-22), so that a surface renders nothing for it (per `../ui/CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-ZOLW) and a fresh turn for that agent in that origin retires it (AGC-FR-29). The registration decides nothing about who a later comment reaches: that is the surface's own rule, read from the conversation's comments (per `../ui/CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-LCFU, CTA-FR-QUXJ), and an agent owed an answer is dispatched to because the author is still writing to it rather than because this module remembered the question. The timeout of CVL-FR-16 bounds a turn's own work and is spent when the question is posted or the proposal recorded, so **no bound of any kind applies to the waiting**: an author may answer or decide in a minute, in three weeks, or never, and neither a question never answered nor a proposal never decided is collected, expired, retried, or reported as a failure. It costs one registration and ends with the running application (AGC-FR-23), leaving the conversation with something in it that anyone may still answer — and, for a proposal, a candidate that stands until the author decides it: beside the draft until they do or until the draft is gone (per `DCP-draft-change-proposals.md` DCP-FR-19, DCP-FR-02), and in the project's own proposal store until they do (per `PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-20).
29. **AGC-FR-29** An answer is given by a **fresh turn** and never by resuming the one that asked or offered. When a turn is dispatched for an agent in an origin where that agent is `awaiting_reply`, the awaiting turn is retired to `delivered` — it did deliver, its contribution being the question or the proposal — and the new turn proceeds as any other: its input is assembled at dispatch from the conversation as it now stands (AGC-FR-13), so the agent's own question and the author's reply, or its proposal and the author's decision with whatever feedback they wrote (per `DCP-draft-change-proposals.md` DCP-FR-15 and `PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-16), are simply the last two comments of the `discussion_history` section (AGC-FR-06), in among everything else the conversation has accumulated. A **question set** reads the same way at greater length: its questions and the author's chosen answers are the comments the submission appended, standing in that section in the order they were asked (AGC-FR-BWZP). An agent whose proposal was accepted additionally reads the changed file in its `artifact` section, that section being assembled from the draft or from the project file as it now stands (AGC-FR-07), so it learns what its rewrite became rather than being told. Nothing of the earlier turn carries over — not its exchange, not its tool results, not the material it had gathered — so an agent re-reads or re-searches whatever it still needs. This is what makes the wait cost nothing to hold and what makes an answer arriving days later, in a conversation reopened after a relaunch by a build that never saw the question asked, an ordinary turn with an ordinary history in front of it. Retirement emits no event (AGC-FR-21).
30. **AGC-FR-30** A turn whose origin target is a `note` whose **note has been deleted** is cancelled and contributes nothing (AGC-FR-20, AGC-FR-18), on exactly the terms a turn is cancelled when the project closes: the model call in flight is abandoned, the exchange is discarded, the session ends, and the conversation the turn would have written into is gone with the note (per `NTC-notes-storage.md` NTC-FR-21). A registration `awaiting_reply` for that origin is discarded with it, an author who deleted the note owing no answer. A turn dispatched afterwards for that origin is refused before it is registered, its discussion resolving to nothing (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-62), so no comment, no turn, and no index entry is created for a deleted note by any route or any race.
31. **AGC-FR-31** A turn terminating `failed` with a recoverable failure is entered in the **recovery registry**: an in-memory map holding **at most one** entry per conversation, which is what makes an offer to retry unambiguous — the author is offered the most recent thing that failed there and never a choice among several. A later recoverable terminal failure in that conversation **replaces** the entry, retiring the one it displaces. An entry is removed when its manual retry is successfully registered (AGC-FR-32), when any later **human-authored** comment is appended to that conversation — an agent-authored one retires nothing, since an agent answering is not the author saying they have moved on — and when the conversation itself goes, by the routes that already take conversations (AGC-FR-30, per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-39). There is no bound on how many conversations hold an entry at once, one entry per conversation being small and a project-wide cap being a rule that would drop the entry the author was about to use.
    An entry holds the `AgentTurn` and nothing beside it: the turn's id, the agent, the origin, the trigger comment, the final failure, `retry_permitted`, and the instants the turn began and ended, which is the ordering a later comment or a later failure is compared against. It holds **no** assembled request, no exchange, no tool call or result, no credential, and no text of any answer, so the whole registry is safe to hand to a surface and there is nothing in it to redact. It is memory of the running application on the terms of AGC-FR-23 and survives a relaunch no better than the turn it describes.
32. **AGC-FR-32** `retry_agent_turn(turn_id)` **registers a new turn** for the failed one's agent, origin, and trigger comment, and returns it `running` on the terms `dispatch_agent_turn` returns any turn (AGC-FR-02). The new turn assembles its input afresh from the conversation as it now stands (AGC-FR-13), begins its own agent session, resolves its own endpoint and key, and carries the retry policy of `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-19 like any other; **nothing** of the failed turn carries over — not its exchange, not its tool results, not its session, and not its credentials — and the failed turn is neither mutated nor resurrected, keeping its own id and its own terminal state for as long as anything holds it. Registering the new turn removes the registry entry, so the offer is consumed by being taken.
    It refuses any turn that is not its conversation's **current** entry — a turn that never failed, one whose failure was not recoverable, one already retried, and one displaced by a later failure alike — and a refusal registers nothing and removes nothing. The refusal carries the same `AgentTurnFailure` vocabulary every other refusal here carries, so a conversation that has been locked since the failure refuses `discussion_locked` and an agent since deleted refuses `agent_not_found`, exactly as a fresh dispatch would.
33. **AGC-FR-33** A `running` turn carries the tool calls that are **active** in it: those the loop has begun and has not yet finished, each naming the tool the model called — a portable tool's own name, and a provider-native tool's entry type (per `../tools/TLC-tool-conventions.md` TLC-FR-02, TLC-FR-21) — under an id unique within the turn and an **activation sequence**, an integer ascending with activation, unique within the turn, and never reused. The sequence is what orders them, so two calls a single model response began are ordered among themselves and a consumer reads which one is the latest from the record rather than from the order events reached it (per `../ui/CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-KWOF). A call enters the list when the loop begins it and leaves it the moment it succeeds, refuses, or is abandoned (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-33), so the list is what is happening now and never what has happened: a turn that has made no tool call carries an empty list, a turn in any terminal state carries an empty list, and the list a `list_agent_turns` reading returns is the same list every event carries. It names tools and their order and nothing else — no argument the model composed, no result a tool produced, and no part of the exchange is in it (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-26).
34. **AGC-FR-34** A change to a running turn's active tool calls emits `"agent turn state changed"` for that turn: one event when a call is activated and one when a call leaves the list, each carrying the whole `AgentTurn` with its state still `running` and its `active_tool_calls` as they now stand. Each event is a whole reading of the turn rather than a difference from the one before it, and the order a status is chosen by is inside the payload rather than in the sequence the events arrived (AGC-FR-33), so a consumer reads what to show from the event it holds, derives nothing from arrival order, and is corrected by the next event wherever it missed one. These events change no state of the turn and are not terminal: they are emitted only between registration and the terminal event, never after it, and a turn's terminal event carries an empty list whatever was active when it ended. They say what an agent is doing and never what it is doing it with, which is what lets a conversation report activity without reporting content (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-27). Nothing else about the module changes with them: the progress operation keeps the label it was registered with (AGC-FR-24), the turn holds its slot on the same terms (AGC-FR-25), and no key is in the payload (AGC-FR-26).

35. **AGC-FR-35** A section carries **ordered content parts** rather than one block of prose, and an image occupies a part of its own in the position the material puts it. Two sources contribute images and no third does. The first is the **draft's prompt**: on a turn whose target is a `draft` the `artifact` section holds the prompt's text interleaved with the images the prompt embeds, each in the position its Markdown reference occupies, read through `DAS-draft-assets.md`'s `read_prompt_images` (DAS-FR-22). The second is a **comment's own image attachments**: a stored image `blob` on any comment of `discussion_history` or on the `current_comment`, read through `CMS-comments-storage.md`'s `read_attachment_bytes` (CMS-FR-67). An image part carries the **bytes**, the **media type**, and the surrounding context the material gives it — the `context` `read_prompt_images` returns for a prompt image, which is its Markdown reference together with its alt text (per `DAS-draft-assets.md` DAS-FR-22), and the filename and the comment it belongs to for an attachment — so an agent is never handed a picture with nothing but a filename beside it. Both sources hand this module the **bytes themselves** rather than a handle: `read_prompt_images` returns each resolved image's `data` in its own entry, and `read_attachment_bytes` returns an attachment's content on the same terms (per `CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-67), so assembling a turn takes no second fetch and no path composed here. A prompt image that resolved to **no** draft-owned asset carries no bytes to send: it contributes its metadata in its own position on the terms of AGC-FR-37, whichever endpoint is serving, because there is nothing to show rather than because anything was refused. The ordering of text and images inside a section is the ordering the prompt or the comment history has, because a diagram means what the sentence before it says it means. Sections, their tags, and their order are unchanged (AGC-FR-06): images are content within a section rather than a fourth section.
36. **AGC-FR-36** Whether a turn may carry image content is decided **before the request is constructed**, from the resolved endpoint's own capability (AGC-FR-14, per `AAP-ai-api-integrations.md` AAP-FR-35), and it is **all or nothing for the turn**. A turn whose endpoint accepts image content carries every image its material holds; a turn whose endpoint does not carries none of them. No request is built with some of its images and not others, no request is sent and then repaired, and a request refused for any reason is never re-issued as a partial multimodal one — the loop repeats a failed call byte-for-byte or not at all (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-20). A capability the provider declares nothing about counts as absent, so the fallback is what an unknown endpoint gets (AGC-FR-37).
37. **AGC-FR-37** Where image content is unsupported, the turn sends a **text-only request carrying the normal material and safe metadata for each omitted image in its original position**. The metadata names the image's media type together with what identifies it — its Markdown reference and alt text for a prompt image, its filename or label for a comment attachment, on the terms that attachment metadata is already written (AGC-FR-09) — and it carries **no bytes, no encoded payload, and nothing fetched from any address**. An image reference the prompt carries that resolves to no draft-owned asset — missing, malformed, unresolved by label, external, or escaping the draft — is metadata in this same way whichever endpoint is serving, there being no bytes for it in either case: it arrives already marked unresolved and carrying its own `context`, so this module places that context where the picture would have been and neither raises it as a failure nor drops the fact that an image stands there (per `DAS-draft-assets.md` DAS-FR-07, DAS-FR-22). An unsafe destination is therefore never a reason to refuse a turn and never a reason to read anything: an author who typed a bad path is answered about the rest of their prompt. The turn is an **ordinary successful conversation**: it is not a typed failure, it carries no value of `AgentTurnFailure`, it is not recoverable, it is entered in no recovery registry, and its answer is delivered into its origin exactly as any answer is (AGC-FR-16). What records it is the turn's own `images_omitted`, true from the moment the decision of AGC-FR-36 is taken and carried in every payload that turn appears in, its terminal event among them (AGC-FR-21), so the surface that dispatched the turn can say that the pictures were not sent (per `../ui/CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-ARBB).
38. **AGC-FR-38** The fallback is **UI and diagnostic state alone**. Nothing about it is added to the agent's instructions, and nothing about it alters the author's prompt, the comment's text, the draft's Markdown, or any line of the conversation's log: no note is appended, no body is rewritten, and no comment is marked. The compiled prompt is what it always is (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-07), the metadata standing in for an image sits inside the material section where the image would have been and nowhere else, and an author who later configures a model that takes images asks again and is answered with the pictures, the conversation on disk being byte-for-byte what it was.
39. **AGC-FR-39** The **notice registry** holds, per conversation, the most recent turn that ended with `images_omitted` true, and `list_agent_turn_image_notices(origin?)` is how a surface reads it. An entry is **replaced** by a later turn in that conversation that also omitted images and **retired** by a later turn in that conversation that carried its images successfully or carried none at all, so what a surface reads is the current state of that conversation rather than a history of it. Entering, replacing, and retiring an entry emit nothing of their own, on the same terms the recovery registry's do (AGC-FR-21). The registry is memory of the running session and is a contribution to nothing: it is in no log, in no discussion, and in nothing any reader of the conversation ever sees (AGC-FR-18), and it ends with the running application (AGC-FR-23). It exists so that a surface mounted after a turn ended still knows to say that its pictures were not sent, exactly as it knows a failure still stands (AGC-FR-31).
40. **AGC-FR-40** No image reaches any path but the conversational one. Asset bytes and attachment bytes are read for a turn's own request and for nothing else: nothing here hands one to `GRD-graduation.md`, to an implementation hand-off, or to `AIC-agentic-integrations.md`, and no image of any kind is written into a comment, a proposal, a log line, an event payload, or a progress label. A turn that ended keeps nothing: the bytes it read live as long as its exchange does and go with it (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-26).
38. **AGC-FR-ZPKW** Every operational part of a turn is **session state and is never persisted**. The `AgentTurn` record, the `AgentRequest` assembled for it, the `AgentExchange` it accumulated (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-26), its `ActiveToolCall` list, its `AgentTurnFailure`, the recovery registry of AGC-FR-31, the image-notice registry of AGC-FR-37, and the agent session the turn ran inside (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-24) are all held in memory for the running application alone. None of them is written to the conversation log, to a checkpoint, to a project file, or into a statistics event payload, and none of them is recoverable after the application closes: a relaunch finds **no turn in flight**, no request, no exchange, no active tool call, no recovery offer, and no notice, and it reconstructs none of them from anything on disk. A **pending question set** is no exception to any of it: the set is durable and this module holds none of it, so a relaunch that restores a discussion's questions restores no agent turn and no model exchange for them (AGC-FR-DKPS). What a turn leaves behind is the comment it contributed, persisted by `CMS-comments-storage.md`, and — for a draft's conversation — the lifecycle and usage lines of AGC-FR-YQMD and AGC-FR-BVNT, which are **already appended** by the time the turn is terminal and which a relaunch neither removes, rewrites, nor replays into the turn model. Clearing operational state and keeping those lines are therefore two different things about two different stores, and neither reaches into the other.
39. **AGC-FR-YQMD** A turn whose origin target is a `draft` records **exactly one `RefinementTurnRecord`** for that draft, composed when the turn reaches a terminal state and appended through `DSS-draft-statistics-storage.md` (DSS-FR-EKZI) keyed by `origin.target.draft_id`. It carries the turn's own id as its stable identity, the draft, the discussion, whether it was fragment-targeted, the instants execution began and ended, the terminal outcome, and `reached_model`. `reached_model` is **true** once one `complete` invocation has been made for the turn (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-11) and **false** where the turn terminated before any — a refused dispatch, a context the builder could not read, a cancellation before the first call — so a turn that reached nothing is recorded as having reached nothing rather than left out and guessed at. One model-reaching logical turn is therefore **one** refinement interaction whatever number of physical attempts, retries, or portable-tool rounds it took (per `DSS-draft-statistics-storage.md` DSS-FR-FXAE), and a turn with `reached_model` false contributes **no** interaction and **no** duration. The record's interval is the turn's **own execution**: it opens at `started_at` and ends at the terminal state, so a turn ending in `awaiting_reply` ends its interval at the question, the question set, or the proposal it asked with, and the wait for the author's reply or decision — however long, and whether or not it ever comes — is outside the record and is recorded nowhere, this module holding nothing open while it waits (AGC-FR-29). A turn whose `reached_model` is false contributes **no Refinement duration at all** however long it ran before it stopped (per `DSS-draft-statistics-storage.md` DSS-FR-IYRG), so the record of it says a turn happened and spent nothing on a model rather than adding time no model was asked for. This module reports the turn's **whole** interval and applies no boundary of its own: a turn that began before the draft's telemetry activation boundary and ended after it is recorded whole here and contributes its post-boundary part alone, the clip being the fold's (per `DSS-draft-statistics-storage.md` DSS-FR-KYTB) so one rule decides it for every producer. The record is composed once and appended once; the append is asynchronous and its failure never affects the turn (per `DSS-draft-statistics-storage.md` DSS-FR-TUMX).
40. **AGC-FR-BVNT** Each reliable provider-reported usage record the loop surfaces for a **draft-scoped** turn is recorded as one `RefinementUsageRecord` for that draft, carrying the provider usage record's own stable identity, the logical round and the physical attempt it belongs to, its representation, and the token values the provider actually reported. A record is appended **once per distinct provider usage identity**, so a retry that reports its own usage contributes its own record while adding no interaction and creating no second turn, and a duplicated line folds away on that identity (per `DSS-draft-statistics-storage.md` DSS-FR-AWKD). This module reports what the loop reported and derives nothing: it never estimates a token count from the input, from the answer, from a character count, from a model's limit, or from a price, and a direction the provider did not report is simply absent from the record.
41. **AGC-FR-LHRC** A turn whose origin target is an `artifact` or a `note` records **no lifecycle record and no usage record anywhere**: it has no draft to record against, and no statistics log gains a line because of it. No record a turn of either kind produces ever carries an `InputSection`, an assembled request, an exchange message, an active tool call's arguments, a tool result, an image or its metadata, a comment body, a rationale, a proposal's candidate, an agent's answer, a failure diagnostic, or any part of a key (AGC-FR-26). A record carries identities, instants, an outcome, a boolean, and reported token numbers, and the whole of what it says about the conversation is that a turn happened in it.
42. **AGC-FR-NKWM** Every command of this module returns the typed `no_project_open` error when no project is open.
43. **AGC-FR-VRHM** A **discussion** turn may end by recording one pending question set instead of answering (per `../tools/ADQ-ask-discussion-questions-tool.md` ADQ-FR-FQPA). It terminates `awaiting_reply` on exactly the terms a turn that asked a question does (AGC-FR-28), and it **appends no comment** — the one `awaiting_reply` that leaves the conversation's log byte-for-byte as it was.
44. **AGC-FR-DKPS** The pending set is **not** a turn record and not session state: it is durable, is owned by `CMS-comments-storage.md` (CMS-FR-XWDA), and outlives both the turn and the application. Everything operational about that turn is discarded on the terms of AGC-FR-ZPKW, so a relaunch restores the set and no turn, request, exchange, or registration.
45. **AGC-FR-TQLC** A submission **retires or discards** every registration outstanding on the discussion it answers, without resuming any of them — the turn that asked among them, whether or not that agent is among the agents the submission dispatches to. A turn is never resumed, re-entered, or continued, and its exchange is long gone. The retirement is by **discussion** rather than by turn: a question a turn posted before the set was recorded is a question the submission has overtaken, so its turn is owed nothing further either.
    - *Why:* The author has answered the discussion, which is what a turn awaiting a reply there was waiting for. Retiring only the turn that recorded the set would need a stored link from a turn to the set it asked with, and would leave an earlier question outstanding against a conversation that has already moved past it.
46. **AGC-FR-BWZP** The answers reach the agents as **fresh later turns**, dispatched once per active agent for the whole submission with the final answer comment as the trigger (per `../ui/CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-PXJB). Each assembles its input at dispatch as any turn does (AGC-FR-13), so every question and answer comment stands in `discussion_history` and the final answer in `current_comment` (AGC-FR-06).
47. **AGC-FR-XRLU** No individual question or answer comment of a submission dispatches a turn of its own, so a set of ten questions costs one turn per active agent rather than ten. A dispatch refused there is refused on the ordinary terms (AGC-FR-02) and rolls back no comment, the append having already committed.
48. **AGC-FR-INFL** A builder places every comment body in `discussion_history` and in `current_comment` with its provider citation markers removed, by the rule of `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-VSSU, and changes nothing else in it. The stored body is unchanged by it.

## Non-functional requirements
- Dispatching is asynchronous and never blocks the surface that requested it: the command returns as soon as the turn is registered, whatever the model is about to do.
- Input assembly reads the material under discussion and the conversation's own log and nothing else; it contacts no network, runs no binary, and reads no file outside the project's active worktree. What the agent gathers afterwards is bounded by the tools rather than by this module: the search tools read in-memory indexes, the two that open a file are confined to the project by the session's own filesystem instance, and the web tools a provider serves natively are carried out by that provider inside the model call itself, this module reaching no network of its own for any of them (per `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-32).
- The recovery registry is one entry per conversation and each entry is one `AgentTurn`, so it costs nothing measurable however long a session runs and however many conversations a project holds. It is a map, not a queue: nothing drains it, nothing polls it, and no timer expires an entry, so an offer to retry that the author never takes costs exactly what one they take immediately costs.
- A turn that ends by asking is the cheapest turn there is, and deliberately so: it costs the calls it made before it asked and then releases everything. Waiting is not an operation here — there is no suspended task, no timer, no queue of pending questions, and nothing to garbage-collect — which is what makes a delay of weeks and a question never answered cost exactly the same as one answered immediately, and what makes the whole mechanism survivable across a relaunch that discards it.
- The price of holding nothing is that an agent answering a reply re-reads what it read before, so a conversation settled over three questions costs three turns' gathering rather than one. That is the right trade at this scale: the material is small, the gathering is local, and an exchange kept alive for a week would be a week's worth of context assembled against a project that had moved on.
- A failed or cancelled turn leaves the conversation byte-for-byte as it was, so an author retrying a question is retrying against unchanged material.
- Several agents answering one message are independent from the moment they are dispatched: none waits on another, and the conversation accepts their answers in whatever order they arrive.
- A turn's active tool calls are a few short records held beside the turn and emitted as they change, so reporting what an agent is doing costs one event per call begun and one per call finished and no poll anywhere. A turn that reaches for no tool emits exactly the two events every turn emits.
- The durable record a turn leaves is two kinds of short line appended asynchronously, so a conversation costs the same whether or not anything is reading its statistics, and a failed append costs the turn nothing at all.
- Clearing operational state on relaunch is free: there is nothing to clear, because nothing of it was written down. The statistics lines a turn appended are on disk and are read by folding them, so the two stores never have to be reconciled with one another.
- The bound of AGC-FR-25 is a property of this module rather than of any provider, so an author who has addressed a dozen agents at once does not present a dozen simultaneous requests to one endpoint.
