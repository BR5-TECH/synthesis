# Action control

**Spec code:** `ACT`

## Intent
The single floating control every main-viewport tab carries in the bottom-trailing corner of its surface, and the actions it gathers. A tab shows exactly one thing — a draft, an artifact, a plain text file, a Flow, a diff of a file, a past revision of one — and everything that thing affords is gathered behind one small mark of chrome rather than spread across a band of buttons, so a tab being read or written in stays about what it shows until something is asked of it. What the control offers is decided by the item behind the tab rather than by the tab: every item affords **Discuss**, which opens a conversation about the whole of it and addresses whichever of the project's agents the author wants an answer from; a draft additionally affords **Graduate**, **Publish to GitHub**, and **Archive**. One control, one place, one gesture, whatever is open — so the way to start a conversation about a specification is the way to start one about a workflow, and neither is learned twice. Out of scope: what a discussion does once it exists — its cards, its replies, its locking and resolution — which `CMT-comments.md` renders and continues and `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` stores; the one contextual surface a discussion is read in and the route that reveals it, which `CVP-conversation-presentation.md` decides and this control only makes room for; the turn an addressed agent answers with, which `../core/AGC-agent-conversations.md` carries; the three actions a draft alone affords, whose behaviour is `NAW-new-artifact.md`'s; and comments on a passage of a text, which the author starts from a selection in the editor (`CMT-comments.md`) rather than from this control.

## User stories
- As an author reading a specification, I want to ask an agent what to do with it without leaving the tab or copying it anywhere.
- As an author, I want the same gesture to start a conversation whatever kind of thing is open, so I never hunt for where the action went.
- As an author, I want the control out of the way of the document until I reach for it, because a row of buttons over a page I am reading is a row of buttons I am not pressing.
- As an author reviewing a diff, I want to raise the whole change with a colleague or an agent from where I am reading it.

- Layout notes: the collapsed control is a single graphical control in the bottom-trailing corner of the surface the tab shows, and the discussions control — where the tab carries one (ACT-FR-20) — sits immediately leading of it on the same line, so the two read as one cluster and neither displaces the host's own corner controls, which keep the trailing end of that line. Expanded, the actions stack in a column directly above the control, trailing-aligned with it, reading Discuss, Graduate, Archive from the top with whichever of the three the item does not afford simply absent. The composer is free of the control's corner rather than anchored to it — horizontally centred and sitting in the window's lower quarter — at the measure the shared composer holds in every owner surface, so the surface an author starts writing in is not a narrower one than the one they keep writing in a moment later. It carries no header: its pending attachments sit in a strip between its text area and its control row, its Attach and Post controls trail on that row, and what is being discussed is carried by its placeholder text and its accessible name rather than by a title bar. The discussion panel floats at the tab's trailing side, bounded in width and height to a share of the tab, its cards in one column at the measure a card holds in the rail, the unresolved discussions leading it in the order they were opened and the resolved disclosure closing it, and it scrolls within itself. The composer is opaque over whatever of the window it reaches, exactly as the discussion panel is opaque over whatever of the tab it reaches. The composer and the panel's message list are the shared `DiscussionComposer` and `DiscussionSurface` components, so they look and behave as they do in the Editor rail, the draft discussion column, and the conversation tab.

## UI contract boundary
- **Owned by the UI**: the control, its two states, its position, and the geometry that keeps it clear of a page; the derivation of the action set from the item the tab is bound to and the fixed order the entries render in; the enablement of each entry; the floating opening composer, its text, its pending attachments, its mention picker, its use of the shared composer, its session-kept text and single-flight posting, and its Ctrl/Cmd+Enter accelerator (ACT-FR-13, ACT-FR-14, ACT-FR-16, ACT-FR-28); the discussions control and the floating discussion panel, its arrangement, its bounds, and its dismissal; the within-tab mutual exclusion of ACT-FR-12; and the inline surfacing of every failure the operations below report. The discussion rendering in the rail and in the panel is the shared surface `CMT-comments.md` and `CVP-conversation-presentation.md` own, and introduces no operation here.
- **Delegated to backend (abstract)**:
  - `"open discussion (target, fragment, body, attachments)"` — owned by `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` — is what the composer's post invokes, the target naming the draft or the artifact the tab is bound to (ACT-FR-02) and the fragment absent (ACT-FR-LXKD).
  - `"dispatch agent turn"` — owned by `../core/AGC-agent-conversations.md` — is what it invokes afterwards for each agent the message addressed (ACT-FR-16). Revealing the new discussion in its owner surface is a UI-owned follow-up of `CVP-conversation-presentation.md` rather than a further backend call, and everything the resulting discussion does thereafter is invoked from the shared surface that shows it rather than here.
  - `"list discussions for target"` — owned by `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` — serves the floating panel on a tab that lends the rail no margin (ACT-FR-20). Where the rail renders the discussions instead, it is `CMT-comments.md` that invokes it.
  - The agents a tag may resolve to are read through `"list project agents"`, owned by `../core/AGR-agent-registry.md`, and the mention picker offering them is owned by `AGT-agents.md`; neither introduces an operation here.
  - **Graduate**, **Publish to GitHub**, and **Archive** invoke the draft operations `NAW-new-artifact.md` declares and introduce no operation here.

## Functional requirements
1. **ACT-FR-01** The action control is a single control floating in the bottom-trailing corner of the surface a main-viewport tab shows. Every tab bound to an item carries exactly one and never more: the New Artifact tab (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-05), the Editor tab (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-64), the Flow tab (per `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-34), the Diff tab (per `DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-39), and the History detail tab (per `HVW-history-viewer.md` HVW-FR-10). It is the tab's own chrome and is always present there, in every mode and every arrangement that tab offers.
2. **ACT-FR-02** The control is bound to exactly one **item** — the thing its tab shows — and everything it offers is decided by that item. The item is the draft a New Artifact tab is open on, and otherwise the project file the tab is showing: the artifact or plain text file of an Editor tab, the Flow of a Flow tab (the Flow file is an artifact target), and, for the two tabs that render something other than the file's current contents, the file **at its current identity in the project** rather than the version in front of the author — the file a Diff tab is comparing, and the artifact a History detail tab is showing a revision of. A conversation opened from a diff or from a past revision is therefore the same conversation the Editor tab on that file shows, there being one file to talk about however it is being read.
3. **ACT-FR-03** The control has exactly two states. **Collapsed** it is a single graphical control and nothing else. **Expanded** — reached by activating it — it renders the item's actions as a column of labelled entries directly above it and turns itself into a dismissal. It collapses again on a second activation, on Escape, on a pointer-down anywhere else in the tab, and the moment an action it offered is activated.
4. **ACT-FR-04** The set of actions is decided by the item (ACT-FR-02) and by whether the tab can act on it, and is exactly this. Every item affords **Discuss** (ACT-FR-13). A draft affords **Graduate**, **Publish to GitHub**, and **Archive** as well (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-17, NAW-FR-28, NAW-FR-GKBP), whatever its prompt would classify as, a draft being a specification in the making. A **History detail tab**, which renders a past revision and offers no editing surface, affords **Discuss** alone whatever its item's type. A **Diff tab** affords Discuss alone: it is one reading of one comparison of a file, so the actions that change a file belong on the file's own tab even though the target revision here is editable (per `DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-39, DFV-FR-41).
   - *Why:* An action that changes an item is taken where that item is being worked on, not from a reading of a past state of it.
5. **ACT-FR-05** The entries render in the fixed order **Discuss**, **Graduate**, **Publish to GitHub**, **Archive**, with whichever of the four the item does not afford simply absent rather than rendered inactive. The order is the same wherever the control appears. Each is a named entry rather than a bare glyph.
6. **ACT-FR-06** An action the item affords but cannot take at this moment renders **disabled** rather than absent, and states why on hover. Only the composition of ACT-FR-04 removes an entry.
7. **ACT-FR-07** A tab whose item no longer resolves in the project — the file was deleted, or moved where the tab could not follow it — carries a control whose every entry is disabled. The tab is not closed on the author's behalf.
8. **ACT-FR-08** The control travels with nothing. It holds its corner while the surface scrolls, while a region that comes and goes is shown or hidden — a file rail, a History rail, a comment rail, a find panel — and while a canvas is panned and zoomed, so it is in the same place on a tab the moment after it was in that place.
9. **ACT-FR-09** Where the tab sets its content on a **page** inset from the tab and laid on a field (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-63), the control stands in the field beside the page and covers no part of it at any window size. That is a question of width: where the field left beside the page is wide enough to hold the control clear, the page runs the full height of the tab; where it is too narrow for that, a strip of field is held open below the page instead. It is the field actually beside the page that decides which of the two holds, so a page slid toward the leading edge to widen the comment margin (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-63) is judged on where it now sits; and where the cards have left that margin for a column below the page (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-64), the control keeps its corner of the tab over that column exactly as it keeps it over the margin. The margin runs to the page's own foot rather than ending above the control (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-62), the two sharing the field's trailing edge: the collapsed resolved disclosure stands beside the control and shares no part of it. The control's corner is the **tab's** own. Where the page shares the tab with a second column — the discussion column of `DDS-draft-discussion.md` DDS-FR-KTVW — that corner falls over the second column, and holds its place there while the column is shown, hidden, and resized. No strip is held open below the page then, the page running the full height of its own column. Where the tab holds a **bar of its own at the foot of the column** — the review bar of `DCR-draft-change-review.md` DCR-FR-11 — that bar runs to the column's foot and no strip is held open below it either, the bar being the chrome the foot ends in. The control keeps its corner and stands **over** whatever chrome that corner falls on, which it may do because the rule that it covers no part of a page is about the author's own text and neither a bar nor a composer carries any. That chrome holds its trailing end clear of the control by the measure the strip uses — the review bar and the discussion column's composer alike — so no control the author must reach is ever under it: a control the author cannot reach is worse than a band of empty field, and the review bar's trailing end is where its whole-proposal actions stand.
10. **ACT-FR-10** Where the tab fills itself with its content instead — a Flow's canvas, a Diff tab's source panes — the control floats over that content in the tab's bottom-trailing corner, leading of whatever controls the host already keeps there, which hold the trailing end of that line. It covers no control of the host's and takes no activation meant for one.
11. **ACT-FR-11** The control, the column it expands into, the composer, and the discussion panel are surfaces anchored within the tab rather than floating overlays of the main window, on the same footing as the band above an Editor's editing surface (per `EFR-editor-find-replace.md` EFR-FR-AVML): opening any of them closes none of the window's mutually-exclusive overlays and none of them closes any of these (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-56).
12. **ACT-FR-12** Within a tab, at most one of the control's expansion, the composer, and the discussion panel is open at any moment, and they share that rule with the transient surfaces the comment rail owns (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-32) and with the add menu a Flow tab's canvas opens (per `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-07): opening any one dismisses whichever was open, without invoking an operation. Expanding the control therefore dismisses the composer, opening the composer collapses the control, opening the discussion panel does both, a card's overflow menu dismisses any of them, and opening a Flow's add menu collapses the control as expanding the control closes that menu. The expansion and the composer are dismissed alike by a pointer-down outside them (ACT-FR-03, ACT-FR-13); the discussion panel is not, being a conversation held open to be read rather than a surface chosen from and left (ACT-FR-20). The mention picker the composer opens within itself is not one of these surfaces but a transient of the composer that owns it (per `AGT-agents.md` AGT-FR-30): opening it dismisses nothing and closes nothing, least of all the composer it is completing a nickname in. A conversation tab is not among these surfaces either, being a tab rather than a surface of this tab: opening one dismisses none of them, and none of them closes one.
13. **ACT-FR-13** The **Discuss** action opens a **floating composer**: the shared `DiscussionComposer` in a small floating surface free of the control's own corner rather than anchored to it — horizontally centred in the window and sitting in its **lower quarter**, clear of a page's own content above it, at the measure the shared composer holds in every owner surface. It is the same composer every discussion uses — the same field geometry, the Attach affordance leading and the Post action trailing on the message's own line, the same pending-attachment strip, the same mention picker, and the same one-to-five-line growth — so an author who has written in any discussion already knows this surface. It carries no header of its own: what is being discussed is named in the placeholder text and in the surface's own accessible name rather than in a title bar. Focus lands in the message field the instant the composer opens while it is enabled; while it is disabled (ACT-FR-17), focus lands on the composer surface itself or on its stated reason. It is closed rather than always present, because a text field that is bulky and permanent takes room from what the tab is for every moment it is not being used. It carries no close control of its own, and is dismissed by **Escape**, by a **pointer-down anywhere outside it**, by activating Discuss again, or by another of the tab's transient surfaces taking its place (ACT-FR-12): it is one small surface lying over the window and it is left the same way the expanded control is left (ACT-FR-03). Outside is measured against the composer and not against the tab. A press inside the composer dismisses nothing, the mention picker and the attach menu it opens within itself being parts of it rather than places outside it. One press is ignored: while a post is in flight the composer is held until it lands, because a refusal renders in the composer it was refused in and keeps the body and the strip so the message is not retyped (ACT-FR-17). Escape returns focus to the control the composer was opened from; a pointer-down does not, the press having itself placed focus where the author chose. The space around its field is the surface's own padding and no more. Where the item already carries a discussion, **Discuss** still opens this composer, and the post opens a further whole-target discussion of the item (ACT-FR-LXKD). This composer is a **pre-creation** surface: it carries no discussion id, and opening or closing it creates no discussion, persists no comment, and dispatches no agent turn.
14. **ACT-FR-14** The composer's typed body and pending attachments are held in the session store of the shared composer, keyed by the item's discussion target key. They survive the composer closing for any reason short of a successful post — Escape, a press outside, activating Discuss again, and another of the tab's transient surfaces taking its place (ACT-FR-12) — and they survive the tab closing and the item being opened again, so reopening **Discuss** restores what was typed. A successful post clears them. They are not persisted beyond the session. They are part of no file, contribute no match to any find panel (per `EFR-editor-find-replace.md` EFR-FR-DDUX), occupy no position in any undo history (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-23), and mark nothing dirty (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-04).
15. **ACT-FR-15** The composer offers the project's enrolled agents through the mention picker of `AGT-agents.md` (AGT-FR-25, AGT-FR-27), on the terms a comment composer offers them (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-VQFJ), the `@all` handle among them leading the picker as it does anywhere (per `AGT-agents.md` AGT-FR-39), so an agent is addressed by typing its nickname into the message — or every one of them by typing that handle — rather than through a control of its own. It carries an **Attach** control and a pending strip on exactly the terms the rail's composers carry them (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-45, CMT-FR-46), a dropped file and pasted image data attaching without the control being opened.
16. **ACT-FR-16** Posting the composer is a **single-flight** operation: Post disables itself the instant it is activated and every further click or accelerator submission is ignored until the request settles, so one press is one attempt however impatiently it is repeated. It invokes `"open discussion (target, fragment, body, attachments)"` with the item as the target, no fragment, the typed body, and the strip's entries, and then invokes `"dispatch agent turn"` once per distinct agent the message's tags resolve to — a single `@all` standing for every enrolled agent that can answer (per `AGT-agents.md` AGT-FR-36), and one turn going to an agent named twice over — each naming the returned discussion and its opening comment as what addressed it (per `../core/AGC-agent-conversations.md` AGC-FR-05). The message stands whether or not any agent answers it, and a message addressing nobody opens a discussion all the same — one whose only tag is an `@all` the project has nobody to answer (per `AGT-agents.md` AGT-FR-38) included — because an item is a thing to think aloud about before it is a thing to ask about. On success the composer is gone rather than emptied and left in the tab, and the discussion is revealed through the single reveal route (per `CVP-conversation-presentation.md`): the tab's owning discussion surface — the rail's Discussion section where this tab lends the rail a margin (ACT-FR-19), or the floating discussion panel and its discussions control otherwise (ACT-FR-20) — shows the new discussion in the shared surface in the position it takes in the order of ACT-FR-21, and focus lands in its reply composer. Every later message in that discussion is posted from that surface rather than here; this composer only ever begins one, and it is never reused for a reply.
17. **ACT-FR-17** The composer is enabled while a comment author identity resolves, the local participant **Me** included, and shows no picker, no missing-token error, and no route to Global settings for it. It is disabled while the identity is refused, stating the same reason and offering the same route the rail's composers state and offer (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-24, CMT-FR-25, CMT-FR-26), because a discussion is a conversation of comments and cannot be attributed to nobody. A post the backend refuses renders its typed error inline in the composer, leaves the body and every entry of the strip intact (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-34, CMT-FR-51), and re-enables Post so the single-flight of ACT-FR-16 covers exactly one attempt at a time rather than blocking every attempt after the first — so a refused attachment is removed and the message posted without being retyped, and nothing was registered against the item in the meantime.
18. **ACT-FR-RVCU** The opening composer posts a project that stores no token as **Me**, through `"open discussion (target, fragment target, body, attachments)"` or `"get or create note discussion (note, body, attachments)"`, and the message it posts displays under the label rule of `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-ZCAE. Selecting a token that resolves for the project changes the label without a new post.
18. **ACT-FR-19** Where the tab lends the comment rail a margin beside a page — the Editor tab wherever that rail renders (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-02): over a Markdown file in WYSIWYG mode, and over a source file on the one surface it has (per `ESH-editor-source-files.md` ESH-FR-SSDV) — the item's discussions are read there, in the Discussion section pinned at the rail's head (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-53). Such a tab carries no discussions control and no floating panel.
19. **ACT-FR-QWNP** On a **New Artifact** tab the control's **Discuss** opens no composer of its own. Where the draft's discussion column is hidden it shows that column (per `DDS-draft-discussion.md` DDS-FR-XQMF) and moves focus to the column's opening composer or reply composer. Where the column already stands, it is disabled.
    - *Why:* The conversation is already a column of this tab, so the action is how the author reaches it rather than how they begin one.
20. **ACT-FR-20** Where it does not — the Editor tab over a Markdown file in raw-Markdown mode, the Flow tab, the Diff tab, and the History detail tab — the discussions are read in a **floating discussion panel** that renders the shared surface, shown and hidden by a **discussions control** sitting immediately leading of the action control and carrying the item's unresolved-discussion count. The control renders only while the item carries at least one discussion, so an item nobody has discussed carries one mark of chrome rather than two, and it appears the moment the first is opened. The panel is otherwise closed, because a conversation read alongside a graph or a diff is a thing to open rather than a column those surfaces give up width for permanently.
21. **ACT-FR-21** The panel holds the item's discussions, each as the shared `DiscussionSurface` (per `CMT-comments.md`): unresolved ones in one column in the order they were opened, and the resolved ones behind the disclosure that closes the panel, on the terms CMT-FR-17 sets for any discussion. It is bounded in width and height to a share of the tab and scrolls within itself, so a long conversation never takes the tab from the thing it is about. It is dismissed by its own close control, by Escape, and by another of the tab's transient surfaces opening (ACT-FR-12).
22. **ACT-FR-22** The panel is served by `"list discussions for target"`, invoked once when the tab opens, and follows the `"discussion changed"` event thereafter on exactly the terms the rail follows it (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-04, CMT-FR-54), so a discussion redraws from the payload of the write that changed it and the tab re-reads for none of it. The unresolved count on the discussions control comes from that same set, so it is current whether or not the panel is open.
23. **ACT-FR-23** Every item this control is bound to **affords** Discuss, an empty file included (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-34). The action is enabled wherever it can be taken, and each state that stops it — the item gone (ACT-FR-07), a modal over the tab, a conversation already standing in a column of its own (ACT-FR-QWNP) — disables it with its own reason (ACT-FR-06).
    - *Why:* An artifact, a plain text file, a Flow, a draft, and whatever a Diff or a History detail tab shows all have material to discuss.
24. **ACT-FR-24** Nothing the control opens is part of what the tab shows. Neither the composer nor the panel contributes a match to a find panel (per `EFR-editor-find-replace.md` EFR-FR-DDUX), occupies a position in an undo history (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-23), marks anything dirty (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-04), or changes a byte of the item; and no action taken in either alters a Flow's document (per `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-26) or anything a Diff tab is comparing.
25. **ACT-FR-25** Whether the panel is open belongs to the tab and is discarded with it. What the discussions themselves hold does not: those are discussions, persisted by `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md`, and outlive every tab that showed them. The scroll position, unread state, pending agent placeholder, failed contributions, question-set answers, and reply text of each discussion are held in session stores keyed by discussion id (per `CVP-conversation-presentation.md`), so they survive the panel closing, the tab closing, and the discussion being shown in another owner surface.
27. **ACT-FR-LXKD** Every discussion this control starts is a **whole-target** discussion: its target is the item (ACT-FR-02) and it carries **no fragment**. A Flow, a Diff, a History detail, an artifact, and a draft each open the same kind of discussion by the same gesture, and the Flow, Diff, and History tabs open it on the artifact they show. The control offers **Discuss** alone for this purpose. Starting a discussion about a **passage** is **Comment**, offered on a selection of a text artifact in the Editor and of a draft prompt in the New Artifact tab (per `CMT-comments.md`, `DDS-draft-discussion.md` DDS-FR-QMBC); it carries a fragment and uses the same opening composer.
    - *Why:* One record, one composer, and two entry points give an author a whole-item conversation or a passage conversation without learning two surfaces.
28. **ACT-FR-RWHV** The opening composer and the discussion surface this control opens are the shared `DiscussionComposer` and `DiscussionSurface` that the Editor rail, the draft discussion column, the Flow, Diff, and History panels, and the conversation tab all render (per `CVP-conversation-presentation.md`). A discussion looks and behaves the same in every owner surface: the same message list, Quote, attachments, agent pending, failed, and Retry states, question-set block, Lock, Resolve, unread divider, and auto-follow when the list is at its end. No behaviour of a discussion is specific to this control.
28. **ACT-FR-28** With the opening composer's message field focused, **Ctrl+Enter** on Windows and Linux and **Cmd+Enter** on macOS posts it and prevents the newline the key would otherwise insert, on exactly the terms the shared composer sets for every discussion (per `CVP-conversation-presentation.md`), the same in every owner surface: it acts only on a body that is not empty and not whitespace alone, does nothing while the composer is empty, while a post is already in flight (ACT-FR-16), while it is disabled for want of a resolved identity (ACT-FR-17), or while an IME composition is in progress, and it leaves the mention picker's own key handling untouched, so Enter completes a nickname where the picker is open and the accelerator posts where it is not. The same accelerator posts the Comment composer (ACT-FR-LXKD). A post the accelerator makes that the backend refuses behaves exactly as one refused through Post (ACT-FR-17).

## Non-functional requirements
- The collapsed control covers no part of a page at any window size (ACT-FR-09). What it opens does: at the sizes the shell supports there is not field enough beside a page for a column of labelled entries or a panel, so both lie over the content's trailing edge; the composer lies over the window's own middle instead, wherever the control that opened it sits. Each is opaque against what it covers, so no two sets of text read through one another, and while any is open a page can be scrolled past its own last line so whatever it covers comes out from under it. None of them resizes the content — an author reaching for an action never has the document reflow under them, and never has a Flow's canvas re-lay itself.
- The collapsed control together with the discussions control is the whole of a tab's action chrome beyond what the host already carries. A tab being read in shows no button whose action is not being taken, which is what keeps its attention on what it shows.
- No action of this control contacts a model on its own: none is reached when a tab mounts, when the control is expanded, when the composer is opened or typed into, or when the panel is opened or scrolled. The only call a discussion ever costs is a turn for an agent a message actually addressed, so an item nobody has asked an agent about costs none at all.
- Posting a discussion never waits on an agent: the message is appended and rendered as soon as the discussion is opened, and the turns it dispatched land in that card afterwards at whatever pace the models answer.
- The control's presence costs a tab no read: the action set is derived from the item's type, which the tab already holds, and no operation is invoked to decide what to render. Only a tab that reads its discussions in the panel invokes anything on mount, and that is the one list of ACT-FR-22.
- The control and everything it opens stay usable and legible at the minimum window size the shell supports (≥1280×800), the panel bounded so the content it floats over keeps the greater part of the tab.
