# Comments

**Spec code:** `CMT`

## Intent
The margin surface of the Editor for **fragment-targeted discussions**: the conversations about ranges of the open artifact's text, rendered in the empty margin the page already leaves beside it. An author selects a passage, starts a discussion about it, and the discussion's card sits in that margin vertically aligned with the first line of what it is about and travelling with it as the body scrolls, so a reader sees at a glance which paragraph is under discussion and how much of the artifact is settled. An artifact carries as many discussions as its author needs; each is a conversation whose comments are written and rendered as Markdown, each can be **locked** so it takes no further comments and **resolved** when the matter it raised is closed, and each comment can quote an earlier message in its own discussion so a long exchange stays legible. A comment carries pictures as well as prose — a screenshot dropped onto the composer, or a link to one already hosted — because the fastest way to say what is wrong with a passage is often to show it. Comments are attributed to the GitHub account the project's credential authenticates as, and discussions persist in the project itself as an append-only log (`../core/CMS-comments-storage.md`), so a review survives a relaunch and travels with the repository to everyone who checks it out. Not every participant is a person: an agent the project has enrolled answers in the discussion it was addressed in, with a comment of its own, so a conversation with a collaborator who is not human is read, quoted, locked, and resolved exactly like one with a colleague. How such a collaborator is summoned, who a message reaches, and what its card shows while an answer is being composed are `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md`'s; what this surface owns is the discussion that holds the exchange. The rail additionally holds the **whole-target discussions** of the open item — conversations about the whole of the artifact, or over a draft in a New Artifact tab about the whole of the draft, rather than about a passage of it — pinned at the head of the margin and begun from that tab's Discuss composer, and read, answered, and routed on exactly the terms a fragment-targeted discussion is. Every card renders the shared discussion surface and composer of `CVP-conversation-presentation.md`, so a conversation looks and behaves the same here as in every other place it is shown; no card offers to move its discussion into another presentation mode, because there are none. Out of scope: an agent's part in a conversation — the mention that summons one, the rules that decide who a comment reaches, the pending and failed contributions, and the notice an omitted image raises — all of which are `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md`'s; where a discussion is revealed from outside the rail, which `CVP-conversation-presentation.md` decides; editing or deleting a posted comment, since a discussion that has outlived its usefulness is resolved rather than rewritten; addressing a comment to a particular **person**, an agent alone being addressed by a tag and routed by one; commenting from raw-text mode or against a historical revision; seeing a comment another process appends arrive without reopening the artifact, nothing here watching the logs; listing the discussions of any artifact but the open one, since these cards serve the artifact in front of the author and the project-wide list is the Comments panel's surface (`CMP-comments-panel.md`); and GitHub's own pull-request review comments, which the Git panel renders (per `GIT-git.md` GIT-FR-05) and which have nothing to do with these.

## User stories
- As a reviewer, I want to select a paragraph and say what is wrong with it, so my objection sits next to the thing it is about rather than in a separate document.
- As an author reading a review, I want each discussion lined up with the passage it concerns, so I can work down the artifact answering objections in order.
- As a participant in a long discussion, I want to quote the specific line I am answering, so a reply three messages later is still unambiguous.
- As an author, I want to mark a discussion resolved and have it leave the rail, so the discussions still in front of me are the ones that still need me.
- As an author closing out a review, I want to lock a discussion so nothing further is added to a decision already made.

- Layout notes: the cards occupy the empty margin the centred page already leaves beside the artifact — no column of their own and no rule between them and the text, so the page keeps its width whether or not they are showing, and its position too wherever the margin fits beside it. On a tab too narrow for that the page slides toward the leading edge to widen the margin, and on one narrower still the cards leave the margin for a column below the page's foot rather than covering it. Each card's top edge aligns with the first rendered line of its fragment and moves with the body as it scrolls; a card that would overlap the one above is pushed down so cards never cover each other and always appear in document order. Fragment ranges are highlighted in the rich body, each with an underline as well as a background, and the focused discussion's range is highlighted distinctly. The orphaned section and the resolved disclosure are pinned to the foot of the margin, orphaned first, and stay put while the aligned cards scroll; over a draft in a New Artifact tab a Discussion section pins to the head of the margin on the same terms, above the first aligned card. The unresolved-discussion count rides in the tab's action cluster.

- Layout notes: the card's header carries the discussion's opener, the discussion's relative last-activity time, and the overflow button, with the fragment's quoted text on a second line truncated to one line. Comments are stacked oldest first, each with its author and relative time on one line above its rendered body; an agent's comment is distinguished from a human's in that line, and one whose participant carries a title takes a second line for it directly beneath, set quieter than the name and above the body. A comment whose participant carries no title, or an empty one, spends no line on it and leaves nothing in its place. A comment's attachments sit below its body, an image as a thumbnail bounded to the card's measure and anything else as a named chip. A pending contribution takes the shape of a comment and reads one short line of activity status — **Thinking…** while the agent waits on its model, and the status of the tool it is using while a tool call is active — its cancel control on its author line where a delivered comment carries none. A failed contribution takes the same shape and the same position, distinguished from both a delivered comment and a pending one by its own marker and by the wording of its body, and carries a **Retry** control on its own line below that body — the one control in a card that is lettered rather than a glyph, because it is an offer the author has to recognise rather than an action they already know is there. The composer states who an untagged reply reaches in its **placeholder** rather than on a line of its own: an empty field reads `Reply to @arch, @sec…` where the conversation has active agents and `Reply…` where it has none, a list too long for the field clipped with an ellipsis at its trailing edge rather than wrapped, and nothing above, beside, or below the field states a recipient anywhere. The composer's pending attachments occupy a strip between the text area and the control row, each removable in place. A body is typeset at the card's own UI type scale rather than at the document scale of the page beside it — a card is a narrow box in the margin, and it is the scale `CMP-comments-panel.md` sets a comment at too, so a comment is the same size whether it is read in the rail or in the panel. A quoted message renders as an attributed block above the body of the comment that quotes it. Each comment carries a Quote control on its header line. The composer is pinned to the card's bottom with Cancel leading and Post trailing; both are glyphs rather than words, as is Quote, so a narrow card spends its width on the conversation. The composer is absent entirely on a locked discussion. Errors returned by a backend operation attach inline at the bottom of the card that triggered them.

## UI contract boundary
- **Owned by the UI**: the margin the cards occupy inside the Editor tab, their layout within it, the division of its height between the sections pinned at its two ends, and the arrangement the cards take on a tab too narrow to hold that margin beside the page; the vertical alignment of each card to the first rendered line of its fragment and the downward stacking that keeps cards from overlapping; the selection affordance that starts a discussion and the draft card it opens; the mapping of a rich-body selection onto a character range in the artifact's Markdown source; the tracking of every fragment's offsets against the in-memory buffer as the author edits; the re-anchor search performed when an artifact loads and the marking of a discussion as orphaned when it fails; the rendering of comment bodies from Markdown to rich text and the plain-Markdown composer that authors them; the title line an agent comment carries below its author line and the absence of one where its participant carries no title (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-KFUF, CTA-FR-KYPK); the Quote action and the seeding of a composer from it; the card's overflow menu and its lock and resolve entries; the orphaned section, the resolved disclosure, the Discussion section, and which discussions each holds; the derivation of a conversation's active agents from its own human comments and the project's current roster, the recipients an empty composer names in its placeholder, and the dispatch rules that follow from both; relative-timestamp formatting; the unresolved-discussion count in the tab's action cluster; the attach affordance, the pending attachments a composer holds and their removal before posting, the encoding of a chosen file into the payload an append carries, and the rendering of a posted comment's attachments as thumbnails, chips, and — for a proposal — a control (CMT-FR-48); the dispatching of a turn for each agent a posted comment addresses, the pending contribution an outstanding turn renders in its card together with the activity status it reads, the fixed wording of every status and the fallback one, the rule that picks which of a turn's active tool calls is spoken for, and the control that cancels one; the failed contribution a recoverable terminal failure renders in that same position, its wording, its distinction from a delivered comment and from a pending one, the labelled **Retry** control it carries, and the withholding of that control on a locked discussion (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-XZUO); and the rail's open/closed state, which is part of the artifact's retained edit state (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-28) and so is held only in memory. The shared discussion surface and composer a card renders, the unread indicator, and the composer accelerator that posts without the pointer are owned by `CVP-conversation-presentation.md` and introduce no operation here.
- **Delegated to backend (abstract)**: `"list discussions for target"`, `"open discussion (target, fragment target, body, attachments)"`, `"add comment to discussion (discussion, body, quotes, attachments)"`, `"set discussion lock"`, `"set discussion resolution"`, `"reanchor discussion fragment"`, `"read comment attachment"`, and `"resolve comment author identity"` — all owned by `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md`, which also owns the discussion and comment records, the participant identity stamped into every comment, the storage of an attachment's bytes, and the append-only log they persist to. The rail follows that module's `"discussion changed"` event and redraws from its payload rather than re-reading (CMT-FR-04). The same operation names serve a draft's files for a draft owner (CMS-FR-36), so the rail invokes one set of names and the module resolves where the discussions live. Opening a discussion is `"open discussion (target, fragment target, body, attachments)"`, invoked from the tab's action control rather than from here (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-16); every later comment in one is posted through the same operations any discussion's are. The UI never composes an author identity of its own and never writes a log line. When `"resolve comment author identity"` reports that a token must be selected, the token picker modal of `GHA-github-authentication.md` (GHA-FR-16) is what resolves it, and when it reports that none is stored the rail renders that typed error with the route of GHA-FR-19; neither introduces a command here. The agent-turn operations the rail invokes, the `"agent turn state changed"` event it follows, and the reading of `"list project agents"` a tag resolves against belong to `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md`, which declares them. The mention picker those agents are offered through, and the tag syntax that addresses one, are owned by `AGT-agents.md` and introduce no operation here. The state a `proposal` attachment's control renders is read through `"list draft change proposals"` and kept current by the `"draft change proposals changed"` event, both owned by `../core/DCP-draft-change-proposals.md`; the state a `prompt_proposal` attachment's control renders is read through `"list prompt change proposals"` and kept current by the `"prompt change proposals changed"` event, both owned by `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md`. The modals those controls open, and every operation that reads or decides a proposal, are `DCR-draft-change-review.md`'s and `PCR-prompt-change-review.md`'s and introduce nothing here. A discussion's pending question set is read through `"read discussion question set"`, kept current by the `"discussion question set changed"` event, and answered through `"submit discussion question answers"`, all owned by `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` and all invoked as `DQA-discussion-question-answering.md` declares (DQA-FR-JWEF, DQA-FR-KDVU); the block that renders them introduces nothing here.

## Functional requirements
1. **CMT-FR-01** Comments occupy the empty margin the Editor tab already leaves to the trailing side of the artifact's page (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-61). That margin is field rather than page (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-63), so a card sits beside the document rather than on it. They take no column of their own and are separated from the page by no rule or panel of their own — the page's own edge is what separates them from it — so the artifact keeps its width whether or not comments are showing, and its position too on any tab leaving field enough for them (CMT-FR-64). That region — named the **rail** throughout this spec — is part of the tab, not a floating overlay of the main window: showing it closes none of the mutually-exclusive overlays and none of them closes it (per `STB-status-bar.md` STB-FR-12).
2. **CMT-FR-02** The rail renders wherever the Editor sets a file's text on its page: the **WYSIWYG** surface of a Markdown file, and the single source surface of a source file (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-63, per `ESH-editor-source-files.md` ESH-FR-SSDV). A Markdown file toggled to **raw-text** mode (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-17) hides the rail without touching a single discussion, and toggling back renders the same discussions against the same fragments.
3. **CMT-FR-03** The rail is available on every artifact the Editor opens, on a plain text file whichever of the two surfaces it is edited on (per `ESH-editor-source-files.md` ESH-FR-ATDS, ESH-FR-BLTT), and on every file of a draft in a New Artifact tab (CMT-FR-37), each discussion's fragment targeting the file's source rather than anything an artifact type or a project location supplies.
4. **CMT-FR-04** On opening an artifact the Editor invokes `"list discussions for target"` once with the artifact's target and renders what it returns: the fragment-targeted discussions as aligned cards, and the whole-target ones in the Discussion section (CMT-FR-53). Thereafter the rail follows the `"discussion changed"` event and redraws the discussion the payload names from the payload itself — a comment posted in this card, one posted from another tab or from a second window, a lock or resolution set anywhere, and an agent's answer arriving alike (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-QTNB) — so it neither re-reads nor polls for any of them. A discussion a different process appends to becomes visible when the artifact is next opened, nothing watching the logs (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-31).
5. **CMT-FR-05** Selecting a non-empty range of text in the surface the rail renders beside (CMT-FR-02) — a rich body, or a source file's source surface — reveals a **Comment** affordance at the end of the selection: a single graphical control beside the passage rather than a filled block over it. Activating it opens a **draft card** in the margin, aligned to the selection like any other card, with focus in its composer, and shows the comments if they were hidden. A selection of zero length reveals nothing.
6. **CMT-FR-06** A discussion's **fragment** is a character range in the artifact's **source** — the Markdown of a Markdown file, the file's own text otherwise — together with the exact source substring that range covers. A selection made on a source surface is already such a range, and the UI maps a rich-body selection onto one, so a fragment means the same passage on whichever surface the file is edited on. A selection lying within one block is found in the source as it stands; a selection spanning blocks is located by its two ends — the first and last rendered lines it covers, the last searched for after the first — and the range between them is taken from the source with the intervening Markdown syntax the rich surface hides included, so an ordinary selection across a heading and the text under it fragments like any other. A selection neither route can place — including one whose last line lies nowhere after its first — opens no discussion.
   - *Why:* Targeting a smaller or a different passage than the author chose is worse than not targeting a fragment at all.
7. **CMT-FR-07** Posting the draft card's first comment invokes `"open discussion (target, fragment target, body, attachments)"` with the artifact's target and the selection's fragment target, and replaces the draft with the returned discussion's card. Cancelling the draft card discards it, invokes nothing, and leaves no discussion behind.
8. **CMT-FR-08** A discussion card stacks its comments oldest first, each showing its author, a compact relative timestamp, and its body rendered as rich text. Hovering a timestamp discloses the comment's absolute instant.
9. **CMT-FR-09** A comment is authored as **Markdown source** in a plain composer and rendered as rich text once posted, so emphasis, code spans, lists, and links in a comment read as themselves in the card.
10. **CMT-FR-10** A comment's author renders as a human or as an agent distinguishably, from the participant the backend stamped (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-11); the UI never derives an author from anything it holds locally.
11. **CMT-FR-11** A card that is neither locked nor resolved carries a composer at its foot. Posting invokes `"add comment to discussion (discussion, body, quotes, attachments)"` and appends the returned comment to the card. Cancelling closes the composer and discards its content without invoking anything.
12. **CMT-FR-12** Every comment in a card carrying a composer (CMT-FR-11) carries a **Quote** action. Activating it seeds the card's composer with an attributed quote of that comment: the part of it the reader has selected, or the whole message when they have selected none of it. A selection lying outside that comment — elsewhere in the artifact, or in another message of the same discussion — is not what is being quoted and is disregarded in favour of the whole message. A composer may carry several quotes, of several different comments in the same discussion, and each may be removed before posting.
13. **CMT-FR-13** A quote is carried structurally in the `quotes` of the posted comment rather than only as text in its body, and renders in the card as an attributed block above the quoting comment's own body, naming the quoted comment's author. Activating it brings the quoted comment into view within the card.
14. **CMT-FR-14** Comments are immutable once posted. No card, menu, or composer anywhere in the rail offers to edit or delete a comment or a discussion; a discussion that has outlived its usefulness is resolved (CMT-FR-16).
15. **CMT-FR-15** The card's overflow menu carries exactly two entries: a lock entry and a resolve entry. The lock entry reads **Lock discussion** on an unlocked discussion and **Unlock discussion** on a locked one, and invokes `"set discussion lock"`. A locked card renders a lock marker and no composer, and its comments stay fully readable.
16. **CMT-FR-16** The resolve entry reads **Mark resolved** on an unresolved discussion and **Reopen discussion** on a resolved one, and invokes `"set discussion resolution"`. A resolved card carries no composer and no Quote action, on the same terms a locked one carries neither (CMT-FR-11, CMT-FR-12, CMT-FR-15), and its comments stay fully readable: a conversation the author has settled is not one to add to, and offering a composer inside the resolved disclosure invites a reply nobody is coming back to read. Reopening it is the one thing its menu still offers that changes that, and it restores both. Withholding them is the rail's own decision rather than a refusal the backend makes — the storage module refuses an append to a locked discussion alone (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-17) — so a turn dispatched before the discussion was settled still delivers its answer into it (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-QTNB) and a resolution set in another window costs no author the comment they were writing. Lock and resolve remain independent as settings: either can be set or cleared on a discussion in any state of the other (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-18).
17. **CMT-FR-17** A resolved discussion leaves the aligned cards and is counted in a **resolved disclosure** pinned at the rail's foot. Expanding it renders the resolved discussions' cards in full; reopening one returns it to its aligned position. The disclosure is collapsed by default and its state is per artifact.
18. **CMT-FR-18** When an artifact loads, each stored fragment is resolved against the loaded Markdown source by searching for the fragment's stored quote nearest to its stored offsets. A discussion whose quote is found is placed at the found range; a discussion whose quote is not found anywhere is **orphaned**.
19. **CMT-FR-19** An orphaned discussion renders in an **orphaned section** pinned at the rail's foot, showing the quote it was targeted at in place of an alignment. It is fully readable, still carries its overflow menu, and still accepts comments while unlocked. It is never hidden and never discarded.
20. **CMT-FR-20** While the artifact is edited, every fragment's offsets track the buffer: text inserted or removed before a fragment moves it, and an edit that destroys the targeted text orphans that discussion for the rest of the session. Fragment tracking is not an edit — it marks the artifact dirty no more than reading it does and occupies no position in the undo history (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-23).
21. **CMT-FR-21** When the artifact is saved and a discussion's resolved fragment differs from the one the backend holds, the rail invokes `"reanchor discussion fragment"` with the current range and quote. A discussion whose fragment is unchanged is not reanchored, and no reanchor is invoked for an orphaned discussion.
22. **CMT-FR-22** Choosing **Load from filesystem** on the external-change modal (per `EXC-editor-external-change.md` EXC-FR-WDAV) re-resolves every fragment against the newly loaded content on the terms of CMT-FR-18, so a discussion whose passage the other writer removed becomes orphaned rather than pointing at unrelated text.
23. **CMT-FR-23** A discussion whose fragment lies inside the WYSIWYG frontmatter region while that region is collapsed expands the region as a peek when its card is focused, on the same principle as an undo that reaches into it (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-25).
24. **CMT-FR-24** The rail invokes `"resolve comment author identity"` when it first renders for a project. While no identity resolves, the Comment affordance (CMT-FR-05) and every composer are disabled and the reason is stated in the rail, because a comment cannot be attributed to nobody.
25. **CMT-FR-25** An identity refused because a token must be selected opens the token picker modal (per `GHA-github-authentication.md` GHA-FR-16); confirming it re-resolves the identity and enables commenting, and cancelling leaves the rail disabled with the reason stated.
26. **CMT-FR-26** An identity refused because no token is stored at all opens no picker: the rail renders that typed error inline with a route to the Global settings GitHub section (per `GHA-github-authentication.md` GHA-FR-19). An identity refused because GitHub is unreachable or the keychain is unavailable is rendered as its own distinct reason, because the three call for different responses from the author.
27. **CMT-FR-27** Each card's top edge aligns with the first rendered line of its fragment, and the cards travel with the body as it scrolls, so a card stays level with the passage it is about. A card that would overlap the card above it is pushed down far enough to clear it, so cards never cover one another and always appear in the same order as their fragments. Discussions with no line to align to stay put instead of scrolling with the body: the orphaned section and the resolved disclosure at the foot of the margin, and the Discussion section at its head (CMT-FR-62).
28. **CMT-FR-28** Every fragment-targeted discussion that is open shows its fragment in the owner's text surface, and the focused discussion's fragment is highlighted distinctly from the rest. A fragment highlight never relies on colour alone: it carries an underline in addition to its background, and an accessible label naming the discussion it belongs to, with a stronger underline and label state on the focused one. Activating a highlight focuses that discussion's card and scrolls it into view; focusing a card scrolls its fragment into view. A card takes focus on the completed click rather than on the press, because focusing it moves it: the fragment scrolls into view and the rail re-arranges around the newly focused card. Moving a card while a press on one of its own controls is still open would carry that control out from under the pointer, and the author's first press on it would be spent scrolling rather than on what they pressed.
29. **CMT-FR-29** The Editor tab's action cluster (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-16) carries a comments control showing the artifact's **unresolved** discussion count, orphaned discussions included. It toggles the rail open and closed, and it is present wherever the file is edited — both editing modes of a Markdown file included — so a switch to raw text never hides the fact that discussions exist.
30. **CMT-FR-30** The rail's open/closed state and the resolved disclosure's state belong to the artifact and are part of its retained edit state (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-28): they survive the tab closing and reopening within the session, are held only in memory, and are discarded with that state. The rail opens by default for an artifact carrying at least one unresolved discussion and stays closed for one carrying none.
31. **CMT-FR-31** Nothing the rail renders is part of the editing surface: its text contributes no match to the Find and Find & Replace panels (per `EFR-editor-find-replace.md` EFR-FR-DDUX), a replacement never rewrites it, and no action taken in it occupies a position in the artifact's undo history (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-23).
32. **CMT-FR-32** The rail's own transient surfaces — a card's overflow menu and the Comment affordance — are anchored within the tab and at most one is open at a time; opening one dismisses any other, and each dismisses on Escape or an outside click without invoking any operation.
33. **CMT-FR-33** While the external-change modal blocks the tab (per `EXC-editor-external-change.md` EXC-FR-VTUH), the rail is inert along with every other operation: no discussion is opened, no comment is posted, and no lock or resolution is set until the author resolves the modal.
34. **CMT-FR-34** A failed operation renders its typed error inline at the foot of the card that triggered it, leaving the composer's content intact so a post is retried without retyping. A comment refused because the discussion is locked (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-17) re-renders the card as locked.
35. **CMT-FR-35** A card's own controls — Post, Cancel, Quote, removing a pending quote — are graphical rather than lettered, because a card is narrow and a row of words costs the comment the width it needs. Each carries the name it would have spelled out, so it stays reachable and operable by that name without a pointer.
36. **CMT-FR-36** A discussion reached from the Comments panel (per `CMP-comments-panel.md` CMP-FR-10) is revealed through the one route `revealDiscussion` (per `CVP-conversation-presentation.md` CVP-FR-06). For a fragment-targeted discussion on an available artifact it lands on its own card: the artifact's Editor tab is put on the surface that renders the rail (CMT-FR-02) — WYSIWYG for a Markdown file, and for a source file the one surface it has — the rail is shown, and that discussion's card is focused with its fragment scrolled into view (CMT-FR-28), whatever mode the tab was in and whether or not its rail was open. A resolved discussion's card is reached by expanding the resolved disclosure (CMT-FR-17). The rail's open state for that artifact is thereafter open, on the same terms as any other change to it (CMT-FR-30). A discussion whose owner is unavailable is reached in the fallback conversation tab rather than here (per `CVP-conversation-presentation.md` CVP-FR-54).
37. **CMT-FR-37** The rail renders identically over a **draft's prompt** in a New Artifact tab (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-14), which lends it the same margin an Editor tab does. Every requirement above holds there unchanged — the selection affordance, the fragment targeting, the composers, the lock and resolve entries, the orphaned section, the resolved disclosure, the addressing of an agent, the extent of the margin and the division of its height (CMT-FR-62, CMT-FR-63), and the arrangement it falls back to on a narrow tab (CMT-FR-64) — because a draft's prompt is Markdown like any artifact, and a review of one should not have to be learned a second time. Four things differ, all owned elsewhere: the discussions are served for a draft owner rather than an artifact owner by the same operations (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-36); they are deleted with the draft rather than outliving it (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-20); a turn dispatched from there names the draft as its target, which is what gives the agent the draft's prompt as the material under discussion (per `../core/AGC-agent-conversations.md` AGC-FR-07); and the whole-target discussions the margin holds at its head are the draft's rather than the prompt's, so they stay put while a past version is being read in that tab and are the one part of the margin that renders there (CMT-FR-53, CMT-FR-54, per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-14). The floating action control the margin shares the field's trailing edge with is not among the differences — every tab carrying this rail carries one (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-01), and a card's overflow menu is one of the tab's transient surfaces alongside that control and its composer in each of them (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-12).
38. **CMT-FR-38** A card is weighted **below** the page it annotates. It carries a surface of its own, distinct from both the page and the field it sits on (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-63), bounded by a hairline without the page's elevation, so the document stays the most prominent thing in the tab and the discussions read as margin notes rather than as a second document competing with the first. A card's body sets in the **UI** role (per `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-13) at a measure narrower and a scale smaller than the page's prose, its author line and its timestamp recede further still, and its own surfaces — the composer, the pending quotes, the overflow menu — inherit that weight rather than reclaiming it. The treatment holds under either theme and changes nothing about fragment targeting (CMT-FR-18), alignment (CMT-FR-27), highlighting (CMT-FR-28), or the operations a card offers.
39. **CMT-FR-45** A composer carries an **Attach** control offering exactly two entries and no third: attaching a **file**, which opens the OS file picker and carries the chosen file's bytes, and attaching a **link**, which takes an address and carries it as a reference. Nothing in any composer attaches a `proposal`, which reaches a comment only by an agent proposing one (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-60) and is therefore never something the author is offered a way to compose. Dropping a file onto the composer and pasting image data into it both attach as a file without the control being opened, because the fastest way to show a passage's problem is to paste the screenshot already on the clipboard. A discussion carrying no composer — locked or resolved (CMT-FR-15, CMT-FR-16) — therefore has no way to attach anything.
40. **CMT-FR-46** Attachments chosen but not yet posted render as a **pending strip** between the composer's text area and its control row, each entry naming what it holds and carrying a control that removes it. A pending attachment is not sent anywhere until the comment is posted, so removing one invokes nothing, and cancelling the composer discards the strip with the typed body (CMT-FR-11).
41. **CMT-FR-47** Posting carries the strip's entries as the operation's `attachments`: a file as its media type, filename, and encoded bytes, and a link as its address and media type. A comment with an empty strip carries an empty list, which is the ordinary case.
42. **CMT-FR-48** A posted comment's attachments render below its body: an image as a thumbnail bounded to the card's own measure so it never widens the rail beyond the margin it has (CMT-FR-01), and anything else as a named chip carrying its filename and media type. Activating either opens it at full size beyond the card, because a thumbnail in a narrow margin is an index of the picture rather than the picture. A **reference** attachment renders as neither: it holds no content to show, so it takes the form of a **control** naming the prompt the change was proposed against, which reveals that change. What it reveals is decided by the attachment's own kind and by nothing else: a `proposal` (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-60) names a draft's prompt and scrolls that draft's document column to the first undecided change (per `DCR-draft-change-review.md` DCR-FR-HVXK); a `proposal_hunk` (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-TQVB) names **one** change of a draft proposal and scrolls that column to it; and a `prompt_proposal` (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-66) names a prompt artifact of the project and opens the review of `PCR-prompt-change-review.md` (PCR-FR-03), which opens or focuses that artifact's Editor tab first where it is not already open (PCR-FR-18). The two controls are rendered alike, name their file alike, and are activated alike; the rail neither derives the target from the path nor keeps a second treatment for one of them. It is the one attachment whose rendering is an action rather than a preview, because what it names is a decision the author has to make rather than a picture they have to see.
43. **CMT-FR-49** A stored attachment's content is fetched through `"read comment attachment"` and a linked one from its own address, in both cases only when the card holding it is rendered, so an artifact whose review carries many images costs nothing for the discussions the author is not looking at. Content that cannot be loaded — a link that does not resolve, a stored attachment the operation refuses — renders as a chip stating that it could not be loaded and naming what it points at, rather than as an empty frame. A `proposal` attachment fetches nothing at any point: its control renders from what the attachment itself carries, and the proposal's own text is read only where a review renders it (per `DCR-draft-change-review.md` DCR-FR-08), so a conversation carrying a proposal costs the rail exactly what one carrying a sentence costs.
44. **CMT-FR-50** An attachment is immutable with the comment that carries it, on the same terms as the comment's own body (CMT-FR-14): no card, chip, or menu anywhere in the rail offers to remove, replace, or re-order an attachment once its comment is posted.
45. **CMT-FR-51** An append refused for its attachments — a media type the backend does not accept, a file beyond its size bound, content it could not decode — renders its typed error inline at the foot of the card on the terms of CMT-FR-34, leaving the composer's body and every entry of its pending strip intact, so the offending attachment is removed and the comment posted without any of it being retyped.
46. **CMT-FR-52** A **discussion** is a conversation about an owner: a file of the project, a draft, or a note. It optionally carries a **fragment target**, a range of the owner's text it is about (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-53). A discussion with a fragment target is shown beside that fragment by the rail; a discussion without one is about the whole owner, and an artifact or draft owner shows it in the Discussion section. A conversation about the whole and a conversation about a line of it are the same kind of record, rendered by the same shared surface (CMT-FR-HQNV), in the same margin. A whole-target discussion is begun from the tab's action control (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-16) and read and continued here.
47. **CMT-FR-53** Whole-target discussions render in a **Discussion section** pinned at the **head** of the rail, above the aligned cards, in the order they were opened. Which discussions those are is the tab's item (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-02): the open artifact's in an Editor tab, and the draft's in a New Artifact tab, where the section renders whichever file of the draft is selected because a whole-target discussion is about the draft rather than about the file in front of the author. An item carrying no whole-target discussion renders no section and no header for one.
48. **CMT-FR-54** The rail invokes `"list discussions for target"` once when the tab opens, with the tab's owner target, and follows the `"discussion changed"` event thereafter on exactly the terms it follows it for a fragment-targeted discussion (CMT-FR-04), so a discussion redraws from the payload of the write that changed it and the rail re-reads for none of it. In a New Artifact tab, selecting another file of the draft shows the fragment-targeted discussions whose fragment lies in that file and leaves the whole-target discussions as they stand, they belonging to the draft rather than to the selection; the selection causes no further read.
49. **CMT-FR-55** A whole-target discussion's card is an ordinary discussion card in every respect that does not depend on a fragment: its comments stack oldest first, its composer posts through `"add comment to discussion (discussion, body, quotes, attachments)"`, its comments are quotable and immutable, it attaches files and links, it carries the same overflow menu with the same lock and resolve entries, its composer names its recipients in its placeholder, it renders pending contributions reading the same activity statuses, and it surfaces a failed operation inline (CMT-FR-08 through CMT-FR-16, CMT-FR-34, per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-ZOLW, CMT-FR-45 through CMT-FR-51, CTA-FR-IAKP, CTA-FR-FBJR, CTA-FR-IWOJ). It carries no fragment quote, highlights nothing in the body, aligns to nothing, is never orphaned (CMT-FR-18), and never moves its fragment (CMT-FR-21).
50. **CMT-FR-ZQKD** A discussion's card holding a **pending question set** renders that set as a block between its comments and its composer, and its composer, its Quote action, and every other route by which the author would add a comment to it are disabled while the set stands, on the terms `DQA-discussion-question-answering.md` sets (DQA-FR-NRZB, DQA-FR-PXNC, DQA-FR-VJHT). Reading, locking, and resolving are untouched.
51. **CMT-FR-56** A resolved discussion leaves the Discussion section for the resolved disclosure at the rail's foot and returns to the section when it is reopened, on the terms CMT-FR-17 sets for any discussion. An unresolved discussion is counted in the tab's unresolved-discussion total like any other (CMT-FR-29), because a conversation waiting on the author is waiting on them whether it names a passage or not.
52. **CMT-FR-61** The rail renders a Discussion section wherever it renders at all — over an artifact in an Editor tab and over a draft's prompt in a New Artifact tab alike (CMT-FR-37) — because the two tabs lend it the same margin and a conversation about the open thing is read in the same place as the remarks pinned inside it. The rail is not the only place a whole-target discussion is read: a tab that lends it no margin reads its discussions in a floating panel instead (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-20), which renders the same shared surface this spec uses and introduces no rendering of its own. What the rail never does anywhere is **begin** a whole-target discussion — it is opened from the tab's action control (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-13) and the rail's own Comment affordance opens a discussion targeted at a fragment and nothing else.
53. **CMT-FR-62** The Discussion section is pinned at the rail's head as the orphaned section and the resolved disclosure are pinned at its foot (CMT-FR-27): all three stay put while the aligned cards scroll with the body. The two ends divide the margin's height between them by what each has to show rather than by a share fixed in advance: each takes the height its own content needs while both fit, and where they do not, the one asking for more gives up its surplus first and scrolls within itself, neither ever reduced below its own header and the first card beneath it. A collapsed resolved disclosure therefore costs the discussions the one row it occupies, and an expanded one holding twenty discussions takes what twenty discussions need out of the height the discussions are not using rather than a fixed share either way. The margin runs the length of the page beside it with its foot level with the page's, in an Editor tab and in a New Artifact tab alike (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-09), so no height anywhere in it is held open for nothing.
54. **CMT-FR-63** The foot is bounded by what else the margin has to show and by nothing else. While the rail holds another discussion to render — a discussion pinned at its head, or an unresolved discussion aligned in the body — the foot takes no more than the margin less the height one card needs, so what is still open is never wholly buried behind what is already settled. While it holds none, the foot has the whole margin and an expanded disclosure fills it: an artifact whose every discussion is resolved has nothing else for that height to be kept for, and a settled review reading in the lower half of an empty margin spends the rest of it on nothing.
55. **CMT-FR-64** The cards render **beside** the page while the margin can hold one at the narrowest measure a card stays readable at, and in a column **below** the page when it cannot. The trailing field is what the margin has, and the tab gives up its leading field first — the page slides toward the leading edge rather than narrowing (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-63) — so the arrangement changes only once a card no longer fits with the page as far leading as it will go. Below the page the rail is a full-width section following the page's foot, bounded to a share of the tab and scrolling within itself, its cards in one column at the measure they hold in the margin and in the order of the passages they annotate, the discussions leading it and the orphaned section and the resolved disclosure closing it. It keeps a scroller of its own rather than being read by scrolling the document past its last line, because the discussions of a narrow window are still the thing the author is working through and a review that begins after the whole artifact is a review nobody reaches; the page keeps the greater part of the tab either way. A stacked card aligns to no line and travels with nothing, there being no margin to travel in (CMT-FR-27); everything else a card does it still does — its composer, its overflow menu, its quotes, the highlighting of its fragment in the body, and the activation that runs each way between a highlight and its card (CMT-FR-28). The rail covers no part of the page in either arrangement, because a comment read through the sentence it is about is worse than one read a scroll further down.
56. **CMT-FR-66** When an edit replaces the buffer with the WYSIWYG surface's serialisation of it (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-66, EDT-FR-67) and that serialisation settles a different spelling on some of the Markdown, every fragment is re-resolved against the new buffer as part of that same step, on the terms CMT-FR-18 resolves a fragment on load. A discussion whose passage survives the change in a different spelling keeps its fragment and carries the new range and quote, so no discussion is orphaned by a rewriting the author did not perform; a discussion whose passage the author's own edit destroyed orphans exactly as CMT-FR-20 has it. Re-resolution is not itself an edit — it marks the artifact dirty no more than the edit already did and occupies no position in the undo history (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-23) — and the fragments it settles reach the backend on the next save through CMT-FR-21.
57. **CMT-FR-67** A comment carrying a reference attachment of either kind is an ordinary comment of its discussion in every other respect (CMT-FR-08, CMT-FR-10, CMT-FR-12, CMT-FR-14): it stacks in order, names its agent author, is quotable, and is immutable, and the control it renders (CMT-FR-48) changes nothing about the conversation — activating it opens a modal and posts nothing. The control renders whatever the proposal has since become, because the proposal's pending, accepted, or rejected state lives in the module that owns it rather than in the attachment (per `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` CMS-FR-60, CMS-FR-66); the rail reads that state from `"list draft change proposals"` for a `proposal` and from `"list prompt change proposals"` for a `prompt_proposal` when the tab opens, and follows `"draft change proposals changed"` and `"prompt change proposals changed"` respectively thereafter, on the same terms it follows every other event it redraws from (CMT-FR-04). What a control says while that reading does not hold its proposal is `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-UKIG's, and the reading such a control asks for is CTA-FR-XVNC's.
59. **CMT-FR-70** Every composer the rail carries posts on **Ctrl+Enter** on Windows and Linux and **Cmd+Enter** on macOS with the composer focused, on exactly the terms `CVP-conversation-presentation.md` CVP-FR-40 sets — a non-whitespace body only, the newline prevented, nothing at all while the composer is empty, posting, disabled for want of an identity (CMT-FR-24), or mid-IME composition, and the mention picker's own key handling untouched (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-VQFJ). A refusal leaves the body and the pending strip intact and renders its typed error inline exactly as a refusal through the Post control does (CMT-FR-34, CMT-FR-51).
60. **CMT-FR-79** A posted comment renders the tags it was written with exactly as they were stored, routing having rewritten nothing (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-KREF), and the turns it dispatched render **one per agent**: each agent's own pending contribution while its turn runs (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-ZOLW), its own comment when that turn delivers (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-QTNB), and its own failed contribution where it failed recoverably (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-MGVJ). The outcomes are independent — one agent's failure neither suppresses another's answer nor produces a second turn for it (per `../core/AGC-agent-conversations.md` AGC-FR-04) — and **Retry** starts one turn for the one agent whose contribution carries it (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-QDDG), so an author watching a message they sent to three agents sees three answers arrive, or two answers and one offer to retry, and never one agent answering twice.
61. **CMT-FR-83** Every attachment affordance is **operable without a pointer**. In a composer, the **Attach** control, each entry of its menu, and each entry of the pending strip together with that entry's remove control are all reachable and activatable from the keyboard and are named for assistive technology by what they hold (CMT-FR-45, CMT-FR-46). On a posted comment, an image thumbnail is focusable, carries the attachment's own name as its accessible name, and opens at full size from the keyboard exactly as it opens by pointer, and a chip and a could-not-be-loaded state each read as what they are (CMT-FR-48, CMT-FR-49). This holds in every conversation the rail renders and is not conditioned on what the conversation is about, so a draft's discussion attaches, removes, focuses, and inspects an image on exactly the terms an artifact's review does (CMT-FR-37, CMT-FR-55) — the affordance is one affordance, and a picture pasted into a draft conversation is attached, rendered, and opened by the same controls everywhere.
62. **CMT-FR-AWIE** A comment body renders with every provider citation marker hidden, by the rule of `../ai/CVL-conversation-loop.md` CVL-FR-VSSU. The stored body is unchanged by it.
71. **CMT-FR-HQNV** Every card the rail renders — fragment-targeted, orphaned, whole-target, and resolved alike — is the shared discussion surface of `CVP-conversation-presentation.md` (CVP-FR-SDMQ) set at the card's measure, with its composer the shared composer. The rail adds only what depends on being a margin: the alignment of a card to its fragment (CMT-FR-27), the highlight of the fragment in the body (CMT-FR-28), and the orphaned section, the resolved disclosure, and the Discussion section. There is no separate comment-thread model: a card renders a discussion, and no card carries a control that moves its discussion into another presentation mode.
72. **CMT-FR-WZTE** Every card carries a text badge, **Fragment** or **Whole**, that names whether its discussion has a fragment target. The badge is a word and never a colour alone, so a card in the Discussion section and a card aligned to a line are told apart by reading.
73. **CMT-FR-RPLC** A discussion whose fragment cannot be restored — its quote is found nowhere (CMT-FR-18), or its owner file is unavailable — renders in the orphaned or unavailable state (CMT-FR-19) and is kept. It stays readable, answerable while unlocked, and listed in the Comments panel. It is never discarded and never converted to a whole-target discussion.

## Non-functional requirements
- A proposal control costs the rail one reading per target — one per draft, one per artifact — rather than one per control, and a control whose proposal has resolved asks for nothing further: the healing ask of `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-LOOE is bounded per reference and never a poll.
- Fragment tracking keeps pace with typing on a document of several thousand lines: the rail's alignment updates without perceptible input lag in the editing surface.
- The re-anchor search runs once per discussion when an artifact loads, not per keystroke, so a heavily-commented artifact opens no more slowly than an uncommented one to a noticeable degree.
- Comment bodies are short-form Markdown; a card truncates a very long comment to a readable height and reveals the rest in place rather than widening the rail beyond the margin it has.
- The margin the cards occupy is bounded above and below, so they stay legible on a wide window and readable on a narrow one; showing or hiding them reflows nothing in the artifact and never moves the caret, in either arrangement (CMT-FR-64). A tab too narrow to hold that margin beside the page moves the page before it moves the cards, and moves the cards before it covers anything: the field the page is centred in is spent first, the cards go below the page once it is spent, and no card is laid over the text it is about at any window size the shell supports.
- No height in the margin is held open for content that is not there. A section takes what it has to show and yields the rest, so the margin of an artifact whose discussions are all resolved, all discussions, or all orphaned reads as full as one carrying a mixture.
- Relative timestamps refresh as time passes without the author reopening the artifact.
- The rail holds only what the open artifact's discussions need; an artifact with no discussions costs one list call and no rendering.
- Every rail action is reachable by keyboard alone: the Comment affordance, each card, its overflow menu, its composer, and the mention picker within it are focusable and operable without a pointer.
- Posting a comment never waits on an agent: the comment appears in its card as soon as it is appended, and the turns it dispatched resolve into the card afterwards at whatever pace the models answer.
- Deriving a conversation's active agents is a read of the comments the rail already holds against the enrolled agents it already holds, and a read that stops at the first comment naming anybody rather than walking the whole discussion, so a follow-up costs no call beyond the turns it dispatches however many agents an `@all` turns out to name, the recipients a composer names in its placeholder cost nothing to keep current, and a conversation nobody has addressed an agent in dispatches nothing at all.
- A draft's discussions and the discussions fragmented in its prompt cost one list call when its tab opens, and nothing afterwards until a discussion is opened or answered.
- A card holding several outstanding turns stays legible: pending contributions occupy the same narrow measure a comment does and never widen the rail beyond the margin it has, and a failed contribution and its Retry occupy that same measure.
- An activity status costs the rail no call of its own: it is read from the turn records the rail already holds and the events it already follows, so a card says what an agent is doing without asking anything further and a turn that reaches for a dozen tools costs the same reads as one that reaches for none.
- A transient failure the application recovered from costs the author nothing to notice: the card reads **Thinking…** throughout and the answer arrives a moment later, so the only difference between a turn that succeeded first time and one that succeeded on its third attempt is how long the author waited.
- Recovering an offer to retry, and recovering an unsupported-image notice, cost one call each: the rail reads `"list recoverable agent turn failures"` and `"list agent turn image notices"` alongside the reads it already performs when an artifact opens, and follows events for every change afterwards, so a conversation carrying no failure costs nothing and one carrying a failure costs no polling.
- The rail never asks the backend what it has already been told: a conversation nobody is writing to costs no call after the artifact's first read, and a conversation being written to costs none at all beyond the events it receives.
- An attachment's content is loaded lazily and at the size the card shows it, so a review carrying dozens of screenshots opens as quickly as one carrying none and scrolling the rail is not gated on decoding pictures.
- Attaching is bounded by what the backend accepts: the composer states the refusal it received rather than pre-empting it, and a refused attachment never costs the author the comment they had written.
