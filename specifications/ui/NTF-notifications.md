# Notifications

**Spec code:** `NTF`

## Intent
The facility through which any surface of the application asks for the author's attention when they are not looking at it, and the address space that takes them back to the right place when they act on it. It exists because the work this application starts outlives the glance that started it — an agent run that takes an hour finishes while the author is reading mail, or stops to ask a question while they are three tabs away — and neither of those authors finds out on their own. It answers both: an operating-system notification reaches the one who has left the window, and a marked tab — or, for work watched in the Runs panel rather than in a tab, a marked activity-bar toggle — catches the one who is still in the window but looking at something else, so attention is asked for on whichever surface the author is actually in front of. One API serves both and serves every surface, so a run, a conversation, and whatever is added next all ask the same way and land the author somewhere by the same rules. Out of scope: deciding what is worth interrupting for, which belongs to each surface that raises; drawing the operating system's notification, which the operating system does (`../core/NTD-notification-delivery.md`); any inbox, history, list, or unread count of what has been raised, nothing raised being recorded and a notification missed being simply gone; marking anything but a main-viewport tab and the Runs toggle, every other panel and bottom-panel target being reached through its notification alone; and any means by which something outside this application could mint an address or hand one in — an address is minted inside the running application, carried by the operating system as opaque data, and parsed on its way back into that same application.

## User stories
- As an author who set an agent running an hour ago, I want to be told when it finishes without keeping the window in front of me, so that I can do something else in the meantime.
- As an author told that an agent is waiting on me, I want one click to put me where the question is, rather than hunting for which tab it came from.
- As an author working in one tab while something happens in another, I want to hear about it once rather than every time it makes progress.
- As an author with three agentic discussions open in three tabs, I want the tab something happened in to catch my eye, so that I can finish the sentence I am writing and go to it when I choose rather than when the operating system says so.
- As an author who does not want the operating system interrupting me, I want one switch that stops it doing so, without the window itself going quiet about what happened while I was in another tab.

## Wireframes

The statement, when an activated notification's address cannot be reached — anchored at the top-trailing corner of the shell, below the top chrome:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  ◈ acme ▾   main ▾  ⟳   [ search…                            ]      ( ✦ 2 )   ☾ ▾    │
├──┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│▤ │  Dashboard  │  RUN-runs.md  ×  │      ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐    │
│▤ ├─────────────┴──────────────────┤      │ That artifact is no longer in the    │  ✕ │
│▤ │                                │      │ project.                             │    │
│▤ │  # Runs                        │      └──────────────────────────────────────┘    │
│▤ │                                │                                                  │
```

- Layout notes: the statement floats at the shell's top-trailing corner immediately below the top chrome, clear of the tab strip's own controls, of the band above an Editor's editing surface (per `EFR-editor-find-replace.md` EFR-FR-AVML), and of the action control every tab carries in its opposite corner (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-01). It is bounded to a measure narrower than the viewport so its sentence wraps rather than running the shell's full width, it carries a dismissal control at its trailing edge and nothing else, and it is opaque over whatever it covers. It resizes nothing: the tab beneath it neither reflows nor scrolls when it appears or goes. Where the vertical panel is on the trailing edge (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-06) it floats over the panel rather than displacing it.

The tab strip with two tabs needing attention, `spec.md` being the active tab and `RUN-runs.md` additionally holding unsaved changes:

```
├──┬───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│▤ │  Dashboard  │◆ RUN-runs.md ● ×│  spec.md    × │◆ plan-draft   × │                  │
│▤ ├─────────────┴─────────────────┴───────────────┴─────────────────┴──────────────────┤
│▤ │                                                                                    │
```

- Layout notes: the mark sits at the tab's leading edge, ahead of the label, in space the strip reserves on every tab whether or not the mark is showing — so a tab taking or losing it changes neither its own width nor the position of any other tab in the strip. The dirty indicator keeps its own place after the label and the close control keeps the trailing edge; the mark covers neither, nor the label. The marked tab's surface additionally carries a tone of its own, distinct from both the active tab's and an ordinary inactive tab's, so shape and tone say the same thing twice. One mark stands on a tab however many raises are indicating it, and the active tab never carries one.

## UI contract boundary
- **Owned by the UI**: the raise API and its shape; the address space, its grammar, its minting and its parsing; the post policy of NTF-FR-08 and the focus and active-surface facts it is decided from; the derivation of the coalescing key; the derivation of the subtitle of NTF-FR-QGSV; the reading of the enable preference and the timing of the permission request; the routing of an activation to a tab or a panel surface; the resolution of an address to an open tab or to the Runs toggle, and the eligibility, the per-key state, the aggregation, the lifecycle, and the accessible state of the **indication** it marks that target with (NTF-FR-26 through NTF-FR-37, NTF-FR-39), whose emphasis the tab strip and the activity bar each render on this facility's behalf (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-26 and `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-70); the withdrawing and clearing rules of NTF-FR-14, NTF-FR-15, and the retraction of NTF-FR-38; and the statement, its anchoring, its bounds, and its dismissal. Nothing the operating system draws is owned here.

### The raise
Every surface asks for attention through one shape, and the facility decides from it whether anything is posted at all:

```
Raise {
  key,        // identity of the thing being raised about; a later raise with the
              // same key replaces the earlier rather than adding a second
  title,      // short line naming what happened
  body,       // the sentence beneath it
  address     // where the author lands on activation (NTF-FR-03)
}
```

A raise carries no subtitle. The facility adds one to every notification it posts (NTF-FR-QGSV):

```
posted notification = { key, title, subtitle: <project name>, body, payload: address }

retract(key)  // the thing that key was raised about is settled and needs the
              // author's attention no longer (NTF-FR-38)
```

### The address
An address is a `synthesis://` URI naming a project, a worktree of it, and a target within that worktree. Every segment but the file path is one percent-encoded component:

```
synthesis://<project-key>/<worktree>/<target>

target :=  dashboard
        |  file/<project-relative path>
        |  draft/<draft-id>
        |  panel/<library | notes | comments | drafts | changes>
        |  bottom/<runs | logs | git | history>
        |  run/<run-id>
        |  settings/<global | project>
```

- **Delegated to backend (abstract)**:
  - `"post notification"`, `"withdraw notification"`, `"withdraw all notifications"`, `"get notification permission"`, and `"request notification permission"`, together with the event `"notification activated"` — all owned by `../core/NTD-notification-delivery.md`. The address travels as that module's opaque `payload` (per `../core/NTD-notification-delivery.md` NTD-FR-08), so no backend parses it.
  - `"load app preferences"` and `"save app preferences"` (shared with `GLS-global-settings.md` and `SNV-shell-navigation.md`, defined by `../core/GSS-global-settings-storage.md` GSS-FR-32) carry the enable switch. This facility reads that record and introduces no command of its own for it; the switch itself is presented by `GLS-global-settings.md` GLS-FR-25.

  - The event `"github polling changed"` (`../core/GPP-github-polling.md` GPP-FR-TYOV) is what the GitHub poll raise of NTF-FR-JLXL follows. This facility invokes no polling operation.

  The indication introduces no operation at all. It reaches neither the notification commands nor the preferences record, is held in memory here for the life of the running application, and is delegated to nothing.

## Functional requirements
1. **NTF-FR-01** Every surface of the application asks for the author's attention through one **raise**, carrying a key, a title, a body, and an address, and through nothing else. There is no second route to the notification centre and no surface reaches `"post notification"` directly, so the policy of NTF-FR-08 through NTF-FR-11 governs every notification the application ever posts. A surface takes attention back the same way, through the one **retraction** of NTF-FR-38, and no surface reaches `"withdraw notification"` directly either — so what is posted and what is taken down are decided in one place for the whole application.
2. **NTF-FR-02** A raise is a request rather than an instruction: the facility decides whether it becomes a posted notification, and a raise that becomes nothing is the ordinary case rather than a failure. The raising surface is told nothing either way and changes nothing about itself on account of a raise.
3. **NTF-FR-03** An address is a `synthesis://` URI naming a project, one of its worktrees, and exactly one target within it, in the grammar of the UI contract boundary. Its targets are the Dashboard, a project file by project-relative path, a draft by id, a vertical-panel surface, a bottom-panel surface, a graduation run by id, and the two settings windows (per `SWN-settings-windows.md` SWN-FR-01). A run is addressable by id rather than reached as a bottom-panel surface alone because a project holds a queue of them and landing on the panel with the wrong one selected is landing nowhere. A Search results, History detail, or Diff tab is not addressable, each being a reading the author composes rather than a place work happens.
4. **NTF-FR-04** An address names the project by the same key the per-project settings slot is keyed by (per `../core/GSS-global-settings-storage.md` GSS-FR-18) and the worktree by its path, so one file in two worktrees of a repository yields two distinct addresses and an address is never ambiguous about which content root it means.
5. **NTF-FR-05** An address is minted by the surface that raises and parsed only here. It is a value rather than a live handle: it holds no reference to a tab, a buffer, an editing session, a thread, or a run, so it costs nothing to keep, and an address minted an hour ago parses exactly as one minted a moment ago.
6. **NTF-FR-06** The address travels to the operating system and back as opaque data (per `../core/NTD-notification-delivery.md` NTD-FR-08). Nothing outside this application mints one, hands one in, or acts on one, and nothing about the grammar is registered with the operating system.
7. **NTF-FR-07** A raise's `key` is passed as the posted notification's key, so a surface raising repeatedly about one thing replaces its own notification in place rather than stacking (per `../core/NTD-notification-delivery.md` NTD-FR-06). Two surfaces raising about two different things carry different keys and occupy two slots.
8. **NTF-FR-08** A raise becomes a posted notification when either no window of the application holds OS focus, or one does but the raise's address does not name what the author is already looking at. It is **suppressed** when a window of the application holds focus and the address names the main viewport's active tab, names a panel surface that is at that moment the visible active surface of its panel, or names the settings window that is at that moment open (per `SWN-settings-windows.md` SWN-FR-05) — the one case in which the author has already been told by the thing itself. A `run` address is held to the same test one step finer, on the terms NTF-FR-39 marks by: it is suppressed only where Runs is that moment's visible active bottom surface **with that run selected**, and is posted where the panel is hidden, where another bottom surface is active, and where Runs is active on a different run, because a queue the author is reading is not the run that has stopped.
9. **NTF-FR-09** A raise made while no project is open is posted, the Project picker never being where any address's target lives.
10. **NTF-FR-10** A raise suppressed by NTF-FR-08 is suppressed silently and totally: it posts nothing, marks nothing, withdraws nothing, replaces nothing, is not queued for later, and is not recorded anywhere. The author is never shown it by some other means, the surface it names being the one already in front of them.
11. **NTF-FR-11** No raise is posted while the enable switch is off (`GLS-global-settings.md` GLS-FR-25), whatever the focus state, and none is posted while the operating system's permission is anything other than granted (per `../core/NTD-notification-delivery.md` NTD-FR-14). Each withholds the posting and nothing else: nothing is queued, nothing is recorded, and the indication of NTF-FR-26 is untouched by either (NTF-FR-29).
12. **NTF-FR-12** The enable switch is read from `"load app preferences"` when the application starts and is kept current with the Notifications section thereafter, so turning it off stops the next raise without a relaunch and turning it on resumes at the next one.
13. **NTF-FR-13** The application never requests notification permission of its own accord: it reads the state with `"get notification permission"` and asks for it with `"request notification permission"` only where the author asked for notifications — enabling the switch, or running the rehearsal (`GLS-global-settings.md` GLS-FR-26, GLS-FR-27). A permission prompt therefore never appears because some background work finished.
14. **NTF-FR-WUUY** When the main window gets OS focus, the facility reads the permission again with `"get notification permission"`, so a change the author made in the operating system's settings applies from the next raise. This read requests nothing and shows no prompt.
15. **NTF-FR-14** The author **reaches** what a raise addressed when an activation of its notification (NTF-FR-16) or a route of their own actually resolves and presents that target — activating or opening that tab, opening that panel surface. The moment they do, every posted notification whose address it reaches is withdrawn and every indication whose address it reaches is cleared (NTF-FR-32), so neither outlives the thing it was about being seen, and clicking a notification for something already read is not something the author is offered. The main window merely gaining OS focus reaches only what its already-active tab is a view onto: an address naming that tab is reached by it, and an address naming anything else is not, focus alone having brought the author no nearer to it.
16. **NTF-FR-15** Closing the project, switching the active project (per `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-11), and changing the active worktree (per `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-12) each withdraw every posted notification and clear every indication whose address names a content root the application is no longer reading, so neither is left pointing into a root nothing can navigate to. Quitting withdraws all the notifications (per `../core/NTD-notification-delivery.md` NTD-FR-12) and takes every indication with the process, no indication outliving the run that made it.
17. **NTF-FR-16** On `"notification activated"` the payload is parsed as an address and routed by NTF-FR-17 through NTF-FR-19. The window has already been raised and focused by the time the event arrives (per `../core/NTD-notification-delivery.md` NTD-FR-09) and this facility does not raise it again.
18. **NTF-FR-17** An address naming the open project and its active worktree opens its target and focuses it: a **file** address opens an Editor or Flow tab according to what the file is, or jumps focus to the tab already open on it (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-02, TAB-FR-05); a **draft** address opens a New Artifact tab on that draft, or jumps focus to the one already open on it (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-17); a **panel** address makes that surface the vertical panel's active surface, opening the panel if it was hidden (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-45); a **bottom** address opens the bottom panel on that surface and marks its toggle active (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-47); a **run** address does the same for the Runs surface and additionally makes its graduation section active with that run selected (per `RUN-runs.md` RUN-FR-10 and `GRH-graduation-history.md` GRH-FR-NCJK); a **settings** address opens or focuses that settings child window, closing the other settings window first where that one is open (per `GLS-global-settings.md` GLS-FR-01, `SET-project-settings.md` SET-FR-01, and `SWN-settings-windows.md` SWN-FR-05 through SWN-FR-07); and a **dashboard** address opens or focuses the Dashboard tab (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-41).
19. **NTF-FR-18** Activation performs exactly the navigation the author's own equivalent gesture performs, and no more — including whatever that gesture already carries with it. A file or draft address activating a tab is a change of the active tab like any other, so the vertical panel follows it while **Selection follows tab** is on, landing on that file or draft in its own panel exactly as it would had the author opened the tab themselves (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-64, SNV-FR-65). Beyond that: no other tab closes, no panel is opened or hidden that neither the address nor that rule names, no active worktree or active project changes, and the persisted state a navigation leaves behind is exactly the state that same navigation leaves when the author performs it from the activity bar or a panel (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-08).
20. **NTF-FR-19** An address that cannot be reached changes nothing at all and is stated instead (NTF-FR-20). It cannot be reached when it names a project other than the open one, a worktree other than the active one, a file or draft that no longer resolves, or a grammar this facility cannot parse — and likewise when it is activated while no project is open, in which case the Project picker is left exactly as it is. In none of these does the application switch a project, switch a worktree, open a project, close a tab, or write a preference on the author's behalf.
21. **NTF-FR-20** The **statement** is a brief line anchored at the top-trailing corner of the shell naming what could not be reached — the artifact, the draft, the project, or the worktree the address named — and offering nothing but a dismissal. It is dismissed by that control, by Escape, and on its own after a short interval, and a second statement replaces the one showing rather than stacking beside it.
22. **NTF-FR-21** The statement takes no focus and intercepts no pointer event, so it is not one of the main window's mutually-exclusive floating overlays: it may be showing while a modal or a dropdown is open without either dismissing the other, and it never blocks a click on what lies beneath it (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-56).
23. **NTF-FR-22** The statement and the tab indication are the whole of what this facility renders inside the window. There is no inbox, no list of what has been raised, no history of it, no unread count, no badge on any control outside the tab strip and the activity bar's Runs toggle (NTF-FR-39), no panel surface, and no tab of its own; nothing raised is recorded, no indication is persisted, and nothing about either survives a relaunch, a project switch, or a worktree change.
24. **NTF-FR-23** Raising never blocks or fails the surface that raised: a raise returns immediately, and a post the backend refuses for any reason — permission, platform, or delivery — is surfaced nowhere, because the author asked no question that an error would answer.
25. **NTF-FR-24** Ordinary work raises nothing. Opening a project, scanning it, indexing it, editing, saving, committing, pushing, searching, and computing a change set post no notification, because a raise is something a surface asks for explicitly. Exactly five things raise: the rehearsal of `GLS-global-settings.md` GLS-FR-27, which raises only when the author activates it; a change an agent has proposed to a **draft's prompt** (per `DCR-draft-change-review.md` DCR-FR-18); a change an agent has proposed to a **prompt artifact the project already holds** (per `PCR-prompt-change-review.md` PCR-FR-17); a **graduation run coming to rest on a decision only the author can take** — a question waiting on an answer, a change set waiting on a review, a publication that stopped, a terminal outcome, and an interruption on a failure reason (per `GRU-graduation-runs.md` GRU-FR-BLSS); and a **GitHub poll that finds new ready tasks** (NTF-FR-JLXL). The last two raise when the proposal is recorded, because each is a decision the author is now owed and the tab holding it may not be one they are looking at, and they differ only in what their address names — the draft address of NTF-FR-03 for one, the project-file address for the other. Nothing raises when such a proposal is **decided**; for a prompt artifact what happens then is the opposite, the review **retracting** what it raised (NTF-FR-38), because a decided proposal is a thing the author no longer needs calling back to. Only the poll is a background job, and it raises only for work that is new and waiting on the author; the rehearsal is the author asking to be shown the mechanism, and the other three are work that has stopped and is waiting on them. A run that is merely still working raises nothing, progress not being news.
26. **NTF-FR-25** The rehearsal's raise goes through this facility on exactly the terms every other raise does — the same shape, the same key, the same policy, the same routing — so exercising it exercises the whole path rather than a special case of it. Its address names the Global settings window of the open project and active worktree, which resolves whether or not that window is still open, so the arrival end of the path is reachable every time.
27. **NTF-FR-FPLB** The rehearsal's title states that it is a test notification, and does not repeat the application's name, which the operating system already shows. Its body tells the author that a click brings them back to Global settings.
28. **NTF-FR-QGSV** Every posted notification carries, as its subtitle, the display name of the project its address names: the last segment of that project's key. Only this facility derives the subtitle. No surface supplies or overrides it, so every notification has one shape: the title states what happened, the subtitle names the project, and the body gives the detail.
29. **NTF-FR-26** The **indication** is the in-app half of a raise: a raise whose address resolves to a main-viewport tab that is open and is not the active tab marks that tab as needing attention, and a raise carrying a `run` address marks the activity bar's Runs toggle on the terms of NTF-FR-39. It marks whether or not the main window holds OS focus, and whether or not anything was posted for the same raise, the two surfaces answering the same question for authors in two different places — the one who has left the window, and the one who is in it but reading another tab.
30. **NTF-FR-27** The tab an indication marks is the one that address's own routing would activate (NTF-FR-17), and exactly that one: the Editor or Flow tab open on a `file` address, the New Artifact tab open on a `draft` address, and the Dashboard tab. A `settings` address marks no tab either, a settings window being a window rather than a tab (per `SWN-settings-windows.md` SWN-FR-18). A Diff tab or a History detail tab open on the same file is not marked, being no more where an activation lands than it is (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-06), so one raise never marks two tabs.
31. **NTF-FR-28** Among tabs, only an open tab is ever marked. A `panel` or `bottom` address marks no tab and is reached through its notification alone; an address whose target has no tab open marks nothing, and no tab is created, opened, focused, or reordered to carry an indication, the notification remaining the whole route to a target the author has not opened. A `run` address marks no tab either — it marks the Runs toggle instead (NTF-FR-39), a run being work the author watches in a panel rather than a thing a tab is a view onto. A `settings` address marks nothing anywhere and is reached through its notification alone. A `dashboard` address while the Dashboard tab is closed marks nothing either, the Home affordance standing in its place being a control of the strip rather than a tab (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-09).
32. **NTF-FR-29** The active tab is never a marked tab, whether the raise naming it was suppressed by NTF-FR-08 or posted because the window was unfocused: a tab the author is on is one they can already see. Nothing else about the posting bears on the indication: an otherwise eligible raise marks its tab while the enable switch is off, while the operating system's permission reads denied, not requested, or unsupported, and on a platform with no notification centre to reach at all. The indication reads no permission, requests none, and no preference turns it off.
33. **NTF-FR-30** An indication is held here, by the raise's key together with the address it resolved, for the life of the running application and nowhere else. It names no tab component and no position in the strip, so a tab remounting, the strip re-rendering, or the tabs around it opening and closing leaves every indication on the tab it belongs to. It is written to no preference, reaches no backend operation, and forms no part of any notification record (per `../core/NTD-notification-delivery.md` NTD-FR-18), so the application starts with none.
34. **NTF-FR-31** A tab renders **one** emphasis however many keys are indicating it, so a second thing happening in a tab already marked makes it no more insistent than the first did. The per-key state stands behind that one emphasis: a later raise carrying a key already indicating replaces that key's indication rather than adding a second, and a key whose address has moved to a different tab clears the tab it was marking and marks the new one, so a key marks at most one tab at a time.
35. **NTF-FR-32** Reaching a target (NTF-FR-14) clears every indication whose address it reaches and no others: a key still indicating the same tab keeps that tab's emphasis standing, and every other tab's is untouched, so clearing one key is never clearing the tab. An indication is cleared by reaching, by unreachability (NTF-FR-15, NTF-FR-33), and by the retraction of NTF-FR-38, and by nothing else — a posted notification leaving the notification centre by any other route clears no indication, the author having read nothing by dismissing it there (per `../core/NTD-notification-delivery.md` NTD-FR-11), and an indication stands perfectly well where nothing was ever posted.
36. **NTF-FR-33** An indication whose tab is gone is gone with it: closing a tab clears every indication it carried, and reopening that target opens an unmarked tab, an indication being the tab's way of saying the author has not been there and a tab they have just opened not being that. Closing a tab withdraws nothing from the notification centre, the target being still reachable and the posted notification still the route to it.
37. **NTF-FR-34** The emphasis is a **mark** at the tab's leading edge together with a distinct surface tone, so shape and tone each carry it and neither colour alone nor motion alone is what the author reads. It is distinct from the dirty indicator the tab already renders, which keeps its own place, and it obscures neither the tab's label nor its close control; the strip reserves the mark's space whether it is showing or not, so a tab taking or losing an indication shifts nothing in the strip.
38. **NTF-FR-35** The emphasis draws the eye once and then stops. On being marked, and on a superseding raise (NTF-FR-31), it pulses a small fixed number of cycles over about a second and settles into a static emphasis that stands until it is cleared. It is rate-limited, so a burst of raises against one tab produces one pulse rather than one per raise, and nothing in the strip animates continuously however long an indication stands. Where the platform reports a reduced-motion preference the emphasis is static from the moment it appears and never pulses at all.
39. **NTF-FR-36** A marked tab carries an accessible **needs attention** state on the tab itself, so a screen reader traversing the strip is told which tabs need attention rather than left to infer it from a treatment it cannot see. Taking the state and losing it are each announced once per change rather than once per key, the tab renders one emphasis whatever the count (NTF-FR-31) and says so the same way.
40. **NTF-FR-37** An indication changes nothing else about the application. It activates no tab, moves no tab, opens or hides no panel, takes no focus from what the author is typing into, plays no sound, and writes no preference; an activated notification does exactly what NTF-FR-17 and NTF-FR-18 say whether or not the tab it lands on was marked; and activating a marked tab is an ordinary tab activation carrying whatever an activation already carries (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-64, SNV-FR-65).
41. **NTF-FR-38** A surface **retracts** by the key it raised with, and retracting says that the thing that key was raised about is settled and needs the author's attention no longer. It is the counterpart of the raise and the only route by which attention is taken back: the facility withdraws every posted notification carrying that key through `"withdraw notification"` and clears every indication derived from it, in one act and **without any reload, re-read, or reopening of anything**. It exists because reaching a target (NTF-FR-14) is not the only way a thing stops being worth the author's attention — a decision they made in another window settles it just as finally as reading it does, and a notification that outlives what it was about sends the author to a surface with nothing left on it.
    Four things are true of it and each rules out a way it could mislead. It acts **whether or not the author ever reached the address**, so nothing is left standing for a thing that was settled elsewhere. It acts **whether or not the main window holds focus** and whatever the enable switch and the operating system's permission say, exactly as the indication of NTF-FR-29 is raised regardless of them — withdrawing what was posted where something was, and clearing what was marked where something was, and doing neither where neither happened. It is **scoped to the key and to nothing else**: every other posted notification and every other marked tab is untouched, so retracting one thing never quiets another, and two things raised about one tab under two keys are retracted one at a time. And it is a **no-op** against a key nothing was raised for, against one already retracted, and against one whose notification the author has already dismissed or reached, so a surface may retract without first knowing what became of what it raised.
    Retraction is the surface's own to call and this facility neither infers one nor watches for the conditions of one: what counts as settled is the raising surface's affair, on exactly the terms NTF-FR-02 makes the decision to raise theirs. It is offered to every surface that raises; in this build one surface calls it — `PCR-prompt-change-review.md` (PCR-FR-26), when a change an agent proposed to a prompt artifact is decided, on the event that proposal's own store emits and with the key it raised that proposal under.
42. **NTF-FR-39** A raise carrying a `run` address marks the activity bar's **Runs toggle** as needing attention, on the same terms every other indication is held under. It marks whenever the author is not already looking at that run's surface — while another bottom-panel surface is active, while the bottom panel is hidden altogether, and while Runs is showing but a different run is selected — and it marks whether or not the window holds focus and whether or not anything was posted for the same raise, exactly as NTF-FR-26 and NTF-FR-29 say of a tab. It does not mark while Runs is the active bottom-panel surface with that run already selected, which is the run's equivalent of the active tab. The toggle renders **one** emphasis however many runs are indicating it, on the terms NTF-FR-31 sets, and carries the same accessible needs-attention state NTF-FR-36 requires, so a strip of nine icons still says in words which one wants the author. The mark stands until the run it names is **reached** (NTF-FR-14) — by activating the notification, or by the author opening Runs on that run themselves — and is otherwise cleared only by the unreachability of NTF-FR-15 and the retraction of NTF-FR-38, so reaching one run leaves another's mark standing. The Runs toggle is always present, so nothing here has the tab-closing lifecycle of NTF-FR-33; a mark it holds is held here for the life of the running application, written to no preference and reaching no backend operation, exactly as NTF-FR-30 already says of every indication. Marking it changes nothing else: no panel opens or hides, no surface becomes active, no run is selected, and no focus moves.
43. **NTF-FR-JLXL** A `"github polling changed"` whose new-issue list is not empty raises **once** for that poll, with the key `github-ready-tasks` and the `bottom/git` address of the open project and active worktree. The body names the new issues' titles where there are at most three, and their count otherwise.
44. **NTF-FR-VALU** A `"github polling changed"` whose new-issue list is empty raises nothing. The backend reports no new issue for a failed, stale, or discarded poll, or for an issue already reported in the polling session (per `../core/GPP-github-polling.md` GPP-FR-RRPB), so none of these raises.

## Non-functional requirements
- A raise is cheap enough to call from an event handler or a tight update loop without measurably delaying the surface that calls it; the decision of NTF-FR-08 and the resolution of an address to an open tab (NTF-FR-27) are both made from state the shell already holds and cost no backend round-trip.
- Marking and clearing a tab are immediate UI operations. A burst of raises against one tab is coalesced rather than re-rendering the strip once per raise, and a strip carrying many indications costs no more to render than one carrying none.
- The pulse of NTF-FR-35 is bounded in cycles and in duration, so nothing in the tab strip is animating while the author reads, and a session left with tabs indicated for an hour is as quiet as one with none.
- The indication takes no focus and plays no sound: nothing about a tab being marked or cleared interrupts typing, scrolling, or a drag in progress.
- Nothing here requires network access.
- The statement never takes focus, never intercepts a pointer event, and never resizes or reflows anything: the tab beneath it is laid out identically whether it is showing or not.
- The statement, the raise, and the routing all stay usable and legible at the minimum window size the shell supports (≥1280×800), the statement bounded so what it floats over keeps the greater part of the viewport.
- The window is already in front of the author before any routing happens, so a tab never opens into a window the author cannot see.
- An address is stable text: it survives being held across a project switch, a relaunch of the surface that minted it, and an arbitrary delay, and parses to the same target or to no target at all — never to a different one.
