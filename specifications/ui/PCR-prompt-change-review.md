# Prompt change review

**Spec code:** `PCR`

## Intent
A proposal against a published prompt artifact is a **whole alternative document**, decided in one act, and this surface is the modal that decides it. This is deliberately not the shape a **draft's** prompt takes: a draft is private working material whose changes are proposed as anchored hunks and decided one by one in the document itself (per `DCR-draft-change-review.md` DCR-FR-20).

The surface on which an author decides what to do with a change an agent has proposed to a **prompt the project already holds**. It is a modal over that artifact's Editor tab showing the proposed version against the file as it stood when the review opened, read in the same three shapes and the same two renderings every other comparison in the application is read in (`DFV-diff-viewer.md`), with the agent's reasoning above it and **Accept** and **Reject** — and a place to say why — pinned at its foot. It exists because an agent that can write a better version of a prompt is only useful if the author can see the whole of what it wrote before any of it lands: a summary in prose cannot be reviewed, and a rewrite performed and reported afterwards is not a proposal. The proposed version is the **candidate**, and the author may **edit it here** on exactly the target-only terms a Diff tab's target is edited on (`DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-41), because a proposal that is right but for one word should not have to be declined and asked for again; what the author edits is the candidate and never the artifact, so the two questions the surface answers — *is this text right?* and *shall it land?* — stay separate, and the file is untouched until the second is answered with **Accept**. The artifact's text as it stood when the review opened is the fixed thing the candidate is read against and is never editable here. Because the work that produced a proposal outlives the glance that started it, this surface also **calls the author back to it**: a proposal recorded while they are looking at something else, or at another application entirely, raises a notification that lands them on the file with the change in front of them. It is deliberately the **same review the author already knows**: everything about how it is laid out, what its three bands hold, how its controls behave, and how the comparison is read and corrected is `DCR-draft-change-review.md`'s and is adopted here unchanged (PCR-FR-02), so reviewing an agent's rewrite is one thing learned once whether the material is a draft or a published prompt. Out of scope: the proposal's storage, its one-pending-per-artifact rule, the candidate's own persistence, and the acceptance itself, all owned by `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md`; composing a proposal, which is an agent's and reaches the author through `../tools/PPC-propose-prompt-changes-tool.md`; the comment the proposal and the decision each take in the conversation, which `CMT-comments.md` renders and `../core/CMS-comments-storage.md` holds; the artifact's own version history, which is Git's and is read in `HVW-history-viewer.md`; deciding what is worth interrupting for, the policy of every raise being `NTF-notifications.md`'s; accepting part of a proposal, which is not offered — the candidate is a complete document and is applied whole, however much of it the author rewrote first; and a second review layout of any kind, this surface introducing none.

## User stories
- As an author, I want to read the whole of what an agent would write before any of it touches a file of my project, so that accepting is a judgement rather than a hope.
- As an author, I want to read that change the same way I read every other comparison in the application, so that nothing about reviewing an agent's work is learned twice.
- As an author who declines a rewrite, I want to say why in the same breath, so that the agent's next attempt is better rather than identical.
- As an author looking at a proposal that is right but for one word, I want to fix that word and accept, so that a good rewrite is not thrown away over a detail.
- As an author who has edited a candidate, I want to be sure my file has not changed until I press Accept, and to be told plainly which of the two has happened.
- As an author who kept editing the file while the review sat open, I want Accept to still do what the review says it will do rather than refuse me for having worked.
- As an author working in another tab, I want to be told when an agent has something for me to look at, rather than discovering it an hour later.

- Layout notes: the modal is centred on the **Editor tab** the artifact is open in, on a scrim covering that tab and stopping at its edges, so the shell around it stays lit and reachable. Its head names the **project-relative** path, the agent, and a relative time, with the agent's title on a line of its own beneath the name where the participant carries one, and the rationale beneath as prose across the modal's full measure. The comparison fills the body edge to edge on the same flat field text is set on elsewhere, without a frame, a recessed fill, or a page edge of its own, because the modal gives it no margin for one to read against. The toggle row is centred as one cluster with a divider between its two groups, and the candidate's write state sits at that row's trailing end. **Accept** is the affirmative control and sits trailing of **Reject**, held further apart than controls elsewhere: they are opposites, each irreversible, and the space between them is what a slipped click lands in. The feedback field beneath them is quiet until it is used — a narrow measure, one line tall, no fill, and a single hairline rule beneath the text rather than a box around it.

## UI contract boundary
- **Owned by the UI**: the modal's binding to the Editor tab and the routes that open it, including the one that opens it from a notification activation; **the derivation of the comparison from the two revisions it holds** — the hunks, the line pairing, the filler rows, the block granularity of rich marking, and the word-level marking within a replaced unit — and its recomputation as the candidate is edited; the capture of the base revision when the review opens and its retention thereafter; the reading of the user-global visualization and rendering modes and the toggle row that sets them; the rationale's rendering; **the candidate's editing surface in Source and in Rich, which of the rendered content is the editable candidate and which the read-only base revision, the candidate buffer this modal holds between openings, the schedule on which it is written, and the presentation of its write state**; the feedback field and its content; the enablement of the two decision controls; the pending indication the Editor tab carries; the statement a decided proposal's foot carries; the inline rendering of every refusal the operations below report; the quiescing and resetting of the artifact's editing session around an acceptance; and the raise made when a proposal is recorded, together with the address it carries.
  The modal computes the comparison but defines none of how it reads, and it edits the candidate but defines none of how editing behaves: every mode, every marking, every rule about what each mode shows, and the whole target-only editing model are `DFV-diff-viewer.md`'s and are applied here unchanged (PCR-FR-06, PCR-FR-20). Where the candidate is Markdown read in **Rich**, the editing semantics and serialisation guarantees are `EDT-editor.md`'s on the terms `DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-47 borrows them.

- **Delegated to backend (abstract)**:
  - `"list prompt change proposals"`, `"load prompt change proposal content"`, `"save prompt change proposal candidate"`, `"apply prompt change proposal"`, `"decline prompt change proposal"`, and `"complete prompt change decision"` — owned by `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md`. The last is invoked by one control and one control alone: the retry the modal offers when an acceptance landed and its decision comment is still owed (PCR-FR-15). The candidate save is the one operation the author's editing invokes, and it writes the proposal's candidate and no file of the project. The two decisions return the proposal together with the comment the decision was recorded as and the origin kind of the conversation it landed in, which is what PCR-FR-14's one dispatch is composed from.
  - `"load artifact contents by id"` — the artifact's current text, which is the comparison's base revision, read through the artifact's own editing session on exactly the terms an Editor tab reads it (per `EXC-editor-external-change.md` EXC-FR-WCOM). Owned by `../core/PST-project-storage.md`.
  - `"load app preferences"` and `"save app preferences"` — the persisted visualization and rendering modes, shared with `DFV-diff-viewer.md` (DFV-FR-23) and defined by `../core/GSS-global-settings-storage.md` (GSS-FR-24).
  - `"dispatch agent turn"` — owned by `../core/AGC-agent-conversations.md`; invoked after a decision lands, once for each distinct agent the conversation's active set holds and not at all where it is empty (PCR-FR-14).
  - The events `"prompt change proposals changed"` (`../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-17) and `"artifact changed externally"` (`../core/PST-project-storage.md` PST-FR-16) keep the modal and the tab's pending indication current.
  - The raise and the retraction are both `NTF-notifications.md`'s facility (NTF-FR-01, NTF-FR-38) and introduce no operation here; the address the raise carries is that spec's project-file target (NTF-FR-03), and the retraction carries the same key the raise did (PCR-FR-17, PCR-FR-26).

## Functional requirements
1. **PCR-FR-01** The review modal is a **modal over the Editor tab** open on the proposal's artifact. It is anchored within that tab rather than being one of the main window's mutually-exclusive floating overlays (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-56), so opening it closes no overlay and no overlay closes it, and it dismisses none of the tab's other transient surfaces and is dismissed by none of them (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-12). Its scrim covers that tab's own bounds and no more: the shell around it — the activity rail, the panels, the tab strip — stays lit and reachable while the author reads.
2. **PCR-FR-02** The modal is composed as three bands: a **head** naming the file, the agent, and the time, and carrying the rationale; a **body** holding the toggle row and the comparison, which alone scrolls; and a **foot** carrying the two decision controls with the optional feedback field beneath them, pinned so they are reachable without scrolling a long comparison.
3. **PCR-FR-ZKTM** The feedback field opens one line tall on a measure narrower than the foot, grows with what is typed to a bound of four lines, and scrolls within that bound. Both decision controls are disabled while the outcome of activating one would be ambiguous, each stating why on hover.
4. **PCR-FR-JQWD** Every inline statement and every refusal renders directly above the foot's controls. A proposal already decided opens for reading, its foot carrying a statement of which way it was decided and when, together with a dismissal, in place of the controls and the field.
5. **PCR-FR-BXPL** Dismissing the modal by its dismissal control, by Escape, or by a pointer-down on the scrim decides nothing and changes no file: no proposal is accepted or declined, and it is still pending afterwards.
6. **PCR-FR-VHNC** The candidate's editing surface carries a focus treatment distinct from the base revision's and an accessible name naming the file and the proposing agent. The base is announced read-only, and **Accept** and **Reject** stay two independently reachable controls with their own accessible names.
7. **PCR-FR-RGSD** A candidate holding no text is an empty editable surface carrying a statement that the proposal would leave the **file** empty, and it leaves **Accept enabled** (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-13).
   - *Why:* An empty candidate is a change the author is entitled to accept, not a state to be corrected first.
8. **PCR-FR-03** The modal shows exactly one proposal, and it is reachable only from the Editor tab open on that proposal's artifact. It is opened from four routes and no others: the **arrival** of a proposal while the session is running (PCR-FR-16), the **proposal control** on the agent's comment in the rail (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-48), the tab's **pending indication** (PCR-FR-16), and the landing of a **notification activation** for that file (PCR-FR-18). **At most one review is open at a time, and every route to one proposal opens or focuses the same instance**: a route activated while that proposal's review is already showing brings it forward and creates nothing, so no proposal is ever open twice and no two instances of a review can disagree about a candidate. A request naming a *different* proposal while one is open leaves the open one standing rather than replacing it: what is showing is the proposal the author is deciding, and swapping it out under their pointer would decide it by accident.
9. **PCR-FR-04** The modal renders the comparison between two revisions. The **base** is the artifact's text **captured at the moment the review opens**, read through that artifact's editing session, and it is held for as long as the review is showing. The **candidate** is the proposal's text as it currently stands, fetched with `"load prompt change proposal content"`. The base is the original of the comparison and the candidate is its target, which is what makes the editing model of PCR-FR-20 the one `DFV-diff-viewer.md` defines rather than one of this surface's own. Line terminators are normalised on both sides before the comparison is derived, on the terms `DFV-diff-viewer.md` normalises them. The base is **never editable**, in any mode.
10. **PCR-FR-05** The captured base does not move. A file rewritten after the review opened — by the author in another surface, by an agent, by a checkout, or by anything else — leaves the comparison on screen exactly as it was: the base is a reading taken at a moment, and moving it under a decision would move the diff the decision is about. What the modal does instead is **say so**, in the standing line above its controls, stating that the file has changed since the review opened and that **Accept** replaces it whole with the version shown. **Accept stays enabled**, and the edited candidate is applied **whole**. Accepting applies it as a complete replacement of the artifact's contents **as they stand at that moment** (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-13), so the change the author read is the outcome they get. Three things are explicitly not in the way of it: **no baseline checksum is compared** anywhere in the decision, so the artifact's baseline having moved on cannot produce a rejection; **no external-change modal is raised or awaited** for the artifact while the review stands, and one already up is dismissed unanswered when **Accept** is activated (PCR-FR-11); and **no conflict is raised** over the base, which stays exactly as captured. An intervening change therefore neither blocks the decision, nor delays it behind a dialog, nor turns it into something the author has to resolve first. A published file's acceptance records no version against the base it replaced, so being refused for having kept working is a worse answer than being told plainly what Accept will do.
11. **PCR-FR-06** The comparison is read in the modes of `DFV-diff-viewer.md` and in no others: the **visualization** group of Unified, Side-by-side, and Final, and the **rendering** group of Source and Rich, in the toggle row and glyph treatment of DFV-FR-07, each mode rendering exactly what that spec defines it to render — the two-column gutter and hunk windowing of DFV-FR-09 and DFV-FR-10, the aligned rows, filler, and single shared scroll position of DFV-FR-11 through DFV-FR-13, the outcome-only marking of DFV-FR-14, the block granularity of DFV-FR-19 through DFV-FR-22, and the word-level marking within a replaced unit of DFV-FR-32 — and each of the six combinations offering the editing the same mode offers there (PCR-FR-20). A comparison read here, one read in a Diff tab, and one read in a draft change review are one thing read in three places, and corrected the same way in all of them.
12. **PCR-FR-07** Which renderings apply is the file's own affair, decided exactly as `DFV-diff-viewer.md` decides it. A prompt artifact whose name ends `.md` is Markdown and both rendering toggles are enabled; one whose name does not is read in **Source** with the rendering group disabled on the terms of DFV-FR-18, the disablement being display-only so the stored preference is untouched. The **Flow** reading of DFV-FR-17 is never reached here, the target's resolved artifact type being `prompt` by the tool's own rule (per `../tools/PPC-propose-prompt-changes-tool.md` PPC-FR-06) and never `flow`. A Source rendering carries the syntax highlighting of DFV-FR-57 on that spec's terms.
13. **PCR-FR-08** The active visualization and rendering modes are the **same user-global choice** `DFV-diff-viewer.md` carries (DFV-FR-23): they are read from `"load app preferences"` and written through `"save app preferences"` when the author activates a different toggle here, so a change made in this modal is in force in every open Diff tab immediately, and a change made in a Diff tab is what this modal opens in. A user who has never chosen reads it in Unified and Source.
14. **PCR-FR-09** The base revision is captured when the modal opens and retained for as long as it is showing, and the candidate is fetched then too **unless the modal already holds a candidate buffer for that proposal** (PCR-FR-21), in which case the buffer is what renders. Switching among the six mode combinations re-renders from held text without a further round-trip. The modal fetches nothing while it is closed: an artifact carrying a pending proposal costs its tab the indication of PCR-FR-16 and no read of any document.
15. **PCR-FR-10** **Accept** invokes `"apply prompt change proposal"` with the proposal and whatever the feedback field holds, having first brought forward any outstanding candidate write (PCR-FR-22) so what lands is the candidate as the author last edited it rather than as the agent composed it. That one call is the whole of what this surface does: the artifact's new bytes and the proposal's accepted state are **one transaction with one commit point**, which the backend performs and recovers (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-14) and which this modal neither sequences, retries, nor compensates for a partial one. The write goes through the project's own artifact write path and through `../core/FSA-filesystem-access.md`'s atomic primitive. On success the modal closes and the artifact holds that candidate. **Every failure short of the commit point leaves the artifact byte-for-byte as it was and the proposal `pending`**, the backend never having written the file on any such path rather than having written and put it back, and this surface renders the typed error inline on the terms of PCR-FR-15 with the modal open, the comparison still rendered, the candidate intact, and the feedback field's content untouched. This surface never states that a change was applied on the strength of anything but the backend's own success or its `acceptance_incomplete` (PCR-FR-15), both of which are past that commit point.
16. **PCR-FR-11** An acceptance is performed against the artifact's **editing session** rather than around it, and it **passes through the ordinary external-change machinery rather than waiting on it**. Three things happen in order, and the order is the requirement.
    Before the decision is invoked the session is **quiesced** (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-81), so no scheduled write of the author's own buffer races the replacement and no half-typed text is written over the accepted bytes. In the same act, **any external-change modal standing over that artifact is dismissed with neither of its resolutions taken** (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-84): the author is about to replace the whole file on purpose, so "Load from filesystem" and "Keep my version" are both questions about a buffer that is about to stop existing, and a decision the author has already made must not be held behind a dialog answering something else. For the same reason **the artifact's baseline checksum is not consulted anywhere in the decision** — not by this surface, not by the session, and not by the module that performs the write (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-13) — so a file that has moved on since the review opened is replaced rather than refused, and no baseline-checksum rejection is reachable from **Accept** by any route. An `"artifact changed externally"` event that arrives between the review opening and **Accept** being activated therefore raises no modal for this artifact while the review is showing, and neither disables nor delays either decision; what it does is update the standing line of PCR-FR-05 and nothing else.
    Then the decision is invoked, and what it applies is the **captured base's successor rather than the captured base's file**: the comparison on screen keeps the base captured at open (PCR-FR-04), and the candidate as the author last edited it is applied as a complete replacement of whatever the artifact holds at that moment (PCR-FR-10).
    Then, **on success only**, the session is **reset** to what the file now holds (per `EXC-editor-external-change.md` EXC-FR-JAWT) — the buffer replaced, the dirty indicator cleared, the undo and redo history discarded, and the checksum the acceptance returned adopted as the new baseline — so every tab bound to that artifact renders the accepted text at once, without a reload, and **no external-change dialog, prompt, or confirmation appears anywhere** for the acceptance's own write (per `../core/PST-project-storage.md` PST-FR-16). The reset is required rather than incidental: a surface that kept the buffer it held before the acceptance would go on showing the old text through the tab closing and reopening, the retained edit state outliving both (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-28).
    An acceptance that **failed** — the typed `write_failed` — resets nothing and lifts the quiesce, leaving the buffer, the dirty indicator, the undo history, and the baseline exactly as they were. That is correct rather than merely tidy: the backend never wrote the file on any failing path, the artifact write being the last fallible step of its transaction and atomic (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-14), so the session is still describing the file that is actually on disk and there is nothing for it to re-read. **No answer leaves the session quiesced**, and none leaves this surface waiting on a recovery. An external change that arrived while the review stood and was passed over on the way into a **failed** acceptance is raised as an ordinary external-change modal once the quiesce lifts, because the reason for passing over it — that the file was about to be replaced whole — did not come true.
17. **PCR-FR-12** **Reject** invokes `"decline prompt change proposal"` with the proposal and whatever the feedback field holds. On success the modal closes and **the artifact has not changed**, whether or not the author edited the candidate first — editing a candidate is not a step toward accepting it, and declining an edited one costs the project exactly what declining an untouched one costs. The modal drops the candidate buffer it held (PCR-FR-21), the candidate itself staying readable afterwards as the author last left it. Declining asks for no confirmation: it writes nothing into the project.
18. **PCR-FR-13** The feedback field is optional for both decisions and is offered for both. Whatever it holds reaches the conversation as part of the decision's own comment (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-16), appended through the same comment workflow every other comment of that conversation uses, and is not carried anywhere else. The decision itself is recorded there too and nowhere else: the discussion holds the offer and the answer in sequence, and no separate log of decisions exists.
19. **PCR-FR-14** After a decision lands, the modal dispatches on **exactly the terms any human comment in that conversation dispatches on** (per `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-LCFU, CTA-FR-QUXJ): the decision's comment is one the author wrote (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-16), so `"dispatch agent turn"` is invoked **once for each distinct agent of the conversation's active set as it stands with that comment appended**, and **not at all** where that set is empty. A decision carrying feedback that names agents is a tagged comment like any other and reaches exactly those, making them active from then on; a decision carrying none is an untagged comment and reaches whoever the author was last addressing there — which is the proposing agent wherever the author has not since turned to somebody else, and is not it where they have. There is no route by which a proposal's own agent is dispatched to for having proposed: a decision is a comment in a conversation, and a conversation reaches the people it is being held with. Nothing is dispatched **yet** where the decision's comment could not be appended — the refused decision of PCR-FR-15, and the accepted-but-incomplete one whose comment is still owed (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-14) — there being nothing for a turn to answer: the retry of PCR-FR-15 that completes the owed comment is what dispatches, on exactly these terms, from the outcome that call returns. Each dispatch names the proposal's thread with the origin kind that thread's target decides — `artifact_discussion` or `artifact_comment` (per `../core/AGC-agent-conversations.md` AGC-FR-05) — and the decision's comment as what addressed it, so the decision comment and the whole conversation are that turn's input like any other turn's, and a dispatch for an agent that was awaiting a reply there retires its awaiting turn (per `../core/AGC-agent-conversations.md` AGC-FR-28, AGC-FR-29). A dispatch that itself fails is not surfaced and does not undo the decision, and one agent's failure neither suppresses another's turn nor repeats it (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-79).
20. **PCR-FR-15** A decision the backend refuses renders its typed error inline above the foot's controls, leaving the modal open, the comparison rendered, the artifact byte-for-byte what it was, and the feedback field's content intact so the decision is retried without retyping. An acceptance refused because the target no longer resolves, or no longer resolves as a prompt (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-19), additionally renders **Accept** disabled with that reason stated, **Reject** staying enabled, because declining is the one thing still left to do with such a proposal. The surface reads the backend's own typed answer and never infers a decision from the file, so which of two sentences it says is decided by which side of the transaction's commit point the answer came from (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-14) and by nothing else. **A `write_failed` is a refusal and says the file has not changed**, which is true of every one of them because the backend never wrote the file on any failing path — its artifact write is the last fallible step of the transaction and is atomic, so a failure means it did not land (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-14). Nothing was restored, because nothing needed restoring; the surface never says a change was applied on the strength of a `write_failed`, whichever step of the transaction produced it. **An `acceptance_incomplete` is not a refusal and is stated as what it is**: the acceptance landed and its decision comment is still owed, so it states that the change **has been applied** and that the conversation has not yet been told; it never states that the file is unchanged, because it is not, and it never withdraws the acceptance, because the commit point was reached. Its retry invokes **`"complete prompt change decision"`** for that proposal (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-14) and nothing else — never a second `"apply prompt change proposal"`, which would be a second decision rather than the completion of one. On success that call returns the proposal, the comment's id, and the thread's origin kind, which is what the one dispatch of PCR-FR-14 is composed from, so the agent is asked to continue exactly as it would have been had the comment landed the first time; the modal then closes. A retry that fails again leaves the statement standing with the retry still offered, the acceptance still landed, and nothing said about the file having changed back.
    **There is no third answer, and this surface has no state for one.** The backend has no outcome meaning the file may or may not have changed and none leaving a proposal unsettled (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-14), so this modal renders no such statement, carries no undecidable state, and never disables a decision on account of one. Every answer it can receive is either a refusal that truthfully says the file has not changed, or a success — with or without its comment owed. No answer anywhere in this surface is ever rendered as "your file has not changed" when that is not true, and none is ever rendered as accepted when the acceptance did not land.
21. **PCR-FR-16** A proposal that **arrives while its artifact's Editor tab is open** opens the review on itself, with nothing for the author to activate. Four things are required of it, and each rules out an interruption nobody asked for: it must be newly seen, because a decision — or a second event about a proposal already read — is not an arrival; it must be `pending`; nothing may already be showing (PCR-FR-03); and **the artifact must have an Editor tab open to arrive in**, the subscriber being the shell's and hearing about every artifact in the worktree (PCR-FR-17). A proposal recorded against a file whose tab is closed is left to the tab's indication and to the notification of PCR-FR-18.
    An Editor tab whose artifact carries a proposal in state `pending` also renders an indication in its action cluster (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-16) saying so, which opens the review on that proposal — the way back to a review that was dismissed, and the way in to one that arrived while the tab was closed. It is present whichever editing mode the tab is in, and it clears the moment the proposal is decided. An artifact carrying no pending proposal renders no indication. There is no third kind of indication: every proposal a surface here is ever shown is `pending`, `accepted`, or `rejected`, the backend settling an interrupted acceptance to one of the three before it answers any command (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-14).
22. **PCR-FR-17** This surface mounts a subscriber to `"prompt change proposals changed"` at the **shell** rather than in the tab, and raises through `NTF-notifications.md` (NTF-FR-01) when a proposal is recorded. A tab-scoped listener would hear nothing for a file whose tab is closed, which is exactly the case the author most needs telling about. The raise carries a key identifying **that proposal** — derived from the proposal's own id, so two proposals against one artifact never share a key and one proposal's retraction never reaches another's notification — the level Info, a title naming the agent and the file, a body naming the prompt, and the project-file address of NTF-FR-03. Nothing is raised when a proposal is **decided**; that same subscriber **retracts** instead, on the terms of PCR-FR-26. The subscriber is therefore the whole of this surface's dealings with `NTF-notifications.md`: it raises on a recording, it retracts on a decision, and it reaches no notification command of its own (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-01).
23. **PCR-FR-18** Whether the raise becomes a notification the author sees is `NTF-notifications.md`'s policy and not this surface's (NTF-FR-02, NTF-FR-08): it is posted while the window is unfocused, and while the window is focused but that artifact's Editor tab is not the active tab, and it is suppressed while that tab is the active tab — the one case in which the tab has already told the author itself (PCR-FR-16). Where that artifact's Editor tab is open but not the active one, the same raise also marks it in the tab strip (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-26, NTF-FR-27). Activating the notification **opens or focuses that artifact's Editor tab** (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-17) and opens the review on the artifact's pending proposal, that being what the author was called back for. This is also the rule for every other route: a route that names a proposal whose Editor tab is not open opens or focuses that tab first and shows the review over it, so the review is never rendered anywhere but over the tab for its own file. A proposal decided before the notification is activated opens for reading on the terms of PCR-FR-JQWD.
24. **PCR-FR-19** The modal offers no way to accept part of a proposal, to stage a hunk, or to write into the base revision. A proposal is taken whole or left whole, because it is a complete document rather than a set of edits (per `../tools/PPC-propose-prompt-changes-tool.md` PPC-FR-04) — and the candidate the author edited is a complete document on exactly those terms. Outside the candidate and the feedback field, the modal accepts no text input and carries no control that writes anything but the candidate and the two decisions. **No route through it creates a file**: an acceptance replaces an artifact that exists and a decline writes nothing at all.
25. **PCR-FR-20** While the proposal is **pending**, the **candidate** is editable and the **base** revision is not, on exactly the terms `DFV-diff-viewer.md` sets for a target and an original (DFV-FR-41): in **Unified** the candidate's added and context rows and no removed row (DFV-FR-44), in **Side-by-side** the right pane alone (DFV-FR-45), in **Final** the rendered outcome (DFV-FR-46), and in **Rich** with the Editor's Markdown editing semantics, frontmatter treatment, and serialisation guarantees (DFV-FR-47) — all of them editable, none of them letting a keystroke reach the base. The candidate is the complete document rather than the changed characters within it, so unchanged text is as editable as added text; selection, typing, paste, undo, redo, and keyboard navigation behave as they do in a Diff tab (DFV-FR-50); and the comparison re-derives against the same captured base as the candidate changes (DFV-FR-43), never re-basing onto it. **The candidate's editing surface is not the artifact's editing session**: it holds its own buffer and its own undo history, and no keystroke in it reaches the artifact's buffer, its dirty state, or its write schedule.
26. **PCR-FR-21** The edited candidate is held as a **candidate buffer keyed by the proposal**, not by the modal: it survives the modal being dismissed and reopened, the Editor tab being closed and the file reopened, and the tab being backgrounded and refocused, so no route back into a review restores the agent's original text over what the author wrote. The buffer is settled by the write of PCR-FR-22 rather than being a second copy that could disagree with storage, so a review reopened after a relaunch reads the candidate from `"load prompt change proposal content"` and finds the author's edits there. It is dropped when the proposal is decided, when the project closes, and when the active worktree changes.
27. **PCR-FR-22** A candidate edit **writes itself into proposal storage**: it schedules `"save prompt change proposal candidate"` a short rest after the last keystroke, so a burst of typing is one write, and the modal presents no Save control. The write happens immediately, without waiting the rest out, when the modal is dismissed, when either decision is activated, and when the Editor tab closes. **That write is not a change to the artifact**: it touches only the proposal's candidate, creates, modifies, and deletes no file of the project, invokes neither decision operation, and leaves the artifact's buffer, dirty state, and recently-edited position exactly as they were (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-22). A write that fails keeps every edit in the buffer, renders its typed error inline above the foot's controls, and disables both decisions until it succeeds; it is retried by the next edit and by the next attempt to decide, and never on a timer. A write refused as stale because the stored candidate has moved on since this modal read it (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-24) raises an explicit conflict over the **candidate**, offering to keep what is on screen or to take what storage holds, choosing neither for the author, and disabling both decisions until one is chosen.
28. **PCR-FR-23** The modal states, in words, which of the two things has happened. While the candidate differs from the text the agent composed it carries a standing line saying the candidate has been edited **and that the file has not changed**, distinct from the write state on the toggle row, which says only whether that edit has reached proposal storage — "candidate saved" is never allowed to read as "file updated". Both are announced to assistive technology when they change, in those terms. The line clears when the candidate is edited back to the agent's own text, and the surface says the file has changed exactly once: after an acceptance succeeds.
29. **PCR-FR-24** The modal follows `"prompt change proposals changed"` while it is showing: a proposal decided from another window closes the modal's decision controls and renders the decided statement of PCR-FR-JQWD rather than leaving controls that would refuse, and any candidate edit still unwritten is dropped with them, there being nothing left to decide. It follows `"artifact changed externally"` for its own artifact quite differently and deliberately so: the event moves nothing. The captured base stays on screen, the comparison is not re-derived, the candidate is untouched, **both decisions stay enabled**, no conflict is raised, and no external-change modal is raised over the tab for as long as the review is showing (PCR-FR-11) — all the event does is put up the standing line of PCR-FR-05 saying the file has changed since the review opened and that **Accept** replaces it whole. A published file has no version history for an acceptance to record against, so there is nothing an intervening change can make ambiguous and nothing for the author to resolve before deciding.
30. **PCR-FR-25** The modal closes with its tab and with its project: closing the Editor tab, closing the project, and changing the project's active worktree (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-14) each dismiss it, and the base revision, the feedback field's content, and the scroll position do not outlive it. The **candidate does**, on the terms of PCR-FR-21, written to proposal storage before the modal goes. A decision in flight when the tab closes is not cancelled, the operation having already been invoked; its outcome reaches the conversation as the comment it appends.
31. **PCR-FR-26** After either decision, every surface that says anything about the proposal updates **without a reload and without the author refreshing anything**, and all of it follows from the one `"prompt change proposals changed"` event the decision emits and from the comment the decision appended, so nothing here polls and nothing re-reads a document. The path is one hop and is the same whichever window decided: `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` emits that event carrying the whole proposal as it now stands (PCP-FR-17); the **shell** subscriber of PCR-FR-17 — the same one that raises, mounted where it hears about every artifact rather than inside a tab — reads the proposal's `state` from the payload; and where that state is `accepted` or `rejected` it does two things and does them together.
    It **retracts the raise** it made for that proposal, through `NTF-notifications.md`'s retraction (NTF-FR-38), with the key it raised under and no other, so every posted notification and every toast for that proposal is withdrawn and every tab marking derived from it is cleared. This is not the reaching of NTF-FR-14 and does not depend on it: the author need never have opened the tab, the window need not hold focus, the notifications switch may be off, and the decision may have been taken in another window entirely — a decided proposal is settled however it was settled, and a notification still calling the author to it would call them to a review with nothing left to decide. A proposal whose raise was suppressed or whose notification was already reached retracts to a no-op (NTF-FR-38), so this surface retracts unconditionally on a decision rather than first working out what became of what it raised.
    And it **clears the proposal from the reading of PCR-FR-27**, from which the Editor tab's pending indication, the review's own state, and the control on the agent's comment in the rail all render — so the indication clears, the review closes or becomes the decided reading of PCR-FR-JQWD, the tab's editing surface renders the accepted text where the decision was an acceptance (PCR-FR-11), the control in the rail states what the proposal became (per `CMT-comments.md` CMT-FR-67), and the decision and any feedback stand in the discussion as the comment that recorded them (PCR-FR-13).
    An acceptance reported as **comment-owed** (per `../core/PCP-prompt-change-proposals.md` PCP-FR-14) is a decided proposal for every one of these purposes: its state is `accepted`, its event is emitted at the commit point rather than at the append, and it retracts and clears exactly as an acceptance whose comment landed does. What is still owed is a line in the conversation, not a decision the author has to be called back to.
32. **PCR-FR-27** Everything this surface says about a proposal it says from **one reading per artifact**: the tab's pending indication, the arrival that opens the review, and the control on the agent's comment in the rail all render from the same held reading, taken with `"list prompt change proposals"` when a surface first needs that artifact's proposals and kept current from `"prompt change proposals changed"` thereafter, so several surfaces showing one file cost one call and never disagree with one another. A reading that failed is not the session's answer: it is asked for again on the terms `CTA-comment-agent-turns.md` CTA-FR-LOOE sets, with one call in flight per artifact. No surface here reports the absence of a proposal from a reading it does not have — an artifact left with a reading that failed once would show no pending indication, open no review from any of PCR-FR-03's four routes, and hold a standing proposal that nothing in the window can decide.
33. **PCR-FR-28** The readings of PCR-FR-27 belong to the content root they were taken from, so they are **discarded when the project closes and when the active worktree changes**, together with the review standing over one (PCR-FR-25). The next surface that needs an artifact's proposals takes a fresh reading, so no indication, arrival, or control is ever rendered from the outgoing tree's.
34. **PCR-FR-29** This surface changes nothing about `DCR-draft-change-review.md`. It opens over a different tab, reads a different store, invokes different operations, and follows a different event; the two never render at once over one tab, and a draft review's behaviour is identical whether or not a prompt proposal stands anywhere in the project. What they share is the layout and interaction of PCR-FR-02 and the comparison of `DFV-diff-viewer.md` — presentation the two hold in common rather than state either keeps for the other.

## Non-functional requirements
- The modal renders its head and its toggle row before the comparison is computed, so a proposal against a large file does not leave the author looking at nothing; the comparison fills in beneath.
- The comparison is derived once per opening per mode-family and held, so switching modes is a local re-render and never a re-fetch.
- The word-level comparison is bounded per unit, so a proposal that rewrote every line of a file costs no more to render than one that rewrote a paragraph.
- Change marking is conveyed by more than colour, on the terms `DFV-diff-viewer.md` sets, so a proposal is reviewable without colour discrimination.
- Every mode soft-wraps: no surface in the modal scrolls horizontally, and the modal never widens past the bounds it holds within the tab.
- The modal, its controls, the candidate's editing surface, and the feedback field are reachable and operable by keyboard alone, and the foot's three stops are the last three of its focus order.
- The modal stays usable and legible at the minimum window size the shell supports (≥1280×800) in every one of the six mode combinations, editable candidate included.
- Editing the candidate costs one call per settled burst of typing and never an artifact write: a review the author edits for an hour leaves the file, its position in the recently-edited list, and its editing session exactly as it found them.
- Re-deriving the comparison under the author's typing is settled rather than run per keystroke, and moves neither the caret nor the scroll position.
- A raise costs the shell one subscription and nothing per artifact: a project with no proposals anywhere raises nothing and reads nothing.
- Nothing here requires network access beyond what the decision's own comment resolves.
