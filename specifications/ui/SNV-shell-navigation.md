# Shell & navigation

**Spec code:** `SNV`

## Intent
Defines the chrome and zones of the main window: where content lives, how zones resize and collapse, where the universal search bar and activity bar are anchored, and the rule that decides which zone a new surface belongs in.

## UI contract boundary
- **Owned by the UI**: zone layout, zone sizing, the vertical-panel splitter and the clamping of the panel's width fraction (SNV-FR-33–SNV-FR-37), the bottom-panel splitter and the clamping of that panel's height (SNV-FR-50–SNV-FR-53), collapse/hide state, the activity-bar icon strip with its two clusters of panel toggles and the toggle-off semantics that hide the panel a toggle governs (SNV-FR-03, SNV-FR-44–SNV-FR-46), the Documents toggle placed immediately after Project (SNV-FR-UCJH), the keyboard shortcut that toggles the bottom panel (SNV-FR-KQWD), the enablement of the History toggle against the active tab (SNV-FR-48), the tooltip every activity-bar toggle carries (SNV-FR-49), the drawing of the needs-attention emphasis on the Runs toggle whose state and lifecycle belong to `NTF-notifications.md` (SNV-FR-70), the **Selection follows tab** behaviour that moves the vertical panel to the item the newly-activated tab is a view onto — the trigger it fires on, the tab kinds it routes and those it ignores, and its silence when the target no longer resolves (SNV-FR-64–SNV-FR-69), the revealing and selecting itself being each panel's own (per `LIB-library.md` LIB-FR-18, `DRP-drafts-panel.md` DRP-FR-34, and `CHG-changes.md` CHG-FR-54), the bottom panel's active surface together with the absence of any surface switcher inside the panel itself (SNV-FR-47), the placement of the status-bar strip along the window's bottom edge below every other zone (SNV-FR-42), whose regions and controls are owned by `STB-status-bar.md`, vertical-panel side (left/right) preference, the Home affordance at the head of the tab strip and the rule that it is rendered only while no Dashboard tab is open (SNV-FR-09, SNV-FR-41), panel placement rule, main window outer size and maximized state, the main window's OS full-screen state and its restoration on mount (SNV-FR-38–SNV-FR-40), persistence of layout preferences through the backend, the native application Edit menu with standard roles (SNV-FR-14) together with its Find and Find & Replace items, whose enablement is computed client-side from the active tab and the editing surface that tab presents and which introduce no backend command (SNV-FR-43), the native application File menu with New File, New Artifact, New Folder, Save, Save All, Close Project, and Exit items (SNV-FR-23–SNV-FR-26, SNV-FR-28–SNV-FR-31) together with the placement of the two settings entries `SWN-settings-windows.md` owns and of the About entry that opens the panel `ABT-about-panel.md` owns (SNV-FR-23, SNV-FR-KXPE), with Close Project leaving OS full-screen before the Project picker is shown (SNV-FR-27) and the enablement of Save and Save All computed client-side from the unsaved state of the active tab and of the session as a whole (SNV-FR-28, SNV-FR-30), and the top chrome's trailing cluster holding the agents control and then the theme selector in that fixed order (SNV-FR-59), the theme selector itself offering light / dark / system, editing the shared user-global theme preference and applying the selection to the application root on change (SNV-FR-15 / SNV-FR-16), while the agents control's count, its roster, and everything that roster offers are owned by `AGT-agents.md`, and the top-chrome project switcher that displays the currently-open project and opens a dropdown of recent projects to switch the active project or reach the Project picker (SNV-FR-17–SNV-FR-22), and the placement of the worktree selector immediately after that switcher in the same leading cluster, followed by the refresh control that brings its branch list up to date and then the Push control that publishes the active branch (SNV-FR-32), the behaviour of the first two — label, dropdown, switching, and refresh — being owned by `WTS-worktree-selector.md` and the behaviour of the Push control being owned by `GIT-git.md`.
- **Delegated to backend (abstract)**: "load layout preferences", "save layout preferences" — payload includes the vertical panel's width as a fraction of the shell's inner width, the bottom panel's height, the bottom panel's active surface, panel side, collapse/hide state, and main window outer size + maximized state; these are persisted per project rather than per worktree (per `../core/PSS-project-settings-storage.md` PSS-FR-06), so the chrome keeps its shape when the active worktree changes. Stubs may persist to in-memory state during the walking skeleton. The shell additionally uses "load app preferences" and "save app preferences" (shared with `GLS-global-settings.md` and defined by `../core/GSS-global-settings-storage.md`) for the two user-global facts it owns: the theme preference behind the top-chrome theme selector, and the main window's OS full-screen state (SNV-FR-38). Both live in one record, so a write of either carries the other through unchanged. The shell **reads** one further field of that same record without ever writing it — the **Selection follows tab** preference of SNV-FR-64, presented by the Global settings Navigation section (`GLS-global-settings.md` GLS-FR-28) and defined by `../core/GSS-global-settings-storage.md` GSS-FR-33 — and the panel selection that preference drives writes no preference of its own, the vertical panel's active surface being no part of layout preferences (SNV-FR-08). The top-chrome project switcher additionally uses "list recent projects" (shared with `GLS-global-settings.md` and `PPK-project-picker.md`, defined by `../core/GSS-global-settings-storage.md` GSS-FR-05) to populate its list and "open project at path" (defined by `../core/PST-project-storage.md` PST-FR-01) to switch the active project; no new backend command is introduced. The File menu introduces no new backend command of its own. Its About item opens the in-window panel of `ABT-about-panel.md`, which introduces none either. Its two settings items open the child windows of `SWN-settings-windows.md`, which introduce none either, and its three creation items all hand off to modal surfaces rather than to a tab: New File hands off to the modal owned by `NFI-new-file.md`, whose `"create file (location, name)"` operation is defined by `../core/PST-project-storage.md` (PST-FR-25), New Artifact hands off to the modal owned by `NTA-new-typed-artifact.md`, whose `"create typed file (location, name, artifact type)"` operation is defined by `../core/PST-project-storage.md` (PST-FR-29), and New Folder hands off to the modal owned by `NFW-new-folder.md`, whose `"create folder (location, name, artifact type)"` operation is defined by `../core/PST-project-storage.md` (PST-FR-24); Close Project drives the existing project-close transition back to the Project picker (`OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-02) and its in-memory/watcher teardown (`../core/PST-project-storage.md` PST-FR-14, `../core/ASC-artifact-scanning.md` ASC-FR-14); and Exit uses the platform-native application-quit role. Both first flush the pending changes of the artifacts and Flows edited in this session through `"save artifact contents"` (owned by `../core/PST-project-storage.md` PST-FR-15 and driven by `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-33 and `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-29) and the pending changes of every edited draft file through `"save draft file contents"` (driven by `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-13, and owned with the rest of the draft surface by `../core/DRS-draft-storage.md`), neither of which introduces a command of its own. No item of this menu creates a draft or invokes `"create draft (name)"`: a draft is created in the Drafts panel alone (per `DRP-drafts-panel.md` DRP-FR-06, DRP-FR-26), and the New Artifact tabs this menu writes through are ones that panel opened. Save and Save All are likewise pure orchestration over those same writes — for an artifact in an Editor tab (driven by `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-34 and EDT-FR-35), for a Flow in a Flow tab (driven by `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-27), and for a draft file in a New Artifact tab (driven by `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-13) — and introduce no command of their own.

## Functional requirements
1. **SNV-FR-01** The main window contains a top chrome strip hosting the universal search bar; the search bar is always visible while the main window is mounted.
2. **SNV-FR-02** The main viewport occupies the dominant center area and renders the active tab.
3. **SNV-FR-03** The activity bar is a vertical icon strip on the same edge as the vertical panel; it contains one toggle button per panel surface — vertical-panel and bottom-panel alike, arranged in the two clusters of SNV-FR-44 — and nothing else. The Global settings and Project settings entry points are not part of it — they live in the status bar's leading region and in the application menu (per `STB-status-bar.md` STB-FR-04, `SWN-settings-windows.md` SWN-FR-13, and `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-07).
4. **SNV-FR-04** The vertical panel renders exactly one panel surface at a time, selected by the activity-bar toggle.
5. **SNV-FR-05** The vertical panel can be (a) at full width, (b) collapsed to icon-only (activity bar visible, panel content hidden), or (c) fully hidden (activity bar still visible).
6. **SNV-FR-06** The vertical panel position (left or right edge) is a user preference; switching sides flips both the activity bar and panel content together.
7. **SNV-FR-07** The bottom panel hosts wide line-shaped content (Runs, the session's diagnostic Logs, Git, History version list); it is resizable in height (SNV-FR-50–SNV-FR-53), collapsible, and hideable. It renders exactly one of those surfaces at a time, selected by the activity bar's bottom-panel cluster (SNV-FR-44).
8. **SNV-FR-08** The vertical panel's width fraction, the height of the bottom panel, the bottom panel's active surface, panel side, collapse/hide state of each panel, the main window's outer dimensions and maximized state, the split ratio each draft's discussion was last read at (per `DDS-draft-discussion.md` DDS-FR-PNXR), and whether each draft's discussion column is hidden (per `DDS-draft-discussion.md` DDS-FR-XQMF) are persisted per project and restored across sessions. The main window's OS full-screen state is not among them; it is persisted separately as a user-global preference (SNV-FR-38).
9. **SNV-FR-09** A Home affordance occupies the leading position of the tab strip and is rendered only while no Dashboard tab is open — a state reachable only while other tabs are open, since the strip never empties (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-15) — standing in the place the Dashboard tab itself occupies when open (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-08). It is a control of the strip rather than a tab — it carries no label, no close control, and no content of its own — so the strip presents the Dashboard exactly once: as the affordance, or as the tab, never as both.
10. **SNV-FR-10** Panel placement rule: tree-shaped or short-item-shaped content lives in the vertical panel; wide line-shaped content lives in the bottom panel. This rule is asserted as a design invariant; no UI control negotiates placement per surface.
11. **SNV-FR-11** On the first launch after install (no persisted main-window state), the main window opens maximized to the available work area of the display.
12. **SNV-FR-12** When the user resizes or unmaximizes the main window, the new outer dimensions and maximized state are persisted; the next launch restores the window to the persisted dimensions and maximized state, not to the maximized default.
13. **SNV-FR-13** Persisted main-window dimensions are clamped on restore to remain within the current display's work area; if the persisted geometry no longer fits any available display, the window falls back to maximized.
14. **SNV-FR-14** The app provides a native application Edit menu with standard roles — Undo, Redo, Cut, Copy, Paste, Select All — bound to the platform-standard accelerators (e.g. ⌘Z / ⇧⌘Z / ⌘X / ⌘C / ⌘V / ⌘A on macOS). Cut, Copy, Paste, and Select All dispatch to the focused surface, so those shortcuts work in the Editor and in other text inputs without each surface re-binding them. Undo and Redo dispatch to the focused surface everywhere except within an Editor tab, where the Editor claims them and applies them to the open artifact's own edit history, which spans both of its editing modes and outlives the tab (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-15, EDT-FR-22 and EDT-FR-28). The menu also carries the Find and Find & Replace items of SNV-FR-43.
15. **SNV-FR-15** The top chrome hosts a theme selector offering exactly three mutually-exclusive choices — light, dark, system — mirroring the Global settings Appearance selector (`GLS-global-settings.md` GLS-FR-04); both controls read and write a single shared user-global theme preference, and the chrome selector initialises from and continuously reflects that preference (via "load app preferences"), including when the preference was last changed from Global settings.
16. **SNV-FR-16** Choosing a theme in the top-chrome selector applies it to the application root immediately with no relaunch and persists it via "save app preferences"; app-wide application of the value and the behaviour of "system" are governed by `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-09 / OVW-FR-10, identically to a selection made in Global settings (`GLS-global-settings.md` GLS-FR-05).
17. **SNV-FR-17** The top chrome hosts a project switcher control at the leading edge, adjacent to the app brand, that displays the name of the currently-open project and, when activated, opens a dropdown of recent projects.
18. **SNV-FR-18** The switcher dropdown is populated from "list recent projects" (`../core/GSS-global-settings-storage.md` GSS-FR-05), honouring that operation's pinned-first / most-recent-first ordering and its missing/pruning semantics; the switcher does not re-sort or re-filter the list.
19. **SNV-FR-19** Selecting a dropdown entry other than the currently-open project switches the active project to it by invoking "open project at path" (`../core/PST-project-storage.md` PST-FR-01) with that entry's path; the resulting in-window teardown and reopen are governed by `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-11.
20. **SNV-FR-20** The currently-open project appears in the dropdown flagged as current and is non-actionable: clicking it neither re-opens the project nor tears down the window, and it counts toward the visible-row budget of SNV-FR-21.
21. **SNV-FR-21** The dropdown's recent-entries region displays at most five rows at once (counting the current-project row); when more entries exist, the region scrolls vertically. No region of the dropdown scrolls horizontally — entry labels wider than the available width are truncated (e.g. with an ellipsis) rather than introducing a horizontal scrollbar.
22. **SNV-FR-22** The dropdown provides an "Open Another Project…" action, presented outside the scrolling recent-entries region, that returns to the Project picker (per `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-11 and `PPK-project-picker.md`).
23. **SNV-FR-23** The app provides a native application File menu, sibling to the Edit menu of SNV-FR-14, containing exactly these items in this order: New File, New Artifact, New Folder, Save, Save All, Global settings, Project settings, Close Project, a separator, About, Exit. The three creation items lead the menu in the same order the Project panel's folder context menu presents them (`LCM-library-context-menu.md` LCM-FR-01). The two settings items and **About** sit in this menu on every platform but **macOS**, where the app-name menu carries them instead — Global settings, Project settings, a separator, About, Quit — and this menu holds the seven items around them. A separator precedes About, and About sits immediately before the quit item. The labels, their order, availability against whether a project is open, and windows of the two settings items are owned by `SWN-settings-windows.md` (SWN-FR-14 through SWN-FR-16), and this spec places them. The panel About opens is owned by `ABT-about-panel.md`. The menu is available whenever the main window is mounted (a project is open); what the application menu offers while the Project picker is the only window is `PPK-project-picker.md` PPK-FR-15's.
24. **SNV-FR-24** New File, New Artifact, and New Folder are entry points that initiate, respectively, creation of a plain file, a typed artifact file, and a folder in the currently-open project, and each hands off to the modal surface that owns that experience: **New File** opens the New File window (`NFI-new-file.md`), which owns selecting the location and the name — extension included — together with its creation operation; **New Artifact** opens the New Artifact window (`NTA-new-typed-artifact.md`), which owns selecting the location, the name, and the artifact type together with its creation operation; and **New Folder** opens the New Folder window (`NFW-new-folder.md`), which owns selecting the parent folder, the name, and the optional artifact type together with its own creation operation. All three open a modal and each defaults its location to the project root, which the user may change (per `NFI-new-file.md` NFI-FR-06, `NTA-new-typed-artifact.md` NTA-FR-07, and `NFW-new-folder.md` NFW-FR-06). **None of the three creates a draft**: a draft is created in the Drafts panel alone (per `DRP-drafts-panel.md` DRP-FR-06, DRP-FR-26), and this menu opens no New Artifact tab. This spec requires only that all three items are present and that activating each opens its window.
25. **SNV-FR-25** Close Project closes the currently-open project and returns to the Project picker, tearing down the current main window and its tabs and panel state (per `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-02), and unmounting the project's in-memory state and filesystem watcher (per `../core/PST-project-storage.md` PST-FR-14 and `../core/ASC-artifact-scanning.md` ASC-FR-14). The pending changes of the artifacts and Flows edited in this session are written first (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-33 and `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-29), and a write that cannot proceed safely cancels the close and leaves the project open (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-32); apart from those writes, the close modifies no file under the project. The retained edit state of every artifact in the project, undo histories included, is discarded with the teardown (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-28).
26. **SNV-FR-26** Exit quits the entire application via the platform-native application-quit role, bound to the platform-standard accelerator (e.g. ⌘Q on macOS); it does not merely return to the Project picker. Pending Editor and Flow changes are written before the application quits, and a write that cannot proceed safely cancels the quit (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-32, EDT-FR-33 and `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-29).
27. **SNV-FR-27** When the main window returns to the Project picker — through Close Project (SNV-FR-25) or through the switcher's "Open Another Project…" action (SNV-FR-22) — while it is in OS full-screen (the platform's native full-screen mode, distinct from a maximized window), the window leaves full-screen as part of that transition, so the Project picker is presented as a normal centered window (per `PPK-project-picker.md` PPK-FR-09 / PPK-FR-12) and never within a full-screen space. The transition does not rewrite the persisted full-screen preference (SNV-FR-38); it records how the picker is presented, not a decision by the user to stop working full-screen.
28. **SNV-FR-28** Save acts on the active main-viewport tab and is bound to the platform-standard save accelerator (⌘S on macOS). Every tab that owns content writes that content itself, so Save brings a write the tab has already scheduled forward rather than starting one. It is enabled only while the active tab holds a write that has not yet landed — an Editor tab whose artifact is dirty (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-04), a **Diff tab** whose target revision is dirty, that target being the same artifact's editing session (per `DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-05, DFV-FR-42), a Flow tab whose Flow is dirty (per `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-26), or a New Artifact tab whose selected draft file is dirty (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-13). It is disabled and rendered greyed-out in every other case: when the active tab is of a type that owns no savable content (Dashboard, Search results, History detail), and when the active Editor, Diff, Flow, or New Artifact tab has nothing outstanding to write. A settings window is no tab and is never what Save acts on: what it holds is written by the window itself (per `SWN-settings-windows.md` SWN-FR-08), and while one is open this menu takes no interaction at all (SWN-FR-02). While Save is disabled the accelerator does nothing.
29. **SNV-FR-29** Activating Save writes exactly the active tab's content and nothing else: an Editor tab writes its artifact (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-34), a Flow tab writes its Flow (per `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-27), and a New Artifact tab writes its selected draft file (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-13). Each performs the write it had scheduled, exactly once, and the schedule is spent rather than firing again afterwards. Unsaved changes held by any other tab or by any artifact without an open tab are left untouched.
30. **SNV-FR-30** Save All is bound to ⇧⌘S and is enabled whenever at least one artifact, Flow, or draft file in the open project holds a write that has not yet landed, whether or not it has an open tab; when nothing anywhere is outstanding it is disabled and rendered greyed-out, and the accelerator does nothing. Its enablement does not depend on which tab is active.
31. **SNV-FR-31** Activating Save All brings forward the pending write of every artifact holding unsaved changes (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-35), every Flow holding unsaved changes (per `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-27), and every draft file holding unsaved changes (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-13), in one sweep. A write that cannot proceed safely does not stop the sweep: the remaining writes still complete and the blocked content is surfaced for the user to resolve (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-36).
32. **SNV-FR-32** The top chrome's leading cluster hosts, in fixed order after the project switcher (SNV-FR-17) and before the universal search bar (SNV-FR-01), the worktree selector, the refresh control that brings its list of worktrees and branches up to date, the **Push** control that publishes the active branch's commits, and the work stream selector (`WSS-work-stream-selector.md` WSS-FR-JVUF). The controls read as one group — the project switcher names which repository is open, the worktree selector names which of its checkouts is active, the refresh control says whether that list is current, and the Push control hands the branch's commits to its remote — and the selector, the refresh control, the Push control, and the work stream selector are all present only while the open project's content root sits inside a Git repository (per `WTS-worktree-selector.md` WTS-FR-02 and WTS-FR-29, `GIT-git.md` GIT-FR-XXLE, and `WSS-work-stream-selector.md` WSS-FR-JVUF), so outside a repository the switcher is followed directly by the search bar. The selector's label, dropdown, switching, and refresh behaviour are owned by `WTS-worktree-selector.md`, the Push control's behaviour is owned by `GIT-git.md`, and this spec places them.
33. **SNV-FR-33** The vertical panel is resized by dragging a splitter that runs the full height of its inner edge — the boundary between the panel and the main viewport. The splitter presents a horizontal-resize cursor on hover and moves the boundary live as the pointer is dragged, so the panel's new width is visible throughout the drag rather than only on release.
34. **SNV-FR-34** The vertical panel's width is held as a fraction of the shell's inner width (the window's content width less the activity bar). The fraction is clamped so the panel is never narrower than 180 px and never wider than 50% of the shell's inner width; a drag that would pass either bound stops at it. When the shell is narrow enough that the 180 px floor exceeds the 50% ceiling, the floor wins and the panel occupies it.
35. **SNV-FR-35** The width fraction is invariant under a change of window size: resizing the main window, maximizing it, entering or leaving full-screen, and restoring it at a different size on a different display all preserve the fraction, so the panel keeps the same proportion of the shell and the main viewport absorbs the difference. The clamps of SNV-FR-34 are re-applied at each new size.
36. **SNV-FR-36** A resize is persisted via "save layout preferences" when the drag ends, not continuously while the pointer moves. The fraction restored on the next mount is the one in effect at the last completed drag.
37. **SNV-FR-37** The splitter is present and draggable only while the vertical panel renders content at full width; it is absent when the panel is collapsed to icon-only or fully hidden (SNV-FR-05). Collapsing or hiding the panel leaves the persisted fraction untouched, so restoring the panel returns it to exactly the width it had.
38. **SNV-FR-38** The main window's OS full-screen state is a user-global preference, persisted via "save app preferences" whenever the window enters or leaves full-screen. It sits outside layout preferences and does not vary by project: it describes how the user wants the main window presented, whichever project is open.
39. **SNV-FR-39** When the main window mounts, it enters OS full-screen if the persisted preference is set (per `../core/GSS-global-settings-storage.md` GSS-FR-19). The project's persisted geometry is restored underneath it: the outer dimensions and maximized state of SNV-FR-12, clamped per SNV-FR-13, are what the window is presented at when the user leaves full-screen.
40. **SNV-FR-40** The persisted full-screen preference is read only when the main window mounts. The Project picker never applies it and is never presented in OS full-screen (per `PPK-project-picker.md` PPK-FR-14). Switching the active project from within the main window (per `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-11) leaves the window full-screen, because the preference is user-global and the reopened main window mounts with it still set.
41. **SNV-FR-41** Activating the Home affordance opens a Dashboard tab in the leading position of the tab strip and focuses it; the affordance is no longer rendered once that tab exists.
42. **SNV-FR-42** The main window's bottom edge is occupied by the status bar (`STB-status-bar.md`), a fixed-height strip spanning the full window width below every other zone: the activity bar, vertical panel, bottom panel, and main viewport all terminate above it, and the vertical-panel splitter (SNV-FR-33) runs the height of the shell above it rather than alongside it. The strip is always present while the main window is mounted and is not resizable, collapsible, or hideable, so it contributes no field to layout preferences (SNV-FR-08) and its height is not a persisted value.
43. **SNV-FR-43** The Edit menu carries **Find** and **Find & Replace** items below Select All, bound to the platform-standard accelerators — ⌘F and ⌘R on macOS, Ctrl+F and Ctrl+R on Windows and Linux. Both are scoped to the tabs that present a Markdown editing surface with the two find panels above it: they are enabled while the active main-viewport tab is an Editor tab, and while it is a New Artifact tab showing its live prompt (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-11, NAW-FR-57), the two tabs carrying one editing surface with one set of panels rather than a surface each. They are disabled and rendered greyed-out in every other case — a New Artifact tab reading a past version, a New Artifact tab whose draft is read-only (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-09, NAW-FR-44, NAW-FR-59), and every other kind of tab, a Flow tab included (per `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-13). Activating either does exactly what its accelerator does in the active tab — opening, switching, or closing the corresponding panel of that tab's own editing surface (per `EFR-editor-find-replace.md` EFR-FR-AYNZ and EFR-FR-BJUY) — so the menu drives the panel behaviour the tab already carries and adds no command path of its own for any kind of tab. While the items are disabled the accelerators do nothing, so ⌘F and ⌘R never reach the universal search bar or any other surface.
44. **SNV-FR-44** The activity bar is organised into two clusters separated by flexible space, so each stays anchored to its own end of the strip however tall the window is. The **leading cluster**, flush to the top of the strip, holds one toggle per vertical-panel surface — Project, Documents, Notes, Comments, Drafts, Changes, in that order, so the surfaces describing the project's own content sit above the two describing its Git state and Changes meets the Git toggle across the gap. The **trailing cluster**, flush to the foot of the strip immediately above the status bar (SNV-FR-42), holds one toggle per bottom-panel surface — Runs, Logs, History, Git, in that order, so the two surfaces that stream lines as they are produced sit together at the head of the cluster and Git sits at the very foot of the strip beside the status bar that reports its branch, staging, and sync state (per `STB-status-bar.md`). Which zone a toggle drives is what decides its cluster, so a new panel surface joins the cluster of the zone it renders in. Both clusters sit on the same edge as the vertical panel and move with it when the panel side changes (SNV-FR-06).
45. **SNV-FR-45** Every vertical-panel toggle acts as a toggle rather than a radio button. Activating the toggle of a surface that is not the active one makes it the active surface, opening the vertical panel at its persisted width if it was hidden. Activating the toggle of the surface the panel is currently rendering hides the panel — state (c) of SNV-FR-05 — leaving the activity bar in place. While the panel is hidden no toggle in the leading cluster is marked active, and the panel's selected surface and persisted width fraction are both untouched, so activating that same toggle again returns the panel to the same surface at exactly the same width (SNV-FR-37).
46. **SNV-FR-46** Every bottom-panel toggle acts as a toggle on the same terms. Activating the toggle of a bottom surface that is not the active one makes it the active surface, opening the bottom panel at its persisted height if it was hidden, and leaves the vertical panel untouched. Activating the toggle of the surface the bottom panel is currently rendering hides the bottom panel. While the bottom panel is hidden no toggle in the trailing cluster is marked active.
47. **SNV-FR-47** The bottom panel presents no surface switcher of its own: the activity bar's trailing cluster is the only control that chooses between Runs, Logs, Git, and History. The panel keeps its own hide control, which hides it exactly as re-activating the active surface's toggle does (SNV-FR-46) and as the keyboard shortcut of SNV-FR-KQWD does. A surface reached programmatically rather than from the strip — a Dashboard widget (per `DSH-dashboard.md` DSH-FR-06), a Search result (per `SCH-search.md` SCH-FR-08), a History version list opened for the active artifact (per `HVW-history-viewer.md` HVW-FR-01), or an activated notification whose address names a bottom surface (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-17) — opens the bottom panel on that surface and marks its toggle active, so the strip always names where the panel is.
48. **SNV-FR-48** The History toggle is enabled only while the active main-viewport tab is bound to an artifact — an Editor or Flow tab — because that tab is what the version list describes (per `HVW-history-viewer.md` HVW-FR-01). In every other case it is disabled and rendered greyed-out, and activating it does nothing. A disabled History toggle still reflects whether History is the bottom panel's active surface: when the active tab changes to one with no artifact behind it while History is showing, the panel stays on History with an empty version list and the toggle greys out while remaining marked active; focusing an artifact-bound tab re-enables it and repopulates the list.
49. **SNV-FR-49** Every activity-bar toggle carries a tooltip naming its surface — Project, Documents, Notes, Comments, Drafts, Changes, Runs, Logs, History, Git. The tooltip is rendered by the application in the current theme, appears after a short hover delay and immediately on keyboard focus, and is dismissed on pointer-leave, blur, Escape, or activation of the toggle. It is positioned on the panel side of the strip — to the strip's inner edge, vertically centred on its button — so it never overhangs the window edge whichever side the panel is on (SNV-FR-06). Each toggle also carries an accessible name equal to its tooltip text, so the label is available without hovering. A disabled toggle shows its tooltip like any other, and the History toggle's tooltip additionally states that an open artifact is required while it is disabled (SNV-FR-48).
50. **SNV-FR-50** The bottom panel is resized by dragging a splitter that runs the full width of its top edge — the boundary between the main viewport and the panel. The splitter presents a vertical-resize cursor on hover and moves the boundary live as the pointer is dragged, so the panel's new height is visible throughout the drag rather than only on release. Dragging upward makes the panel taller and the viewport shorter.
51. **SNV-FR-51** The bottom panel's height is held in pixels and clamped so the panel is never shorter than 120 px and never taller than 75% of the shell's height — the window's content height less the status-bar strip (SNV-FR-42). A drag that would pass either bound stops at it. When the shell is short enough that the 120 px floor exceeds the 75% ceiling, the floor wins, on the same terms the vertical panel's floor does (SNV-FR-34): a panel below its floor cannot render a line of output legibly, where one that takes most of a very short shell is merely cramped.
52. **SNV-FR-52** The clamps of SNV-FR-51 are re-applied whenever the shell's height changes — a window resize, maximize, or entering or leaving full-screen — so a panel sized against a tall window is brought back under the 75% ceiling on a short one rather than crowding the viewport out. The stored height is left as the user set it, so restoring the taller window returns the panel to it.
53. **SNV-FR-53** A resize is persisted via "save layout preferences" when the drag ends, not continuously while the pointer moves; the height restored on the next mount is the one in effect at the last completed drag, clamped per SNV-FR-51 against the shell it mounts into. The splitter is present and draggable only while the bottom panel is visible; hiding the panel (SNV-FR-46) leaves the persisted height untouched, so reopening returns it to exactly the height it had.
54. **SNV-FR-54** A toggle marked active (SNV-FR-45, SNV-FR-46) is distinguished from an inactive one by more than the shade of its icon: it carries a filled accent tint behind the icon together with an accent rail along the strip's outer edge beside it, so which panels are open is readable at a glance across a strip of nine icons without hovering any of them or comparing them to each other. A disabled toggle that is nonetheless marked active (SNV-FR-48) keeps that marking at reduced contrast rather than losing it, because it still names where the panel is; hovering it restores nothing, since there is nothing to activate.
55. **SNV-FR-55** The activity bar spans the full height of the shell — from beneath the top chrome to the top of the status-bar strip (SNV-FR-42) — and the bottom panel begins at the strip's inner edge rather than beneath it. Opening, resizing, or hiding the bottom panel therefore changes the height of the vertical panel and the main viewport, which both terminate at that panel's top edge, and never the height of the strip. This is what keeps the trailing cluster of toggles pinned to the foot of the window (SNV-FR-44): the control that opens the bottom panel does not move when the panel opens.
56. **SNV-FR-56** The main window's **floating overlays** are mutually exclusive: at most one is mounted at a time, and opening one closes any other that is open rather than coexisting with it. This governs the window's modal action surfaces, its chrome-anchored dropdowns, and its context menus alike — the New File, New Folder, commit-message, and GitHub-token-picker windows, the Project panel's tree context menu together with the recursive-delete confirmation that menu may raise (per `LCM-library-context-menu.md` LCM-FR-01, LCM-FR-11), the rollback confirmation the Changes panel raises (per `CHG-changes.md` CHG-FR-59), the tab strip's tab context menu (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-32), the project switcher's dropdown (SNV-FR-17), the worktree selector's dropdown and its New worktree dialog (per `WTS-worktree-selector.md` WTS-FR-05), the work stream selector's dropdown, its New stream dialog, the stream update window and the update resolution window it opens (per `WSS-work-stream-selector.md` WSS-FR-XZRD, WSS-FR-NLXD, WSS-FR-KDVU), the Create a PR window that the work stream selector, the Changes panel, and the Git panel's PRs section open (per `CPR-create-pull-request.md` CPR-FR-IWDK), the agents roster the top chrome's agents control opens (SNV-FR-59, per `AGT-agents.md` AGT-FR-03), the status bar's in-flight operations overlay (per `STB-status-bar.md` STB-FR-12), and the About panel the application menu opens (per `ABT-about-panel.md` ABT-FR-GCPK). A tab is not an overlay, and neither is a region belonging to one: the main viewport's tabs, every panel surface, the find panels above an editing surface (SNV-FR-43), a tab's comment rail, and the floating action control every tab carries with the composer and the discussion panel it opens (per `ACT-action-control.md` ACT-FR-11) neither close an overlay nor are closed by one. A discussion is never a floating overlay: it is read in its owner's surface or in a conversation tab, both of which are tab regions that neither close an overlay nor are closed by one (per `CVP-conversation-presentation.md`). A settings window is outside the rule too, being a window of its own rather than a surface of this one (per `SWN-settings-windows.md` SWN-FR-01): opening one closes no overlay and an overlay opening closes none of it, and while one is open this window accepts no interaction at all (SWN-FR-02). Activity-bar tooltips are likewise outside the rule, being transient hover affordances that take no focus, and so is the notification toast stack (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-21), which takes no focus and intercepts no pointer event outside the toasts either, and which also shows the toast for an address that cannot be reached (NTF-FR-20), and so is the pull request notice that a created pull request leaves (per `CPR-create-pull-request.md` CPR-FR-RDJP), which opens no window and closes none.
57. **SNV-FR-57** A file's name and a project-relative path render in exactly the case they carry on disk, in every surface of the main window that shows one — a panel header, a group header, a tab's own header, a row, or a status line. A display treatment never case-transforms them. The micro-labels those surfaces set beside them — a panel's name, a section's name, a state's name — are typographic labels rather than content and carry their own casing treatment, so a header pairing the two shows the label transformed and the filename untouched. A path is the name the user goes looking for on disk, and a window that spells the same file two ways makes them look twice.
58. **SNV-FR-58** A vertical-panel surface offering both a text filter and one or more scope-, mode-, or lens selectors arranges them in one order across every such surface: the text filter first, directly beneath the panel's header, and the selectors beneath it, broadest first, so the narrowest of them sits immediately above the list it narrows. Every one of them is pinned above the scrollable list and stays visible while it scrolls. A surface offering only one of the two renders it in the position it would occupy in the pair, so a surface carrying a text filter alone still sits directly above its list.
59. **SNV-FR-59** The top chrome's **trailing cluster** holds, in fixed order after the universal search bar (SNV-FR-01), the agents control and then the theme selector (SNV-FR-15). The two are grouped by what they are about rather than by what they do: the leading cluster names what is open — the project, its worktree, and the freshness of that list (SNV-FR-32) — while the trailing cluster holds the two things that belong to the author rather than to the repository, who they can talk to and how the window looks. The agents control is present whenever a project is open, whether or not that project has enrolled anyone; its count, its roster, and everything that roster offers are owned by `AGT-agents.md` (AGT-FR-02 through AGT-FR-08), and this spec places it.
60. **SNV-FR-60** A vertical-panel surface with nothing at all to list renders its **first-class empty state** as one block in place of the list, composed in the same fixed order across every such surface: a short line stating what the surface is empty of, a sentence naming what the absent thing is, and at most one action — that surface's own primary affordance, rendered as a button. The block is centred both across the panel's width and down the region the list would have occupied, and its sentence wraps on a measure narrower than the panel rather than running the panel's full width, so an empty panel reads as a state that was designed rather than as a run of text left in its top-left corner. The selector and filter controls of SNV-FR-58 are not rendered beside it, because there is nothing for them to narrow. A surface with nothing to offer in the state — a read-only one, or one whose action would mean nothing there — renders the block without an action rather than with a disabled one. The block occupies the list's region alone; what a surface pins below its list is governed by that surface.
61. **SNV-FR-61** A list narrowed to nothing by a filter, lens, or scope selector is **not** the empty state of SNV-FR-60: it renders within the list's own region, with every selector and filter control still present and still holding what the author typed or picked. Every surface tells the two apart, because they call for different things — one says nothing of the kind exists yet and offers the way to make one, the other says the controls already in the author's hands admit nothing and leaves them there to be changed.
62. **SNV-FR-62** Every selector SNV-FR-58 places renders as one row of **toggle buttons behaving as a single radio group**: one button per position, exactly one active at any moment, and activating a button making its position the active one. There is no dropdown to open — every position the row renders is one activation away. Each button carries its position's name as a short text tag, set in the same treatment a panel's rows give an artifact-type tag so the control and the content it filters read as one vocabulary, and discloses that position's full name in a tooltip on hover and immediately on keyboard focus, so a name shortened to fit the row is still readable in full.
63. **SNV-FR-63** A selector row occupies exactly one line and neither wraps nor scrolls. Where the panel is too narrow to hold every button, the row is **clipped at its trailing edge**, and widening the panel (SNV-FR-33) is what reaches the rest. Its leading button, its trailing button, and whichever button is currently active are exempt and render at every width, so the two positions that bound what the selector can admit and the one naming what it admits right now are never what a narrow panel takes away.
64. **SNV-FR-64** While the user-global **Selection follows tab** preference is on (`GLS-global-settings.md` GLS-FR-28, `../core/GSS-global-settings-storage.md` GSS-FR-33), a qualifying change of the main viewport's active tab (SNV-FR-65) makes the vertical panel show the item that tab is a view onto: the panel that item belongs to becomes the vertical panel's active surface, opening the panel at its persisted width if it was hidden, exactly as activating that surface's own activity-bar toggle does (SNV-FR-45); and that panel then reveals and selects the item (SNV-FR-66). The two halves are the panel navigation the author could have performed themselves followed by the reveal that panel already offers every other caller, so a tab change costs the author the walk down the tree they would otherwise make to find where they are.
65. **SNV-FR-65** The trigger is an **activation** of a tab that is not already the active one, and nothing else. It fires however the activation was made — a click on the tab, keyboard navigation across the strip, a focus jump from a surface that opened or re-reached the tab (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-05), or an activated notification landing on a file or draft (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-17) — because each of them is the author arriving at a different tab. None of these is a trigger: activating the tab that is already active; the main window gaining or losing OS focus, and OS focus entering a tab; activating a panel or a panel's toggle, which changes no tab; anything happening inside the active tab, its content, dirty state, or mode included; the tab the viewport mounts on when a project opens, when the active project is switched, or when the active worktree changes (per `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-04, OVW-FR-11, OVW-FR-12), none of which is a transition from one active tab to another; and focus landing on a different tab because a tab was **removed** — the author closing the active tab, the automatic closures of `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-19 and TAB-FR-22, and the Dashboard that opens when the strip would empty (TAB-FR-15). A tab going away is a consequence rather than a navigation, and moving the panel on the author's behalf while they are committing or deleting is exactly the wrong moment to do it.
66. **SNV-FR-66** Which panel a tab selects in is decided by the kind of tab and by the identity that tab already carries. A **New Artifact** tab selects in the **Drafts** panel, by the id of the draft it is bound to (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-17, `DRP-drafts-panel.md` DRP-FR-34) — the id rather than the draft's display name or the path of its prompt, so a draft renamed or moved is still the row that is selected. An **Editor** tab and a **Flow** tab both select in the **Project** panel, by the tab's own file (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-04, `LIB-library.md` LIB-FR-18). A **Diff** tab selects in the **Changes** panel, by the file half of the pair identifying it (per `DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-04, `CHG-changes.md` CHG-FR-54). Every other kind of tab selects nowhere and changes nothing: a Dashboard, Search results, History detail, conversation, Map (per `SMP-specification-map.md`), Document, or PDF Viewer tab (per `TAB-tabs.md` TAB-FR-LKCT) has no item in any vertical panel to name, and activating one leaves the panel showing whatever it was showing, selection included.
67. **SNV-FR-67** A target that cannot be resolved changes nothing at all. Where the tab's file or draft is no longer in the panel it would be selected in — removed, graduated, or belonging to a content root the panel is no longer reading after a project or worktree transition — the vertical panel keeps its active surface and its selection exactly as they were, is neither opened nor hidden, and nothing is reported: there is no question the author asked that an error would answer. No substitute is ever selected in the target's place, then or later: a tree reload, a change-set refresh, or a `"project tree changed"` event arriving afterwards re-renders its panel on its own terms and selects nothing on account of this rule.
68. **SNV-FR-68** The selection is performed once per qualifying activation and is never re-applied. Once it has landed the panel is the author's again: they may select another item, change that panel's filters, expand or collapse nodes, scroll, or switch to another panel entirely, and nothing — no watcher event, no re-render, no window or tab focus change, no return to a tab that is already active — restores the tab's own item over what they did. The next selection happens at the next qualifying activation and at no other moment.
69. **SNV-FR-69** While the preference is off, a change of the active tab does nothing to the vertical panel: its active surface, its selection, its expansion, its filters, its scroll position, and its focus are all exactly what they would be had the tab never changed, and every panel behaves as it does for an author who has never turned this on. Changing the preference is not itself a navigation either — turning it on selects nothing and switches no panel, and turning it off undoes no selection already made — so it takes effect at the next qualifying activation rather than at the moment it is set.
70. **SNV-FR-70** The activity bar's **Runs toggle** renders the needs-attention emphasis carried by the graduation runs it is the way to (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-39), the strip drawing what that facility holds rather than deciding any of it: the toggle tracks no attention state of its own and reads no run record. The emphasis is a mark on the toggle together with the accessible needs-attention state that facility requires, distinct from the active marking of SNV-FR-54 and standing whether or not the toggle is active, so a hidden bottom panel and an open one showing another surface each say the same thing. It occupies space the cluster reserves whether it is showing or not, so a toggle taking or losing it moves no other toggle in the strip. It is presentation and nothing else: a marked toggle activates, opens, and hides the bottom panel exactly as an unmarked one does (SNV-FR-46), the panel opens and closes on account of no emphasis appearing or going, and nothing about it is persisted with the layout (SNV-FR-08).
71. **SNV-FR-KQWD** A keyboard shortcut toggles the bottom panel: ⌘J on macOS, Ctrl+J on Windows and Linux. It is a keyboard route to the panel state the trailing cluster gives (SNV-FR-46), and it selects no surface of its own. While the panel shows, the shortcut hides it. While the panel is hidden, the shortcut opens it at its persisted height on its persisted active surface, and marks that surface's toggle active. The vertical panel stays as it is. The shortcut does not open the panel while the persisted active surface is one whose toggle is disabled (SNV-FR-48), because the panel then has nothing to show; it always hides a panel that shows, whether the toggle of that surface is enabled or not. The shell binds the shortcut while the main window is mounted, whatever surface holds the focus, so it operates from the Editor and from other text inputs. While a floating overlay is mounted (SNV-FR-56) the overlay holds the keyboard and the shortcut does nothing. Each toggle writes the panel's visibility through "save layout preferences" exactly as a click on a toggle does (SNV-FR-08), so the next launch opens the panel only if it was open when the application closed.
72. **SNV-FR-UCJH** The **Documents** toggle is the second toggle of the leading cluster: it sits immediately after Project and before Notes (SNV-FR-44). Activating it makes Documents the vertical panel's active surface on the terms of SNV-FR-45, and the panel it shows is owned by `DPN-documents-panel.md`.
73. **SNV-FR-KXPE** While a project is open, the **About** entry of the application menu is available. Activating it opens the About panel over the main window (per `ABT-about-panel.md` ABT-FR-KMVD, ABT-FR-QZHW). The panel is one of the floating overlays of SNV-FR-56 and is dismissed as `ABT-about-panel.md` ABT-FR-DXGC states.

## Non-functional requirements
- Layout state must restore on next launch without a visible reflow after first paint.
- The persisted full-screen preference is applied as the main window mounts, so the window is never seen windowed and then animated into full-screen after first paint.
- Dragging the vertical-panel splitter tracks the pointer continuously, with the panel and viewport reflowing during the drag rather than only on release.
- Activity-bar tooltips never take focus and never intercept pointer events, so they are transient hover affordances rather than floating overlays: one may be showing while a modal or dropdown is open without either dismissing the other, and a tooltip never blocks a click on the toggle beneath it.
- Toggling a panel open or closed from the activity bar or from the keyboard shortcut (SNV-FR-KQWD) reflows the shell in a single paint, with no intermediate frame at a different width or height.
- Following the active tab never delays the tab: the newly-activated tab renders when it would have rendered anyway, and the panel's activation and reveal run from the identity that tab already carries rather than from a backend round-trip of their own.
- Zones must remain usable at minimum window sizes typical of laptop displays (≥1280×800).
