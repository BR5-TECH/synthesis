# Tabs

**Spec code:** `TAB`

## Intent
The main viewport is a tab strip. This file defines what may be opened as a tab, the single-tab-per-artifact rule that prevents in-IDE duplicate-edit races, the focus-jump consolidation behavior when the same artifact is reachable from multiple surfaces, the rule that the strip never empties — the Dashboard is what remains once everything else is closed, so the viewport always has something to render — the automatic closures the strip performs when a tab's reason to exist is gone, and the fact that a tab is a **view onto** an artifact's editing session rather than the owner of it, so tabs can be opened, switched, and closed freely without costing the author work. That last is what lets an artifact be edited from more than one tab at once — an Editor tab and the target of a Diff tab on the same file are two views of one session, not two sessions — which is why the single-tab rule can exempt them without reopening the race it exists to prevent. A Flow tab is the exception that proves the rule: it owns its Flow's unsaved changes outright, so closing it writes them and keeps nothing behind. The single-tab rule covers in-IDE duplicate-open only. Out of scope: external concurrent edits (an open artifact's file changed on disk), which are handled by the Editor's external-change resolution (`EDT-editor.md`).

## Wireframes
The strip overflowing its width — `Dashboard` and `spec.md` pinned, `RUN-runs.md` needing attention and holding unsaved changes, `plan.md` the active tab, and one further tab clipped at the trailing edge:

```
├──┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│▤ │▣ Dashboard  ×│▣ spec.md   ×│◆ RUN-runs.md ● ×│  plan.md    ×│  archite          │
│▤ ├──────────────┴─────────────┴─────────────────┴──────────────┴───────────────────┤
│▤ │                                                                                 │
```

The context menu on `spec.md`, right-clicked while `plan.md` is the active tab and every tab standing to its left is pinned:

```
│▣ Dashboard  ×│▣ spec.md   ×│  RUN-runs.md ×│  plan.md    ×│
│              └──────────────────────────────┐
│              │ [i] Unpin tab                │
│              ├──────────────────────────────┤
│              │ [i] Close tabs to the left   │ (greyed)
│              │ [i] Close tabs to the right  │
│              │ [i] Close other tabs         │
│              ├──────────────────────────────┤
│              │ [i] Close tab                │
│              └──────────────────────────────┘
```

- Layout notes: the pinned marker `▣` sits at the tab's leading edge in space the strip reserves on every tab whether the marker is showing or not, in its own place within the narrow leading gutter it shares with the needs-attention mark `◆` (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-34), each mark keeping a place of its own so the two can stand on one tab at once, so pinning and unpinning shift nothing in the strip. That gutter is reserved space and nothing else: it is the only leading inset a tab carries, so an unmarked, unpinned tab reads as its type icon and label set against the tab's leading edge rather than pushed inward by the marks' width. The dirty indicator `●` keeps its place after the label and the close control keeps the trailing edge; the pinned marker covers neither, nor the label. The glyphs shown are illustrative — the contract is that the pinned state is carried by a persistent mark **and** a treatment of the tab's surface rather than by any particular glyph, so shape and tone say the same thing twice as they do for the needs-attention emphasis.
- The strip is one row and never wraps. Its tab region scrolls horizontally and draws no scrollbar at any moment; the trailing `archite` above is the tab straddling the region's edge, clipped by that edge alone and brought wholly inside by scrolling to it. The Home affordance, rendered at the strip's head while no Dashboard tab is open (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-09), sits outside the scrolling region and stays reachable at every scroll position.
- `[i]` marks a leading icon, required on every entry (TAB-FR-33) exactly as it is on the Project panel's menu (per `LCM-library-context-menu.md` LCM-FR-07); the glyphs are illustrative. The menu is anchored to the tab that was right-clicked rather than to wherever the pointer landed, and is bounded within the window so a menu opened on a tab near the trailing edge does not overhang it. A disabled entry renders greyed in place rather than disappearing, so the menu holds the same five entries at the same positions on every tab and an author reaches for **Close other tabs** in the same place whatever they clicked.

## UI contract boundary
- **Owned by the UI**: tab strip rendering, tab focus, tab close, single-tab-per-artifact enforcement, focus-jump behavior, the exemptions of TAB-FR-06 and the tracking they need, the automatic closures of TAB-FR-19, TAB-FR-22, and TAB-FR-KUIN, the Document and PDF Viewer tabs with their identity and closure (TAB-FR-LKCT, TAB-FR-QXRF, TAB-FR-KUIN, TAB-FR-ZINL), the never-empty invariant and the Dashboard fallback that upholds it, the drawing of the needs-attention emphasis whose state and lifecycle belong to `NTF-notifications.md` (TAB-FR-26), the strip's single-row layout together with its overflow scrolling and the absence of a scrollbar on it (TAB-FR-27, TAB-FR-28), the keyboard traversal of the strip and the reveal that brings a reached tab into view (TAB-FR-30), the tab context menu with its five entries, their enablement, and their anchoring and dismissal (TAB-FR-32, TAB-FR-33, TAB-FR-39), that same menu on the Home affordance together with what its entries may do there (TAB-FR-40), the pinned state, its marker, and its session-only lifetime (TAB-FR-34, TAB-FR-35), and the mass closes together with the eligible set each computes (TAB-FR-37, TAB-FR-38).
- **Delegated to backend (abstract)**: identity of an artifact (a stable key the UI compares for the single-tab rule). Stubs may issue synthetic stable keys during the walking skeleton. The strip additionally follows the `"project tree changed"` event (`../core/ASC-artifact-scanning.md` ASC-FR-10) for the removed paths it carries, which is how it learns that a tab's backing file is gone (TAB-FR-19), and emits its own diagnostic records through `"append log records (records)"` (`../core/LGC-logging.md`). The paths a commit recorded reach the strip the same way — as something reported to it rather than something it asks for: `"commit paths (message, paths)"` returns them (`../core/GTC-git.md` GTC-FR-19) and the commit message window hands them on (`CMW-commit-message.md` CMW-FR-07), which is what closes the Diff tabs of TAB-FR-22. None of the three introduces an operation of its own. The strip's overflow scrolling, its context menu, and the pinned state that menu sets are the UI's own throughout and reach no operation at all: nothing about a scroll position or a pinned tab is recorded anywhere outside the window (TAB-FR-28, TAB-FR-32, TAB-FR-35).

## Functional requirements
1. **TAB-FR-01** Tabs hold all opened editing and viewing content; the main viewport contains no non-tab content.
2. **TAB-FR-02** The following are tabbable: artifacts (open in Editor), plain text files carrying no artifact type (open in Editor, per `ESH-editor-source-files.md` ESH-FR-ATDS), Flows (open in Flow, per `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-01), drafts (open in New Artifact, per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-01), conversations about a note or about an unavailable owner (open in a **conversation tab**, per TAB-FR-QXMV), Dashboard, Search results, History detail views, Diff views, and the viewer-only **Document** and **PDF Viewer** tabs of the Documents collection (per TAB-FR-LKCT).
3. **TAB-FR-03** The following are NOT tabbable: any vertical-panel surface (Project, Documents, Notes, Comments, Changes, Drafts), any bottom-panel surface (Runs, Git, History version list), and Global settings and Project settings, each of which is a native child window of the application rather than a surface of the main viewport (per `SWN-settings-windows.md` SWN-FR-01, SWN-FR-18).
4. **TAB-FR-04** Single-tab-per-artifact rule: an artifact — or a plain text file (TAB-FR-02) — identified by its stable key is open in at most one editing tab at a time. The key is path-derived and exists for every file the scan surfaces (per `../core/ASC-artifact-scanning.md` ASC-FR-13), so the rule binds classified and unclassified content alike.
5. **TAB-FR-05** When a user requests to open an artifact that already has an editing tab, focus jumps to the existing tab; no new tab is created.
6. **TAB-FR-06** Two kinds of tab are exempt from the single-tab rule, and multiple of either may coexist with the artifact's Editor tab. A **History detail tab** is exempt because it shows an old revision and offers no editing surface, so it can start no edit to race with one. A **Diff tab** is exempt because its editable target is the artifact's own editing session rather than a second one (per `DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-42): however many tabs are open on the file, there is one buffer, one dirty state, one undo history, and one write, which is the whole of what the rule protects. A Diff tab is identified by the pair of its file and its comparison, so a request for one that is already open jumps focus to it rather than creating a duplicate (per `DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-04).
7. **TAB-FR-07** When the same artifact is reachable from multiple surfaces (e.g. selected as a node in Flow while open in Editor), focus jumps to the existing Editor tab; no read-only mirror tab is created.
8. **TAB-FR-08** Tabs are closable, the Dashboard tab included: it renders like any other tab, with its label and its own close control, whose one enablement condition is TAB-FR-16. Closing it does not block other tabs; the Home affordance takes its place at the head of the strip and reopens it (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-09, SNV-FR-41).
9. **TAB-FR-09** Switching the active tab is a display change only: an Editor tab or a Diff tab that is not the active tab keeps the artifact's buffer, dirty state, editing mode, and undo/redo history (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-30), a Diff tab additionally keeping its held original revision, its visualization and rendering modes, and its scroll position (per `DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-52), and a Flow tab that is not the active tab keeps its graph and dirty state (per `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-30); re-activating any of them neither reloads its content nor resets any of that state. The rule governs what a tab holds and not what the rest of the shell does around it: the vertical panel may follow the newly-active tab to the item that tab is a view onto (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-64), which changes the state of no tab in the strip.
10. **TAB-FR-10** Closing an Editor tab or a Diff tab ends the tab, not the artifact's editing session. A write the artifact had scheduled but not yet performed is brought forward and completed before the tab closes, and the artifact's editing state and undo/redo history are retained for the rest of the application session, so reopening it — in either kind of tab — resumes where the close left off (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-28, EDT-FR-29, EDT-FR-31, EDT-FR-72). Closing one of several tabs on the same artifact writes only what is still outstanding and leaves the session standing for the tabs that remain.
11. **TAB-FR-11** A close request for an Editor tab or a Diff tab is refused while the artifact's write cannot proceed safely — an unresolved external-change modal, or a failed write (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-32). The tab stays open and focused with the blocker visible, and closes on a subsequent request once the blocker is resolved.
12. **TAB-FR-12** Closing a Flow tab ends the Flow's editing session with it. A write the tab had scheduled but not yet performed is brought forward and completed before the tab closes, and nothing is retained afterwards, so reopening the Flow loads it from disk (per `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-28).
13. **TAB-FR-13** A close request for a Flow tab is refused while its write fails. The tab stays open and focused with the error visible, and closes on a subsequent request once the write succeeds.
14. **TAB-FR-14** Changing the project's active worktree closes every tab (per `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-12), conversation tabs among them, and every discussion surface goes with them on the same terms. Every artifact, Flow, and draft file holding unsaved changes is written first, and a write that cannot proceed safely cancels the whole switch, so no tab closes and the active worktree is unchanged (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-32, EDT-FR-33, `FLO-flow.md` FLO-FR-29, and `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-24). Once the tabs close, no editing session outlives them: the retained edit state of every artifact is discarded (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-28), because a tab's content belongs to the content root it was opened from and the stable key it is identified by (TAB-FR-04) names a path in that root rather than in the new one. The strip does not stay empty once they close: a Dashboard tab opens in their place (TAB-FR-15), which is the fresh-open state `OVW-overview.md` OVW-FR-12 describes.
15. **TAB-FR-15** The tab strip never empties while a project is open: whenever the last open tab closes, a Dashboard tab opens in the leading position of the strip and takes focus. The rule holds however the strip was emptied — the user closing the final remaining tab, or the active-worktree change of TAB-FR-14 closing every tab at once.
16. **TAB-FR-16** The Dashboard tab's close control is active only while at least one other tab is open. While the Dashboard is the only open tab the control renders greyed-out and activating it closes nothing, so the strip is never emptied by closing the Dashboard itself. Every other tab's close control is unconditional.
17. **TAB-FR-17** A New Artifact tab is identified by the id of the draft it is bound to rather than by a path, because a draft is not a file (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-02). The single-tab rule binds it on those terms: a draft is open in at most one New Artifact tab, and a request to open one already showing jumps focus to it. A draft's prompt is edited inside that tab and never opens as a tab of its own, so a draft's prompt and an artifact of the same name never contend for the strip.
18. **TAB-FR-18** Closing a New Artifact tab ends the tab, not the draft. Unsaved draft files are written before the tab closes (per `NAW-new-artifact.md` NAW-FR-13) and the draft survives with its prompt, version history, status, discussions intact, so reopening it from the Drafts panel resumes where the close left off. A close request is refused while a write fails: the tab stays open and focused with the error visible and closes on a subsequent request once the write succeeds.
19. **TAB-FR-19** A file that no longer exists holds no tab. When `"project tree changed"` reports removed paths (per `../core/ASC-artifact-scanning.md` ASC-FR-22), every tab whose content is one of those paths — or any path beneath a removed folder — closes automatically, without the user acting and without a prompt. This binds every kind of tab that names a path: Editor tabs, Flow tabs, and the view-only Diff and History detail tabs of TAB-FR-06 alike, so a removed file leaves nothing of itself in the strip. A rename is a removal of the path it renamed away from, so a tab open on the old path closes on the same terms rather than following the file to its new one; the file reopens under its new name like any other. New Artifact tabs are untouched, being identified by a draft id rather than a path (TAB-FR-17), and Document and PDF Viewer tabs are untouched on the same terms, being identified by a document id (TAB-FR-QXRF), and drafts live outside the scanned tree entirely; conversation tabs are untouched on the same terms (TAB-FR-24). The never-empty invariant still holds: if these closures empty the strip, a Dashboard tab opens in their place (TAB-FR-15).
20. **TAB-FR-20** An automatic close under TAB-FR-19 writes nothing. The write-before-close of TAB-FR-10 and TAB-FR-12 does not apply, because the only file it could write to is the one that was just removed and writing it would recreate it — the removal is the user's most recent expression of intent and the strip does not undo it. Unsaved edits in such a tab are discarded, and so is the retained editing session that TAB-FR-10 would otherwise preserve for the rest of the application session, so reopening a file recreated at that path later starts from disk with an empty undo history. The close refusals of TAB-FR-11 and TAB-FR-13 likewise do not apply: a tab blocked on an unresolved external-change modal or a failed write still closes, since both of those blockers guard a write that is no longer going to happen.
21. **TAB-FR-21** Each tab closed under TAB-FR-19 or TAB-FR-22 produces one `DEBUG` record through `"append log records (records)"` (`../core/LGC-logging.md`) under the `frontend` domain, naming the path, the kind of tab that closed, and which of the two rules closed it. A tab that held unsaved changes discarded under TAB-FR-20 says so in the same record, so work dropped without a prompt is accounted for somewhere the user can find it. No record is produced for a tab the user closes themselves.
22. **TAB-FR-22** A **Diff tab whose file a commit has successfully included closes automatically**, without the user acting and without a prompt: the comparison it was open on is the change that has just been committed, and a review the author has acted on is not a thing the strip should keep asking them to finish. It binds Diff tabs alone — no Editor tab, Flow tab, or New Artifact tab is closed by a commit, and the artifact's editing session survives untouched (per `EDT-editor.md` EDT-FR-28), because committing a file says nothing about whether the author is finished writing it. Every Diff tab whose file was committed closes, so several tabs on one file under several comparisons all go together, and a Diff tab on a file the commit did not include stays open.
    The strip acts on **confirmed success alone**, and on the paths that commit actually recorded: `"commit paths (message, paths)"` returns them (per `../core/GTC-git.md` GTC-FR-19) and the commit message window hands them on as it closes (per `CMW-commit-message.md` CMW-FR-07), so a rename recorded at both its locations closes a Diff tab open on either. A commit merely started, abandoned, or refused closes nothing, and no fallback ever closes every Diff tab — a strip that cannot name what was committed closes nothing at all, because a Diff tab the author still needs is worth more than one they no longer do.
    The close is the ordinary one of TAB-FR-10 and TAB-FR-11 rather than the discarding one of TAB-FR-19 and TAB-FR-20: the file still exists, so an outstanding write is brought forward and completed before the tab is removed, and a tab whose write cannot proceed safely stays open and focused with its blocker visible, closing on the next attempt once it is resolved. The never-empty invariant still holds: if these closures empty the strip, a Dashboard tab opens in their place (TAB-FR-15).
23. **TAB-FR-23** A **conversation tab** is identified by the id of the conversation it is bound to rather than by a path, because a conversation is not a file and outlives every file it was about. The single-tab rule binds it on those terms: a discussion is open in at most one conversation tab, and a request to reveal one already showing jumps focus to it rather than creating a second (TAB-FR-05, TAB-FR-QXMV). It coexists with the Editor, Diff, and History detail tabs of the file that conversation is about, none of which contends with it for the strip. Several conversation tabs may be open at once, one per discussion revealed there.
24. **TAB-FR-24** A conversation tab is not closed by the removal of any path. The automatic closure of TAB-FR-19 binds the tabs that name a path, and this tab names a conversation instead; a conversation whose artifact has been removed renders its unavailable-owner state in the tab it is already in rather than being taken off the strip with the file, because the conversation still exists, is still readable, and is still one the author can post to. The commit-driven closure of TAB-FR-22 binds Diff tabs alone and reaches it no more than it reaches an Editor tab.
25. **TAB-FR-25** Closing a conversation tab ends the tab, not the conversation, and never the conversation's session state: the typed text, the pending attachments, the scroll position, and the unread state stay in the session stores keyed by discussion id. No comment is posted, no lock or resolution is set, and no discussion is written to by the close. The tab owns no file, so the write-before-close of TAB-FR-10 has nothing to bring forward and no close request for it is ever refused. An outstanding agent turn in the conversation is neither cancelled nor waited on. The never-empty invariant still holds: if closing it empties the strip, a Dashboard tab opens in its place (TAB-FR-15).

26. **TAB-FR-QXMV** A discussion is revealed in a conversation tab in exactly two cases: its target is a **note**, which has no owner tab, and its owner is **unavailable** (the artifact was removed, or the draft was deleted). Every other discussion is revealed in its owner's tab or panel (per `CVP-conversation-presentation.md`). The tab hosts the shared discussion surface, and the opening composer where a note has no discussion yet. The route is one per discussion: it focuses the conversation tab that already shows the discussion and never opens a second tab for the same discussion, and a request for a note that has no discussion yet focuses the tab already open for that note. A conversation tab opens at the end of the strip and takes focus, unless the author's route asked for none. A conversation tab for a discussion whose owner becomes available again stays in the strip until the author closes it. The strip has no maximize action, no restore action, and no detached or minimized form of a conversation.
26. **TAB-FR-26** A tab renders the **needs-attention** emphasis carried by whatever it is a view onto (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-26, NTF-FR-34), the strip drawing what that facility holds rather than deciding any of it: no tab tracks attention state of its own, and a tab remounted or shifted by its neighbours opening and closing renders whatever that facility says of the target it is a view onto. The emphasis is presentation and nothing else. It changes no rule here — a marked tab opens, focuses, and closes exactly as an unmarked one does, contends for the strip no differently under the single-tab rule (TAB-FR-04), is reached by the automatic closures of TAB-FR-19 and TAB-FR-22 on the same terms, and leaves the never-empty invariant untouched (TAB-FR-15) — and no tab is opened, closed, moved, or focused on account of an emphasis appearing or going.

27. **TAB-FR-27** The tab strip is a **single horizontal row** that never wraps to a second line. A tab holds a legible width — enough for the slots the strip reserves (TAB-FR-35, per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-34), a label, and a whole close control — rather than shrinking without bound as the strip fills, so the twentieth tab is as usable as the first. Once the tabs together exceed the width available, the strip's tab region **scrolls** horizontally and every tab is reachable by scrolling to it: nothing is dropped from the strip, folded into an overflow list, or made reachable only from another surface.
28. **TAB-FR-28** The scrolling region renders **no horizontal scrollbar** — none permanent, none on hover, and none while a scroll is in progress — in any theme and on every platform, including those that draw persistent scrollbars rather than transient overlaid ones; the overflow is clipped at the region's edges instead. The pointer scrolls the strip on its own: a wheel over the strip scrolls it along its one axis whichever axis the wheel reports, a trackpad's horizontal gesture scrolls it, and Shift-wheel does the same, so a device reporting only vertical wheel movement reaches the far end of the strip as readily as one reporting both. The keyboard reaches it through the reveal of TAB-FR-30 rather than by scrolling it directly. No tab is ever reachable only by dragging a control the strip does not draw.
29. **TAB-FR-29** Clipping costs no tab its usability. A tab's contents are laid out against that tab's own width — its label truncating with an ellipsis where the width demands it, while its close control, its dirty indicator, its pinned marker (TAB-FR-35), and the needs-attention mark of TAB-FR-26 each keep their reserved place whole — so nothing a tab needs to be operated is lost to the strip being full. What the region's edge clips is the tab straddling it and nothing else, and no tab is left permanently half-shown: reaching it (TAB-FR-30) scrolls the region until the whole of it stands inside. The Home affordance sits outside the scrolling region at the strip's head and is reachable at every scroll position (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-09).
30. **TAB-FR-30** The strip is operable from the keyboard. It takes one tab stop, which lands on the active tab; the arrow keys move a focus ring from tab to tab across the whole strip, Home and End reach the first and the last, and Enter or Space activates the focused tab. Moving focus **does not** activate: a tab is reached and then chosen, so traversing a strip of twenty tabs does not activate nineteen of them on the way. Focus reaching a tab scrolls it fully into view, and so does a tab becoming active by any route, which is how the keyboard reaches a tab the strip is not currently showing.
31. **TAB-FR-31** Every tab carries an **accessible name** naming what the tab is a view onto — in the case that name carries on disk (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-57), carrying whatever distinguishes it from another tab on the same file (per `DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-02), and stating its pinned state (TAB-FR-35) and its unsaved state alongside it — so a tab truncated to a few characters is still identified in full to a screen reader. Each tab's close control carries an accessible name of its own naming the tab it closes rather than the word *close* alone, which is what tells two adjacent close controls apart. The needs-attention state is announced by the facility that owns it and not here (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-26 through NTF-FR-37).
32. **TAB-FR-32** Any tab is **right-clicked** — or reached with the platform's keyboard context-menu gesture while it holds focus — to open a **context menu anchored to that tab**. Opening it changes nothing about the strip: the tab is neither activated nor closed and the active tab is unchanged, so the menu acts on a tab the author is not reading without first taking them away from the one they are. The menu is one of the window's mutually-exclusive floating overlays (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-56). It closes on an entry being chosen, on Escape, on the pointer or the focus leaving it, and on any activation outside it; every route but the first invokes nothing, and Escape returns focus to the tab the menu was opened on. It is operable from the keyboard throughout — the arrow keys move through its entries, Enter chooses one, Escape leaves — and every entry carries an accessible name.
33. **TAB-FR-33** The menu holds exactly **five entries in one fixed order** whatever the tab: the pin action, then **Close tabs to the left**, **Close tabs to the right**, **Close other tabs**, and **Close tab**, with a separator after the pin action and another before **Close tab**, so the entry acting on one tab is not adjacent to the three acting on many. The pin action is **Pin tab** on an unpinned tab and **Unpin tab** on a pinned one; exactly one of the two renders and never both. Every entry carries a leading icon, as the Project panel's menu entries do (per `LCM-library-context-menu.md` LCM-FR-07), so the window's two context menus read as one kind of surface. Every entry acts on the **context-clicked tab** and on no other, whether or not that tab is the active one.
34. **TAB-FR-34** **Pin tab** marks the context-clicked tab pinned and **Unpin tab** clears the mark, and neither does anything else: the active tab is unchanged, focus returns where it was, no tab opens or closes, and the strip does not reorder — a tab holds its position among its neighbours whether pinned or not, there being no pinned group and no leading cluster for pinned tabs to be gathered into. Neither action is ever disabled, on any tab, the Dashboard included. Pinning excludes a tab from the mass closes of TAB-FR-37 and does nothing further: it disables no close control, refuses no close, and reaches none of the automatic closures — a pinned tab whose backing path is removed closes under TAB-FR-19, a pinned Diff tab whose file a commit includes closes under TAB-FR-22, and a worktree change closes it with every other tab under TAB-FR-14. Pinning guards a tab against the author's own bulk gesture and against nothing else.
35. **TAB-FR-35** Pinned state belongs to the tab for as long as the tab exists and no longer. It is held in memory alone: no operation records it and nothing about it survives a relaunch, a project switch, or a worktree change — closing a tab drops its pinned state with it, and reopening the same target opens an unpinned tab, exactly as reopening a target opens an unmarked one (per `NTF-notifications.md` NTF-FR-33). A pinned tab renders a **persistent marker** distinct from the dirty indicator and from the needs-attention mark of TAB-FR-26, in space the strip reserves on every tab whether the marker is showing or not, so a tab taking or losing it changes neither its own width nor any other tab's position; it obscures neither the tab's label nor its close control.
36. **TAB-FR-36** **Close tab** closes the context-clicked tab and no other, pinned or not, down exactly the path that tab's own close control takes: the write-before-close and session retention of TAB-FR-10, TAB-FR-12, TAB-FR-18, and TAB-FR-25 by kind, the close refusals of TAB-FR-11 and TAB-FR-13, and the never-empty fallback of TAB-FR-15 all apply unchanged. Pinning never guards a tab from its own close — a pinned tab's close control is as unconditional as any other's (TAB-FR-16), and **Close tab** on a pinned tab closes it. The entry is disabled on one tab alone: the Dashboard while it is the only open tab, on the terms of TAB-FR-16.
37. **TAB-FR-37** The three mass closes act on **every unpinned tab in their range and on nothing else**. **Close tabs to the left** takes every unpinned tab positioned before the context-clicked tab in the strip, **Close tabs to the right** every unpinned tab positioned after it, and **Close other tabs** every unpinned tab but it. None of the three ever closes the context-clicked tab — whether or not that tab is itself pinned, the tab the author pointed at being the one they said to keep — and none ever closes a pinned tab, which is the whole of what pinning buys. Because the context-clicked tab always survives, no mass close can empty the strip.
38. **TAB-FR-38** A mass close attempts every eligible tab, in strip order from the leading end, each down the same path **Close tab** takes (TAB-FR-36), so an outstanding write is brought forward and completed before its tab goes. A tab that **refuses** — an unresolved external-change modal, or a failed write (TAB-FR-11, TAB-FR-13, TAB-FR-18) — stays open with its blocker visible and does not end the pass: every remaining eligible tab is still attempted, so one file the author must attend to does not leave a dozen they are finished with standing behind it. Where at least one tab refused, the first refusing tab in strip order becomes the active tab with its blocker visible; where none did, the context-clicked tab becomes active if the previously-active tab was among those closed, and the active tab is unchanged if it was not. A mass close is the user closing tabs themselves and produces no log record for any of them (TAB-FR-21).
39. **TAB-FR-39** Each of the three mass closes is **disabled exactly while its own eligible set is empty**, rendering greyed in place rather than disappearing and closing nothing when activated. The condition is the eligible set and never the clicked tab's position in the strip: **Close tabs to the left** is disabled while no unpinned tab stands before the context-clicked tab, which is as true of a tab preceded by three pinned tabs as of the strip's first tab, and **Close tabs to the right** is disabled on the same terms trailing; **Close other tabs** is disabled while no unpinned tab other than the context-clicked one is open. A menu opened on the only tab in the strip therefore offers three greyed mass closes, and the pin action is the only entry that acts.
40. **TAB-FR-40** The **Home affordance** answers a right-click with the same menu the tabs carry, opened and dismissed on the terms of TAB-FR-32 and holding the same five entries in the same fixed order as every other (TAB-FR-33), so the head of the strip is not a dead spot the author has to aim around. What those entries may do follows from the affordance being a control rather than a tab (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-09) and from its standing at the head of the strip: **Pin tab** and **Close tab** name a tab that does not exist, and **Close tabs to the left** has nothing to its left, so all three render greyed rather than being dropped — the menu keeps every entry at the position it occupies everywhere else, and an author reaches for **Close other tabs** in the same place whatever they right-clicked. **Close tabs to the right** and **Close other tabs** each take every unpinned tab in the strip, those being one and the same set seen from the head, and each is disabled while that set is empty on the terms of TAB-FR-39. Closing every tab this way leaves the never-empty invariant standing: a Dashboard tab opens in their place (TAB-FR-15) and the affordance gives way to it, the Dashboard being represented in the strip exactly once or not at all (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-09).

41. **TAB-FR-41** A rollback that has **removed a file** from the working tree closes **every tab bound to it** — Editor, Diff, and Flow alike, however many comparisons the Diff tabs held — without the user acting and without a prompt, there being no file left for any of them to show (per `CHG-changes.md` CHG-FR-63). This close does **not** take the write-before-close path of TAB-FR-10 and is refused by neither guard of TAB-FR-11: the artifact's pending write is dropped rather than brought forward and its retained edit state is discarded with the tabs rather than outliving them (per `EXC-editor-external-change.md` EXC-FR-ZXWI), because a flush here would write back the very file the author asked to be rid of, and a failed-write or external-change guard would hold open a tab over content the filesystem no longer has. The never-empty invariant is unaffected: a Dashboard tab opens if these closes empty the strip (TAB-FR-15).
42. **TAB-FR-42** A rollback that **restored** a file closes nothing. Its tabs stay open and reload the restored content on their own terms — an Editor tab through the session reset of `EXC-editor-external-change.md` EXC-FR-JAWT, a Diff tab additionally re-deriving its comparison (per `DFV-diff-viewer.md` DFV-FR-58) — which is the opposite of what a commit does to a Diff tab (TAB-FR-22), and deliberately so: a commit finishes the review a Diff tab was opened for, while a rollback undoes the change that review was about and leaves the author looking at the file they still have. A file the rollback did not confirm as either restored or removed keeps its tabs and everything in them exactly as they were.
43. **TAB-FR-KXMW** The Map of specifications is also tabbable, in one **Map tab** per open project (per `SMP-specification-map.md` SMP-FR-WBNL). A request to open the map while that tab is open jumps focus to it (TAB-FR-05). It names no path and no draft, so the closures of TAB-FR-19, TAB-FR-22, and TAB-FR-41 never close it.
37. **TAB-FR-LKCT** The Documents panel opens two viewer-only kinds of tab. A **Document tab** shows a Markdown or text document (per `DTV-document-text-viewer.md`), and a **PDF Viewer tab** shows a PDF document (per `PDV-pdf-viewer.md`). Neither kind is an Editor tab. Neither holds an editing session or a savable buffer, so File → Save stays unavailable while one is active (per `SNV-shell-navigation.md` SNV-FR-28).
38. **TAB-FR-QXRF** A Document tab and a PDF Viewer tab are each identified by the document id (per `../core/DCL-documents-collection.md` DCL-FR-QKJO). The single-tab rule binds them: a document is open in at most one viewer tab, and a request to open one already showing focuses that tab. A viewer tab coexists with an Editor tab on the same file.
39. **TAB-FR-KUIN** A viewer tab closes automatically when the snapshot that `"remove document source (path)"` returns no longer holds its document id (per `../core/DCL-documents-collection.md` DCL-FR-ATNL). The close needs no prompt, writes nothing, and leaves the file untouched. A document that stays in the collection through another source keeps its tab. The never-empty invariant still holds (TAB-FR-15).
40. **TAB-FR-ZINL** A document that leaves the collection through any change other than a source removal, such as a deleted file in a selected folder, keeps its viewer tab, and the tab shows its unavailable state (per `DTV-document-text-viewer.md` DTV-FR-BEQL and `PDV-pdf-viewer.md` PDV-FR-SLWS).
41. **TAB-FR-FJXM** Closing a viewer tab ends the tab and nothing else: no write is brought forward, no close request is refused, and no source reference changes.
42. **TAB-FR-ZMGX** Each viewer tab closed under TAB-FR-KUIN produces one `DEBUG` record through `"append log records (records)"` (`../core/LGC-logging.md`) under the `frontend` domain, naming the document id, the kind of tab, and the rule that closed it. No record is produced for a viewer tab that the user closes, and no record carries a path or a file name.

## Non-functional requirements
- Tab focus and close are immediate UI operations (no backend round-trip required to respond), except for an Editor, Diff, or Flow tab close that must first write pending changes. An automatic close under TAB-FR-19 writes nothing and is therefore immediate in every case.
- A burst that removes many paths at once closes every affected tab in one pass rather than one re-render per tab, so deleting a folder holding a dozen open files does not visibly cascade through the strip. A commit closing several Diff tabs at once (TAB-FR-22) does the same, and so does a mass close removing a dozen tabs at the author's word (TAB-FR-38).
- Scrolling the strip, opening the context menu, and pinning a tab each make no backend round-trip. The strip's scroll position is held in memory for the life of the strip and nothing persists it, so a strip scrolled to its far end starts at its leading end again after a relaunch.
- Revealing a tab (TAB-FR-30) takes the smallest scroll that brings the whole of it inside the region, so a tab already fully visible moves the strip not at all and switching between two adjacent tabs never makes the strip jump.
